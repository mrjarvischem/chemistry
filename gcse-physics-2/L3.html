Lesson 3 · Required practical: resistance of a wire

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Electricity 4.2.1.3 · Required practical · about 25 minutes

Investigating length and resistance

How does the resistance of a wire depend on its length? A thin wire is taped along a metre ruler. A crocodile clip is fixed at 0 cm and a second clip is moved along the wire.

Variables

Independent: length of wire between the clips
Dependent: resistance (worked out from V and I)
Control: same wire (material and thickness), temperature

Method

  1. Set the clips 10 cm apart.
  2. Close the switch; read the ammeter and voltmeter.
  3. Open the switch. Calculate R = V ÷ I.
  4. Repeat every 10 cm up to 100 cm.

Take your readings

Move the clip, record the meters, and build the graph of resistance against length.

Clip at cm
The graph is a straight line: resistance is directly proportional to length. Double the length → double the resistance. A longer wire means the charges collide with more ions in the metal.

Getting good results

The wire heats up

A current heats the wire, and a hotter wire has a higher resistance. Keep the p.d. low and open the switch between readings so the temperature stays the same.

The line misses the origin

If the graph crosses the resistance axis above zero, there is extra resistance from the clips and connecting leads (or the clip at "0 cm" is not exactly on zero). This is a systematic error — it shifts every reading by the same amount.

Repeat each length and calculate a mean to reduce the effect of random errors, like reading the ruler at a slightly different place.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Ammeter in series, voltmeter across the length of wire being tested. R = V ÷ I.
  • Resistance is directly proportional to length: a straight line through the origin.
  • Keep the temperature constant: low p.d., switch off between readings.
  • A line that misses the origin shows a systematic error (resistance of clips and leads).
