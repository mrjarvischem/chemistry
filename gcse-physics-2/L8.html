Lesson 8 · Mains electricity and safety

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Electricity 4.2.3 · about 25 minutes

Direct and alternating current

Direct current (d.c.)

Flows in one direction only. Comes from cells and batteries. The p.d. stays the same.

Alternating current (a.c.)

Keeps changing direction. The p.d. goes positive, then negative, many times a second. Mains electricity is a.c.

UK mains supply: a.c. at a frequency of 50 Hz and about 230 V. 50 Hz means it changes direction and back 50 times every second.

The three-core cable

Most appliances connect to the mains with a three-core cable. Each wire has a colour and a job. Tap a wire, then tap the terminal in the plug it connects to.

Tap a wire.
Memory trick: brown goes bottom right (live), blue goes bottom left (neutral). The earth is at the top in the middle.

Why the live wire is dangerous

The live wire is at about 230 V. Your body is at 0 V (earth potential). Touching the live wire makes a large p.d. across your body, so a current flows through you — an electric shock. This can happen even if the switch is off, because the live wire is still connected to the supply up to the switch.

What happens

Press the fault button.
The earth wire is a safety wire. If a fault makes the metal case live, a large current flows through the earth wire to the ground, and the fuse melts and cuts off the supply. The earth wire only carries a current if there is a fault.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • d.c. flows one way; a.c. keeps changing direction. UK mains: a.c., 50 Hz, about 230 V.
  • Live: brown, 230 V. Neutral: blue, about 0 V, completes the circuit. Earth: green and yellow, 0 V, safety.
  • Touching the live wire causes a large p.d. across your body → current → shock, even with the switch off.
  • Earth wire + fuse: a fault current flows to earth and melts the fuse.
