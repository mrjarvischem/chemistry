Lesson 9 · Electrical power and energy transfers

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Electricity 4.2.4.1–4.2.4.2 · about 25 minutes

Energy transfers in everyday appliances

Appliances are designed to transfer energy electrically from the mains or a battery to useful stores. Tap one. The power rating tells you how much energy it transfers every second.

Tap an appliance.
The energy an appliance transfers depends on how long it is switched on and its power.

Calculating power

P = V IP = I2 R Learn both
SymbolQuantityUnit
Ppowerwatts, W
Vpotential differencevolts, V
Icurrentamps, A
Rresistanceohms, Ω

Use P = V I when you know the p.d. and current. Use P = I2 R when you know the current and resistance.

Current I = A
Resistance R = Ω
Power depends on current squared: double the current through a resistor → four times the power (heating effect).

Energy transferred

E = P tE = Q V Learn both

E in joules (J), P in watts (W), t in seconds (s), Q in coulombs (C), V in volts (V).

E = Q V explains what p.d. really means: 1 volt = 1 joule per coulomb. A 230 V supply gives each coulomb of charge 230 J of energy to transfer.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Appliances transfer energy electrically to useful stores; power = energy per second.
  • P = V I and P = I2 R (learn both).
  • E = P t and E = Q V (learn both). Time in seconds.
  • 1 V = 1 J per C.
