Lesson 2 · Potential difference and resistance

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Electricity 4.2.1.3 · about 25 minutes

Potential difference

Potential difference (p.d.) is what pushes charge round a circuit. It tells you how much energy each coulomb of charge transfers between two points. It is measured in volts (V).

Measuring it

A voltmeter is connected in parallel — across the component — because it compares two points: one on each side.

Current, p.d. and resistance

Resistance is how much a component opposes the current. The current through a component depends on the p.d. across it and its resistance.

V = I R Learn this — not on the equation sheet
SymbolQuantityUnit
Vpotential differencevolts, V
Icurrentamps, A
Rresistanceohms, Ω
Supply p.d. = V
Resistance R = Ω
For a given p.d., the greater the resistance, the smaller the current. For a given resistance, the greater the p.d., the bigger the current.

Using V = I R

Rearranging

I = V ÷ R    R = V ÷ I

Cover the one you want in the triangle V over I × R if that helps.

Prefixes

1 kΩ = 1000 Ω
1 mA = 0.001 A
Convert before you substitute.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • p.d. is measured in volts with a voltmeter connected in parallel.
  • V = I R (learn it): volts, amps, ohms.
  • Bigger resistance → smaller current for the same p.d.
  • Convert kΩ → Ω and mA → A before substituting.
