Lesson 4 · I–V characteristics

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Electricity 4.2.1.4 · Required practical · about 25 minutes

Investigating how current depends on p.d.

An I–V characteristic is a graph of current (I) against potential difference (V) for a component. It shows how the component behaves.

Method

  1. Set up the circuit. The variable resistor changes the p.d. across the component.
  2. Record the p.d. (voltmeter) and current (ammeter).
  3. Change the variable resistor and repeat for at least 6 values.
  4. Swap the connections to the supply to get negative values of p.d. and current.
  5. Plot I (y-axis) against V (x-axis).

Plot the characteristics

Choose a component. Drag the p.d. from negative to positive and record readings to build the graph.

p.d. V = V

Ohmic and non-ohmic

An ohmic conductor: at constant temperature, current is directly proportional to p.d. — a straight line through the origin. Its resistance stays the same.

Filament lamp

As the current increases, the filament gets hotter. The metal ions vibrate more, so it is harder for the charges to get through — resistance increases. The graph curves and gets flatter.

Diode

Current flows in one direction only. In the reverse direction the resistance is very high, so the current is zero. In the forward direction, a current flows once the p.d. passes about 0.6 V.

Work out resistance at a point on the graph with R = V ÷ I. Don’t use the gradient.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Variable resistor changes the p.d.; reverse the supply for negative values. Plot I against V.
  • Resistor at constant temperature: straight line through the origin (ohmic).
  • Filament lamp: curve — resistance increases as temperature increases.
  • Diode: current in one direction only; very high resistance in reverse.
