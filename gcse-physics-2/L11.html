Charging by friction

When two insulators are rubbed together, electrons are transferred from one to the other. Only electrons (negative charges) move — the positive charges stay put.

What happened

The object that gains electrons becomes negatively charged. The object that loses electrons becomes positively charged — by an equal amount.

Electric fields and sparks

A charged object creates an electric field around it. Any other charged object in the field feels a force. Use the slider to move a small positive charge closer and further away.

Field lines

  • Point away from positive charges and towards negative charges — the direction of the force on a small positive charge.
  • Lines are closer together near the sphere: the field is stronger there.
Sparks: if an object becomes very highly charged, the electric field near it can become strong enough to pull electrons off air particles. The air conducts and a spark jumps — e.g. a small shock from a car door, or lightning.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Rubbing insulators transfers electrons. Gains electrons → negative; loses electrons → positive.
  • Like charges repel, unlike charges attract — non-contact forces.
  • A charged object has an electric field; the force on another charge is stronger closer to it.
  • Field lines point away from + and towards −. A strong enough field causes sparking.
