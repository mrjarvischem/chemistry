The National Grid

The National Grid is a system of cables and transformers linking power stations to homes, schools and businesses all over the country. Step through the journey.

Step-up transformers increase the p.d. from the power station to the very high p.d. of the transmission cables.
Step-down transformers decrease it to a much lower, safer p.d. for homes (230 V).

Why transmit at such a high p.d.?

A power station sends 10 MW (10 000 000 W) along cables with a total resistance of 5 Ω. Choose the p.d. it is sent at.

For the same power, a higher p.d. means a lower current (P = V I). A lower current means much less heating in the cables (P = I2 R), so less energy is wasted. That is why the National Grid is an efficient way to transfer energy.

Transformers

Step-up

Output p.d. is higher than the input. Output current is lower. Used at power stations.

Step-down

Output p.d. is lower than the input. Output current is higher. Used near homes and in phone chargers.

Power in = power out Physics only Higher tier

If a transformer is 100% efficient, the power going in equals the power coming out:

Vp × Ip = Vs × Is

p = primary (input) coil, s = secondary (output) coil. This equation is on the equation sheet. You will meet transformers again in Magnetism and electromagnetism.

Lesson summary

  • National Grid: cables and transformers linking power stations to consumers.
  • Step-up transformers increase p.d. for transmission; step-down transformers decrease it for homes.
