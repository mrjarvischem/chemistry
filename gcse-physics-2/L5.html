Lesson 5 · LDRs, thermistors and sensing circuits

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Electricity 4.2.1.4 · about 25 minutes

Light-dependent resistors

The resistance of an LDR depends on how much light falls on it.

Light intensity:

Resistance against light

As light intensity increases, the resistance of an LDR decreases. In the dark its resistance is very high.
Uses: switching on street lights or security lights when it gets dark.

Thermistors

The resistance of a thermistor depends on its temperature.

Temperature: °C

Resistance against temperature

As temperature increases, the resistance of a thermistor decreases.
Uses: thermostats in heating systems, fire alarms, car engine temperature sensors.

A night light that switches itself on

An LDR and a fixed resistor are in series with a 6 V supply. They share the 6 V. The bigger resistance gets the bigger share. When the p.d. across the LDR goes above 3 V, the light switches on.

Light outside:

How the 6 V is shared

Explain it as a chain: darker → LDR resistance increases → LDR gets a bigger share of the p.d. → light switches on.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • LDR: more light → lower resistance. Used for automatic lights.
  • Thermistor: higher temperature → lower resistance. Used in thermostats.
  • In series, components share the supply p.d. — the bigger resistance gets the bigger share.
  • Explain sensing circuits step by step: condition → resistance → p.d. share → output.
