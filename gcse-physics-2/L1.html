Electric current

A current is a flow of electrical charge. In a metal wire the charges are electrons. For charge to flow, the circuit must be closed and there must be a source of potential difference (e.g. a cell).

What the meters show

The dots show the direction of conventional current: out of the positive terminal (long line) and round to the negative. The current is the same at every point in a single closed loop — both ammeters always agree. Current is not "used up" by the lamp.

Current is the rate of flow of charge. Ammeters measure current in amps (A), and are connected in series.

Lesson summary

  • Learn the 14 circuit symbols, including LDR, thermistor, diode and LED.
  • Current = rate of flow of charge. Needs a closed circuit and a source of potential difference.
  • Q = I t (learn it): C, A, s. Convert mA → A and minutes → s.
  • In a single closed loop the current is the same at every point. Ammeters go in series.
