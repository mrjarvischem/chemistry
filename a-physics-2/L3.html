The principle of superposition

When two waves meet, the resultant displacement at any point is the vector sum of the individual displacements.
Press “Send pulses”.

After they meet, the pulses carry on unchanged — they pass straight through each other.

How a stationary wave forms

A stationary (standing) wave forms when two progressive waves with the same frequency (and similar amplitude) travel in opposite directions and superpose — usually a wave and its reflection.

Nodes and antinodes

Nodes (N): points of zero amplitude — the two waves always cancel (destructive).
Antinodes (A): points of maximum amplitude — always constructive.
Adjacent nodes (or antinodes) are λ/2 apart; a node to the next antinode is λ/4.

Stationary waves on strings — harmonics

A string fixed at both ends must have a node at each end. Only certain wavelengths fit, so only certain frequencies (harmonics) can form stationary waves.

first harmonic: f = 12l √(T/μ)   T = tension, μ = mass per unit length. Harmonic n has frequency n × f1.

Stationary microwaves (and sound)

A microwave transmitter faces a metal plate. The reflected wave superposes with the incoming wave. Move the detector: the reading drops to a minimum at each node.

Detector position: cm

The same idea works for sound (e.g. a loudspeaker facing a wall, or air in a tube). Measuring the distance between several nodes gives λ/2, so λ — and c = fλ.

Required practical 1: stationary waves on a string

Investigate how the first-harmonic frequency depends on length, tension and mass per unit length. A vibration generator drives the string; masses on a hanger over a pulley set the tension (T = mg).

Find f1 by slowly increasing the frequency from low until the string shows one loop with maximum amplitude. Measure l from the vibration generator to the pulley with a metre rule. To check, look for the second harmonic at 2f1. A graph of f against 1/l should be a straight line through the origin.

Lesson summary

  • Superposition: resultant displacement = vector sum of individual displacements.
  • Stationary wave: two waves, same frequency, opposite directions. Nodes (zero amplitude), antinodes (maximum). Node–node = λ/2.
  • No energy transfer; points between adjacent nodes in phase, adjacent loops in antiphase.
  • First harmonic on a string: f = (1/2l)√(T/μ); harmonics at nf₁.
  • RP1: vary l, T (= mg), μ; find f₁ as one loop of maximum amplitude.
