Lesson 5 · Diffraction and diffraction gratings

AQA A-level Physics 3.3.2.1 · including Required Practical 2 (part 2) · about 25 minutes

Diffraction through a single slit

Diffraction is the spreading out of waves as they pass through a gap or around an obstacle. It is most noticeable when the gap is similar in size to the wavelength.

Slit width: μm
Wavelength: nm

The pattern

A wide, bright central maximum, with narrower, much dimmer maxima either side. The central maximum is twice as wide as the others.

What changes its width?

Central maximum gets wider if the slit is narrower or the wavelength is longer. (Red light diffracts more than blue.)
White light: a white central maximum with coloured edges; side maxima are spectra, violet on the inside.

Only a qualitative treatment of single-slit diffraction is needed — no equation.

Diffraction gratings

A grating has hundreds of slits per millimetre. Light from every slit interferes, giving very sharp, bright maxima at specific angles.

d sin θ = nλ    d = slit spacing = 1 / (lines per metre); n = order
Lines per mm:
Wavelength: nm

Where does d sin θ = nλ come from?

Convert lines per mm to slit spacing in m: 300 lines/mm = 3.0 × 105 lines/m, so d = 1/3.0×105 = 3.33 × 10−6 m. For the maximum order, use sin θ = 1 and round down.

Applications and Required practical 2 (part 2)

Spectral analysis

A grating spreads light into a spectrum. Each element gives a unique line spectrum, so gratings (in spectrometers) identify elements — in the lab or in distant stars.

X-ray crystallography

The regular rows of atoms in a crystal act like a grating for X-rays, whose wavelength (~10−10 m) is similar to the atomic spacing. The diffraction pattern reveals the crystal structure — used to find the structure of DNA.

RP2: measuring λ with a grating

A laser shines through a grating onto a screen. Measure the distance from the central maximum to the n-th order maximum (x) and from the grating to the screen (D). Then θ = tan−1(x/D).

Screen distance D: m
Order measured:
Measure x to the left and right n-th orders and halve the total — this avoids error if the central maximum isn’t at your zero. Using higher orders gives larger angles and a smaller % uncertainty.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Single slit: bright central maximum (twice as wide), dimmer side maxima. Narrower slit or longer λ → wider central maximum.
  • White light single slit: white centre, coloured edges.
  • Grating: d sin θ = nλ; d = 1/(lines per metre); max order: n ≤ d/λ, round down.
  • Applications: spectral analysis, X-ray crystallography.
  • RP2: θ = tan⁻¹(x/D); measure both sides; use higher orders.
