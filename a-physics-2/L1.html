What is a progressive wave?

A progressive (travelling) wave transfers energy from one place to another without transferring matter. The particles of the medium just oscillate about their rest positions. Watch the red particle.

The wave equation and wave graphs

c = fλ     f = 1/T

In one period T the wave moves forward one wavelength λ, so speed = λ/T = fλ.

Two graphs that look the same — but aren’t

Always read the x-axis label first. Peak-to-peak on a distance axis is the wavelength; on a time axis it is the period.

Phase and phase difference

Phase describes where a particle is in its cycle. Phase difference compares two points: how far one is ahead of the other, as a fraction of a cycle. Move point Q.

Distance from P to Q: cm (λ = 40 cm)
phase difference = 2π Δx / λ radians  =  360° × Δx / λ
One whole wavelength = 2π rad = 360°. In phase: Δx = 0, λ, 2λ …   Antiphase (π rad, 180°): Δx = λ/2, 3λ/2 …

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Progressive waves transfer energy, not matter; particles oscillate about rest positions.
  • c = fλ and f = 1/T.
  • Displacement–distance graph shows λ; displacement–time graph shows T.
  • Phase difference = 2πΔx/λ rad (360°Δx/λ). In phase: whole wavelengths apart. Antiphase: half a wavelength (π rad).
