Interference, path difference and coherence

Two sources sending out waves produce an interference pattern where the waves superpose. Tap anywhere on the pattern to measure the path difference to that point.

Source separation: λ
Wavelength: px
Tap a point on the pattern.
Path difference = difference in distance from the two sources to a point.
Constructive (maximum): path difference = nλ (waves arrive in phase).
Destructive (minimum): path difference = (n + ½)λ (waves arrive in antiphase).
A stable pattern needs coherent sources: the same frequency (wavelength) and a constant phase difference. A laser gives coherent, monochromatic (single wavelength) light, so one laser beam shone on two slits makes two coherent sources.

Young’s double-slit experiment

Wavelength λ: nm
Slit separation s: mm
Distance to screen D: m
fringe spacing w = λDs   (valid when D ≫ s)

White light

Each wavelength makes its own fringes. The central fringe is white (zero path difference for every colour). Other fringes are coloured, with blue/violet nearest the centre and red furthest out, because red has the longest λ and so the widest spacing.

Laser safety

Never look into the beam or at reflections; avoid shiny surfaces in the beam path; point it away from people (e.g. below eye level); display a warning sign; use the lowest power laser possible.

Measure across many fringes (e.g. 10) and divide: this reduces the percentage uncertainty in w. Measure D with a metre rule or tape measure (large, so small % uncertainty); s is usually given by the manufacturer or measured with a travelling microscope.

Evidence for the wave nature of light

In 1801 Thomas Young’s double-slit experiment showed interference — something only waves can do. It challenged Newton’s particle (corpuscular) theory, which most scientists had accepted because of Newton’s reputation. The wave theory became accepted as more evidence (diffraction, Maxwell’s EM theory) supported it — an example of how scientific knowledge changes when new evidence appears.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Constructive: path difference nλ (in phase). Destructive: (n + ½)λ (antiphase).
  • Coherent = same frequency and constant phase difference. Lasers are coherent and monochromatic.
  • w = λD/s. White light: white central fringe, coloured fringes with blue nearest the centre.
  • Measure across many fringes to reduce uncertainty; follow laser safety rules.
  • Young’s experiment was key evidence for the wave nature of light.
