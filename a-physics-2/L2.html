Transverse and longitudinal waves

Transverse

Oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer.
Examples: all electromagnetic waves, waves on a string, S-waves (earthquakes), ripples on water.

Longitudinal

Oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer, making compressions and rarefactions.
Examples: sound, P-waves (earthquakes), compression waves on a slinky.

Electromagnetic waves are transverse oscillations of electric and magnetic fields, at right angles to each other. They need no medium and all travel at the same speed in a vacuum, c = 3.00 × 108 m s−1. Sound needs a medium and cannot travel through a vacuum.

Polarisation

A plane-polarised wave oscillates in one plane only. A polarising filter only lets through the component of oscillation in one direction. Only transverse waves can be polarised — longitudinal waves already oscillate in just one direction (along the wave).

Polarisation is evidence that a wave is transverse. Light can be polarised, so light is a transverse wave. Two filters at 90° (“crossed”) block all of the light.

The exact intensity between the filters (Malus’s law) is not on the spec — just know that it falls from maximum at 0° to zero at 90°.

Applications of polarisation

Polaroid sunglasses

Light reflected from water, glass or roads is partially polarised (mostly horizontally). Polaroid sunglasses have vertical transmission axes, so they block much of this reflected light and reduce glare. Photographers use polarising filters for the same reason.

TV and radio aerials

Transmitters send out plane-polarised radio waves. The receiving aerial’s rods must be aligned with the plane of polarisation of the transmitter for the strongest signal.

Lesson summary

  • Transverse: oscillations ⟂ direction of energy transfer (EM waves). Longitudinal: oscillations ∥ direction of energy transfer (sound).
  • All EM waves travel at c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ in a vacuum.
  • Polarised = oscillations in one plane only. Only transverse waves can be polarised → evidence for transverse nature.
  • Applications: Polaroid sunglasses reduce glare; aerials aligned with the transmitter’s plane of polarisation.
