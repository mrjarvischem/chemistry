Each dot is one place the electron could be found. and show the sign of the wave function there. dots lie in the shell at radius r. Drag the picture to turn it.

Three graphs, one distance axis

1. Wave function, R(r)

The radial part of ψ. It can be positive or negative. Where it crosses zero there is a radial node.

2. Probability density, R(r)²

The chance of finding the electron in one tiny volume at distance r. This sets how tightly the dots are packed.

3. Radial probability, r²R(r)²

The chance of finding the electron anywhere in a thin shell at distance r: density × size of the shell (area 4πr²). Bars count the dots at each distance.

Distance r is in Bohr radii (a₀ = 52.9 pm). Drag on any graph to move the shell.
