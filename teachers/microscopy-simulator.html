Estimate the size of a cell

  • On low power, lay the clear ruler across the field and read its width in mm.
  • Count how many cells fit across the field.
  • Cell size = width of field ÷ number of cells.
  • 1 mm = 1000 µm.

Magnification practice

magnification = size of image ÷ size of real object

Biological drawing checklist

  • Sharp pencil, clear continuous lines, no shading or colour
  • Draw only what you can see, large enough to fill half the page
  • Ruled label lines that touch the structure, no arrowheads
  • Title with the specimen and the magnification
Teacher notes

Method this models

  • Peel a single layer of onion epidermis, mount it in water on a slide, add iodine solution and lower a coverslip at an angle.
  • Start with the lowest power objective and the coarse focus, then move to higher power and use only the fine focus.
  • Draw and label a few cells and record the magnification.

Questions to draw out

  • Why start on low power? Compare how much of the slide you see at ×40 and ×400.
  • Why does the image go darker and blur when you switch to the ×40 objective?
  • Why is a stain needed? Which structures show up? Which cannot be seen with a light microscope?
  • Which structures are in the onion cells but not the cheek cells?
  • Estimate the length of one onion cell from the ruler on low power, then check it with the scale bar on high power.

The data are simulated. Cell sizes are typical values: onion epidermal cells about 0.3 mm long, cheek cells about 0.06 mm across.
