Teacher notes

Variables

  • Independent: direction of light (or the direction of gravity relative to the seed).
  • Dependent: height of the seedlings and the direction they grow.
  • Control: number and type of seeds, volume of water, temperature, age of seedlings.

Method this models

  • Germinate mustard or cress seeds on damp cotton wool in three Petri dishes.
  • Put one dish in full light, one in a box with a slit on one side, and one in a dark cupboard.
  • Measure the height of each seedling every day for a week and draw labelled diagrams.

Questions to draw out

  • Which seedlings are tallest? Why are the seedlings in the dark tall, thin and yellow?
  • Switch on auxin for the one-sided light. Where does auxin collect, and what does it do to those cells in a shoot?
  • Why is a mean of several seedlings used?
  • In the gravity investigation, roots and shoots respond in opposite directions to the same stimulus. How does auxin explain this?
  • Why are these responses an advantage to the plant?

The data are simulated. Growth rates and bending angles are typical values for mustard seedlings and bean roots.
