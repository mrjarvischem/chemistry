Teacher notes

Variables

  • Independent: distance of the lamp from the pondweed (which sets the light intensity).
  • Dependent: number of bubbles of oxygen released in one minute.
  • Control: same piece of pondweed, temperature (beaker of water as a heat shield, LED lamp), concentration of hydrogencarbonate, counting time.

Method this models

  • Pondweed, cut end uppermost, in a boiling tube of sodium hydrogencarbonate solution inside a beaker of water.
  • Lamp at 10 cm. Leave the pondweed to adjust, then count the bubbles for one minute. Repeat twice.
  • Move the lamp to 20, 30, 40 and 50 cm and repeat.

Questions to draw out

  • Count along at ×1 with the live counter off. How far apart are the class counts at 10 cm, and why?
  • Why is counting bubbles a weak measure of oxygen volume? Bubbles differ in size; a gas syringe is better.
  • Higher tier: doubling the distance from 10 cm to 20 cm quarters the light intensity. Check it against the 1 ÷ d² column.
  • Plot against light intensity. Why does the line curve over at high intensity? Compare the three hydrogencarbonate solutions to show carbon dioxide becoming the limiting factor.

The data are simulated from a limiting-factor model; real pondweed is far less predictable.
