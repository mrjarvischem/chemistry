Teacher notes

Variables

  • Independent: potential difference across the component.
  • Dependent: current through the component.
  • Control: the component itself and, for the resistor, its temperature (small currents, switch off between readings).

Method this models

  • Connect the component in series with the ammeter and the variable supply. Connect the voltmeter in parallel across the component.
  • Increase the supply in steps, recording the p.d. and the current each time.
  • Reverse the supply connections and repeat to get the negative readings.
  • For the diode, add a protective resistor in series and use a milliammeter, because the currents are small.
  • Plot current (y-axis) against potential difference (x-axis).

Questions to draw out

  • Resistor: why is the graph a straight line through the origin? Compare 10 Ω, 20 Ω and 50 Ω: which line is steepest, and why?
  • Lamp: show the resistance column. Why does the resistance rise as the p.d. increases? Link to the filament getting hotter.
  • Diode: why is there almost no current with the supply reversed? Roughly what p.d. is needed before it conducts?
  • Diode: why does the voltmeter reading stay well below the supply setting once the diode conducts? The rest of the p.d. is across the protective resistor.
  • Why do the 50 Ω readings look stepped? The ammeter only reads to 0.01 A.

The data are simulated. The lamp is modelled on a 6 V, 0.3 A filament bulb, and the diode on a silicon diode with a 100 Ω protective resistor.
