Life cycle assessments (LCAs)

A life cycle assessment is carried out to assess the environmental impact of a product at each stage of its life.

Limitations of LCAs

Use of water, resources, energy sources and production of some wastes can be fairly easily quantified. But allocating numerical values to pollutant effects is less straightforward and needs value judgements, so LCAs are not purely objective.

Selective or abbreviated LCAs can be devised to evaluate a product, but these can be misused to reach pre-determined conclusions — for example, in support of claims for advertising purposes.

Reducing the use of resources

Reducing, reusing and recycling materials reduces the use of limited resources, the use of energy sources, waste and environmental impacts.

Metals, glass, building materials, clay ceramics and most plastics are produced from limited raw materials. Much of the energy used comes from limited resources (fossil fuels). Obtaining raw materials from the Earth by quarrying and mining causes environmental impacts.

Glass

Glass bottles can be reused. Other glass products are crushed and melted to make different glass products.

Metals

Metals are recycled by melting and recasting or reforming into different products. The amount of separation needed depends on the metal and the product — e.g. some scrap steel can be added to iron from a blast furnace to reduce the amount of iron that needs to be extracted from iron ore.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • LCA stages: extracting and processing raw materials; manufacturing and packaging; use and operation; disposal at end of life.
  • Energy, water and resource use are easy to quantify; pollutant effects need value judgements.
  • Selective LCAs can be misused, e.g. in advertising.
  • Glass is reused or crushed and melted; metals are melted and recast.
