Walkthrough 6 · Corrosion and its prevention (chemistry only)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.10.3.1 · Chemistry only — not Combined Science · about 25 minutes

Corrosion CHEMISTRY ONLY

Corrosion is the destruction of materials by chemical reactions with substances in the environment. Rusting is an example of corrosion — it only happens to iron and steel.

iron + oxygen + water → hydrated iron(III) oxide (rust)
Aluminium is more reactive than iron but does not corrode much: it has a thin oxide layer that forms on its surface and protects the metal underneath. Rust is flaky and falls off, exposing more iron.

Preventing corrosion: barriers

Corrosion can be prevented by applying a coating that acts as a barrier, stopping air and water reaching the metal.

Barrier coatings

  • greasing / oiling (moving parts, e.g. bike chains)
  • painting (cars, bridges)
  • electroplating (coating with a thin layer of another metal, e.g. tin cans)
  • plastic coating

Problem

If the coating is scratched or damaged, air and water reach the iron and it rusts.

Sacrificial protection and galvanising

Some coatings are reactive and contain, or act as, a more reactive metal. This provides sacrificial protection: the more reactive metal oxidises (corrodes) instead of the iron.

Galvanising: iron is coated with zinc. The zinc is a barrier, and if scratched, the zinc still corrodes in preference to the iron (sacrificial protection).

Summary

  • Corrosion: destruction of materials by reactions with substances in the environment.
  • Rusting needs both air (oxygen) and water.
  • Barrier methods: greasing, painting, electroplating.
  • Sacrificial protection: a more reactive metal (e.g. Mg, Zn) corrodes instead of iron. Galvanising = zinc coating.
