Walkthrough 8 · The Haber process (chemistry only)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.10.4.1 · Chemistry only — not Combined Science · Higher tier for the compromise conditions · about 35 minutes

The Haber process CHEMISTRY ONLY

The Haber process is used to manufacture ammonia, which can be used to produce nitrogen-based fertilisers.

{N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3}

The forward reaction is exothermic.

Raw materials and conditions

Raw materials

  • Nitrogen is obtained from the air.
  • Hydrogen may be obtained from natural gas (methane) or other sources.

Conditions

  • temperature: about 450 °C
  • pressure: about 200 atmospheres
  • catalyst: iron
On cooling, the ammonia liquefies and is removed. The remaining hydrogen and nitrogen are recycled back into the reactor.

Explore the conditions HT

Change the temperature and pressure. Watch the yield, the rate and the cost.

Yields are approximate equilibrium values. In practice the gases pass through the reactor too quickly to reach equilibrium, so only about 15% is converted on each pass — which is why the unreacted gases are recycled.

Why 450 °C and 200 atm? HT

Temperature

Equilibrium: if T increases, the equilibrium shifts to decrease T to oppose the change, so it moves in the endothermic direction — to the left. So a lower temperature gives a higher yield.

Rate: a lower temperature gives a slower rate.

450 °C is a compromise between a reasonable yield and a fast enough rate. The iron catalyst helps the rate.

Pressure

Equilibrium: if P increases, the equilibrium shifts to decrease P to oppose the change, so it moves to the side with fewer molecules of gas — left 1 + 3 = 4, right 2 — so to the right. A higher pressure gives a higher yield and a faster rate.

But high pressure is expensive (energy to compress the gases, strong equipment) and dangerous. 200 atm is a compromise.

The catalyst does not change the yield. It increases the rate so equilibrium is reached faster — allowing a lower temperature to be used.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • {N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3}; forward reaction exothermic.
  • N₂ from air; H₂ from natural gas. 450 °C, 200 atm, iron catalyst.
  • Ammonia is liquefied by cooling and removed; unreacted gases are recycled.
  • HT: lower T → higher yield but slower rate; higher P → higher yield and rate, but expensive. Conditions are a compromise.
