AQA A-level Physics 3.5.1.3 · including Required Practical 5 · about 25 minutes

Resistivity

resistivity ρ = RA/L (Ω m) — a property of the material. So R = ρL/A: longer → more resistance; thicker → less.

RP5: resistivity of a wire

Length between crocodile clips: m
Wire: · diameter 0.32 mm
Plot R against L: a straight line through the origin with gradient = ρ/A, so ρ = gradient × A. Measure d with a micrometer at several points; use a low current (or switch off between readings) so the wire doesn’t heat up and change its resistance.

Thermistors and superconductivity

Temperature: °C

An NTC thermistor’s resistance decreases as temperature increases (more charge carriers are released). Used as a temperature sensor.

Superconductivity

Below its critical temperature, a superconductor has zero resistivity. Uses: power cables with no energy loss, and very strong magnetic fields (MRI scanners, particle accelerators) without heating.
