Lesson 1 · Current, potential difference and resistance

AQA A-level Physics 3.5.1.1 · about 25 minutes

Charge and current

current I = ΔQ/Δt — the rate of flow of charge (A = C s⁻¹). Charge carriers in metals are electrons; the charge on an electron is −1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.
Current: A
Conventional current flows from + to −. Electrons actually drift the other way, from − to +.

Potential difference and resistance

potential difference V = W/Q — the work done (energy transferred) per unit charge (V = J C⁻¹)
resistance R = V/I (Ω)
Battery: V
Resistor: Ω
An ideal ammeter has zero resistance (connected in series). An ideal voltmeter has infinite resistance (connected in parallel). Neither changes the circuit it is measuring.

Exam practice

e = 1.60 × 10−19 C.

Lesson summary

  • I = ΔQ/Δt; 1 A = 1 C s⁻¹.
  • V = W/Q; 1 V = 1 J C⁻¹.
  • R = V/I; 1 Ω = 1 V A⁻¹.
  • Ideal ammeter: zero resistance; ideal voltmeter: infinite resistance.
