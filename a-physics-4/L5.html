Lesson 5 · Potential dividers

AQA A-level Physics 3.5.1.5 · about 25 minutes

The potential divider

Two resistors in series share the supply p.d. in the ratio of their resistances: Vout = Vin × R2/(R1 + R2)  (Vout across R2)
Vin: V
R₁: kΩ
R₂: kΩ
Swap R₂ for a thermistor or LDR to make a sensor: as the temperature or light level changes, its resistance changes, so Vout changes. An LDR’s resistance falls as light intensity increases.

A potentiometer

A variable resistor used as a potential divider (a potentiometer) can give any output from 0 V up to Vin. Used as a simple variable resistor in series, it cannot reduce the output to zero. That’s why potential dividers are used for volume controls and to vary the p.d. across a component from zero (e.g. when plotting I–V characteristics).

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Vout = Vin R₂/(R₁ + R₂); the bigger resistor gets the bigger share.
  • Thermistor/LDR in a divider → temperature or light sensor.
  • A potentiometer can vary the output from 0 to Vin.
