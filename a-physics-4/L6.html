EMF and internal resistance

emf ε = energy transferred to electrical energy per unit charge by the source: ε = E/Q
Real cells have internal resistance r: ε = I(R + r) = V + Ir. The “lost volts” Ir are energy per coulomb wasted inside the cell.
emf ε: 6.0 V · internal r: Ω
Load R: Ω

Required practical 6: measuring ε and r

Vary R, recording terminal p.d. V and current I. Since V = ε − Ir, a graph of V against I is a straight line: y-intercept = ε, gradient = −r.

Load R: Ω
Include a protective resistor and open the switch between readings so the cell doesn’t warm up (r would change) or run down. A cell’s r is often small, so use a fresh cell or add a known resistor in series as part of “r”.

Exam practice

Lesson summary — and the end of section 3.5!

  • ε = E/Q; ε = I(R + r) = V + Ir.
  • Terminal p.d. falls as current increases (more lost volts).
  • RP6: V–I graph, intercept = ε, gradient = −r.
  • Cells in series: emfs and internal resistances add. Identical cells in parallel: same emf, smaller r.
