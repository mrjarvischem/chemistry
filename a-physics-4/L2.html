Lesson 2 · Current–voltage characteristics

AQA A-level Physics 3.5.1.2 · about 25 minutes

I–V characteristics

Choose a component and drag the voltage (it can be reversed). The point traces out the component’s characteristic.

p.d.: V
Ohm’s law: for an ohmic conductor at constant temperature, I is directly proportional to V (straight line through the origin, constant R).
Resistance at a point = V/I — not the gradient of the graph.

Explaining the shapes

Filament lamp

More current → filament gets hotter → metal ions vibrate more → electrons collide more often → resistance increases. The graph curves (gets less steep).

Semiconductor diode

Current flows in one direction only (forward bias), once the p.d. is above about 0.6 V for silicon. In reverse bias the resistance is very high.

Ohmic resistor

Constant temperature → constant resistance → straight line through the origin.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Ohmic conductor: I ∝ V at constant temperature.
  • Filament lamp: R increases with current (temperature).
  • Diode: conducts above ~0.6 V forward; very high resistance in reverse.
  • R = V/I at a point, not the gradient.
