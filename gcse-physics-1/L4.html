Lesson 4 · Elastic potential energy

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Energy 4.1.1.2 · about 25 minutes

The elastic potential store

Stretching or squashing a spring transfers energy to its elastic potential store. Let go, and that energy can be transferred to other stores.

Ee = ½ k e2 Given on the equation sheet
SymbolQuantityUnit
Eeelastic potential energyjoules, J
kspring constant — how stiff the spring isnewtons per metre, N/m
eextension (how much longer it has got)metres, m

The equation only works if the limit of proportionality has not been exceeded.

Extension e = cm
Just like kinetic energy, the extension is squared. Double the extension → four times the energy stored.

Extension, not length

Two traps catch lots of students: using the length instead of the extension, and forgetting to convert cm to m.

extension = stretched length − original length

A spring 12 cm long is stretched to 20 cm. The extension is 20 − 12 = 8 cm = 0.08 m — not 20 cm.

What the graph shows

The energy stored is the area under the force–extension graph. For the straight part that area is a triangle: ½ × F × e = ½ × (ke) × e = ½ k e2.

Catapults and launchers

A stretched catapult launches a stone straight up. Step through the energy changes (ignoring air resistance).

Energy stores (J)

For chains like this, the energy at each stage is the same number (in a closed system). Work out the energy once, then use it in the next equation.

Lesson summary

  • Ee = ½ k e2 (equation sheet). k in N/m, e in m.
  • Extension = stretched length − original length.
  • Only valid up to the limit of proportionality (the straight part of the force–extension graph).
  • Energy chains: elastic → kinetic → gravitational. In a closed system the energy is the same at each stage.
