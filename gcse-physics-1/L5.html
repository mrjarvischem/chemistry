Specific heat capacity

Give the same energy to 1 kg of water and 1 kg of copper, and the copper gets much hotter. Different materials need different amounts of energy to warm up.

The specific heat capacity of a substance is the amount of energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of the substance by 1 °C. Unit: J/kg °C.

Same energy, same mass — different temperature rise

Energy supplied to each 1 kg block: J
Water has a very high specific heat capacity (4200 J/kg °C). That is why it is used in central heating and hot-water bottles: it stores a lot of energy for each degree, so it cools down slowly.

The equation

ΔE = m c Δθ Given on the equation sheet
SymbolQuantityUnit
ΔEchange in thermal energyjoules, J
mmasskilograms, kg
cspecific heat capacityJ/kg °C
Δθtemperature change (θ is "theta")°C
Δθ is the change in temperature: final − start. Heating water from 20 °C to 75 °C → Δθ = 55 °C, not 75 °C.

Worked example — step through it

How much energy is needed to heat 0.50 kg of water from 18 °C to 100 °C? c = 4200 J/kg °C.

Required practical: measuring c

An electric heater and a thermometer are put into holes in a 1.0 kg aluminium block. A joulemeter measures the energy supplied. Run the experiment.

Improving accuracy

  • Insulate the block — reduces energy lost to the surroundings.
  • Put a drop of water or oil in the thermometer hole — better thermal contact.
  • Use the gradient of a temperature–energy graph rather than one pair of readings.

Why the answer comes out too high

Some energy from the heater warms the surroundings, not the block. So the temperature rise is smaller than it should be. Dividing by a smaller Δθ gives a bigger value of c than the true value (900 J/kg °C).

Lesson summary

  • Specific heat capacity: energy to raise 1 kg by 1 °C. Unit J/kg °C.
  • ΔE = m c Δθ (equation sheet). Δθ = final − start temperature.
  • Required practical: heater + joulemeter + thermometer in a block. c = ΔE ÷ (m Δθ).
  • Energy lost to the surroundings makes the measured c too high — insulate the block.
