Lesson 2 · Kinetic energy

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Energy 4.1.1.2 · about 25 minutes

Kinetic energy

Every moving object has energy in its kinetic store. How much depends on its mass and its speed.

Ek = ½ m v2 Learn this — not on the equation sheet
SymbolQuantityUnit
Ekkinetic energyjoules, J
mmasskilograms, kg
vspeedmetres per second, m/s
Square the speed first, then multiply by the mass, then halve.
Example: a 2 kg ball at 3 m/s → Ek = ½ × 2 × 32 = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J
Writing ½ × 2 × 3 = 3 J and then squaring, or doing (½ × 2 × 3)2. Only the speed is squared.

What matters more — mass or speed?

Change the mass and speed of the vehicle and watch the kinetic energy. Use the × 2 buttons to compare before and after.

Mass m = kg
Speed v = m/s

Kinetic store

Ek is proportional to mass: double the mass → double the energy.
Ek is proportional to speed squared: double the speed → four times the energy. Triple the speed → nine times.

Finding mass or speed

Exams often give you the energy and ask for the speed. Rearrange first, or put the numbers in and solve.

Finding mass

m = 2 Ek ÷ v2

Finding speed

v = √(2 Ek ÷ m)

Worked example — step through it

A 60 kg runner has 1920 J in her kinetic store. Calculate her speed.

Don’t forget the square root at the end — it is the most common lost mark. Check your answer is sensible: a runner at 64 m/s would be faster than a racing car!

Exam practice

Show your working — most marks are for the steps, not just the final answer.

Lesson summary

  • Ek = ½ m v2 — learn it. Units: J, kg, m/s.
  • Square the speed first. Convert g → kg (÷ 1000) and km/h → m/s if needed.
  • Double the mass → double Ek. Double the speed → four times Ek.
  • To find speed: v = √(2Ek ÷ m) — remember the square root.
