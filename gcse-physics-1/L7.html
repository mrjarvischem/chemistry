Lesson 7 · Wasted energy, conductivity and insulation

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Energy 4.1.2.1 · about 25 minutes

Conservation and dissipation

Energy can be transferred usefully, stored or dissipated — but it cannot be created or destroyed.

Whenever there is a change in a system, some energy ends up spread out in the surroundings — usually warming them up a tiny bit. This is wasted energy: it is still there, but it is so spread out that it is no longer useful. We say it has been dissipated.

Useful vs wasted

Reducing unwanted transfers

  • Lubrication (oil, grease) reduces friction between moving parts.
  • Thermal insulation reduces energy transferred by heating.
  • Streamlining reduces air resistance.

Thermal conductivity and buildings

The higher the thermal conductivity of a material, the higher the rate of energy transfer through it by conduction. Insulate this house and watch the energy loss.

Wall thickness: cm
The rate of cooling of a building depends on the thickness of its walls and their thermal conductivity. Thicker walls and lower-conductivity materials → slower energy transfer → the house cools more slowly.
Cavity wall insulation and loft insulation work because they trap air (or use foam). Air has a very low thermal conductivity, and trapping it stops it moving and carrying energy away.

Investigating insulators Physics only

Combined Science students: this practical is not required, but the ideas help with exam questions. A beaker of hot water is wrapped in different numbers of layers of newspaper. The temperature is recorded every 2 minutes.

Choose the number of layers and record a curve. Try several.

Making it a fair test

Independent variable: number of layers (or type of material). Dependent variable: temperature drop in a set time. Control variables: volume of water, starting temperature, same beaker, a lid on each time, same total time.

Lesson summary

  • Energy can be transferred, stored or dissipated, but not created or destroyed.
  • Wasted energy is dissipated — spread out into the surroundings, usually by heating.
  • Reduce unwanted transfers: lubrication, thermal insulation, streamlining.
  • Higher thermal conductivity → higher rate of energy transfer. Thicker walls with low conductivity → building cools more slowly.
