Lesson 3 · Gravitational potential energy and falling objects

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Energy 4.1.1.2 · about 25 minutes

Gravitational potential energy

Lifting an object up transfers energy to its gravitational potential store (g.p.e.). The higher you lift it, and the heavier it is, the more energy it gains.

Ep = m g h Learn this — not on the equation sheet

Mass m = kg

Falling: g.p.e. → kinetic

When an object falls, its gravitational potential store empties and its kinetic store fills up. Drop the ball and watch the stores.

Drop height = m
Mass = kg

Energy stores (J)

Ignoring air resistance: g.p.e. lost = kinetic energy gained
m g h = ½ m v2 — so a heavy ball and a light ball dropped from the same height hit the ground at the same speed.
With air resistance, some energy is transferred to the thermal store of the surroundings (and the ball), so less ends up in the kinetic store and the ball lands more slowly.

Two-step problems

"Calculate the speed it hits the ground" needs both equations: work out the g.p.e. lost, then use it as the kinetic energy gained.

A 0.20 kg apple falls 1.8 m from a tree. g = 9.8 N/kg. Ignoring air resistance, calculate its speed just before it lands.

Write "g.p.e. lost = kinetic energy gained" as your first line. It earns the method mark even if you slip later.

Exam practice

Use g = 9.8 N/kg.

Lesson summary

  • Ep = m g h — learn it. g = 9.8 N/kg on Earth.
  • h is the change in height. Mass must be in kg.
  • Falling (no air resistance): g.p.e. lost = kinetic energy gained, so m g h = ½ m v2.
  • Air resistance transfers some energy to the thermal store of the surroundings.
