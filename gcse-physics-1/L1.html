Lesson 1 · Energy stores and systems

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Energy 4.1.1.1 · about 25 minutes

Energy stores

Energy is not a "thing" you can see. Physicists keep track of it by saying it is held in different stores. When anything happens, energy moves from one store to another.

Tap a store to see what it means and an example.
There are 8 energy stores you need to know: kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical, nuclear, magnetic, electrostatic. Energy is measured in joules (J).

How energy is transferred

Energy moves between stores in four ways. These are called the transfer pathways.

💪

Mechanically

A force moves an object — work is done. e.g. pushing a trolley, a spring launching a ball.
⚡

Electrically

A current flows — charges do work. e.g. a battery running a motor.
🔥

By heating

Because of a temperature difference. e.g. a hob heating a pan of water.
🌈

By radiation

Carried by waves — light, infrared or sound. e.g. the Sun warming the Earth.
"Light energy", "sound energy" and "electrical energy" are not stores. Light and sound are ways energy is transferred (by radiation). Electricity is a pathway, not a store.

Systems and energy changes

A system is just the object or group of objects you are looking at. When a system changes, energy moves between its stores. Pick a situation and step through it.

Energy in each store (J)

In a closed system (nothing can get in or out) there is no net change to the total energy. Energy is never made or destroyed — it is only transferred between stores. This is the law of conservation of energy.

Lesson summary

  • 8 stores: kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical, nuclear, magnetic, electrostatic.
  • 4 pathways: mechanically (a force doing work), electrically, by heating, by radiation.
  • A system is the object or objects being studied. In a closed system the total energy stays the same.
  • Always name the store energy leaves and the store it goes to.
