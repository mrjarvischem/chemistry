Lesson 6 · Power and efficiency

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Energy 4.1.1.4 and 4.1.2.2 · about 25 minutes

Power

Power is the rate at which energy is transferred, or the rate at which work is done. A more powerful machine does the same job in less time.

P = E ÷ tP = W ÷ t Learn both
SymbolQuantityUnit
Ppowerwatts, W
Eenergy transferredjoules, J
Wwork donejoules, J

1 watt = 1 joule per second. 1 kW = 1000 W.

Motor race: both motors lift a 10 kg load through 5 m

Power calculations

Time must be in seconds. 2 minutes = 120 s. And power in kW must be changed to W (× 1000) before you use it to find energy in joules.

Efficiency

No device transfers all its energy usefully — some is always wasted, usually by heating the surroundings. Efficiency tells you what fraction is useful.

efficiency = useful output energy transfer ÷ total input energy transfer
efficiency = useful power output ÷ total power input Learn both

Efficiency has no unit. Give it as a decimal (0.25) or × 100 as a percentage (25%). It can never be more than 1 (100%).

Total input: J
Useful output: J

Increasing efficiency Higher tier

  • Lubricate moving parts — less friction, so less energy heats the machine.
  • Insulate — less energy lost by heating, e.g. a kettle with an insulated wall.
  • Use wires with low resistance — less heating in the wires.
  • Streamline shapes — less air resistance.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Power = rate of energy transfer. P = E ÷ t and P = W ÷ t. 1 W = 1 J/s.
  • Time in seconds; kW × 1000 → W.
  • Efficiency = useful output ÷ total input (energy or power). Never more than 1 (100%).
  • Wasted energy = total input − useful output. Reduce it by lubricating, insulating and lowering resistance.
