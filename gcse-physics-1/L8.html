Lesson 8 · National and global energy resources

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Energy 4.1.3 · about 25 minutes

Energy resources

We use energy resources for transport, electricity generation and heating. Tap each one. A green line means renewable, red means non-renewable.

Tap a resource.

A renewable resource is one that is being (or can be) replenished as it is used. Non-renewable resources will run out: fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas) and nuclear fuel.

Reliability: supply vs demand

Demand for electricity changes during the day. Build a mix of power stations and see whether supply meets demand over 24 hours.

Wind and solar depend on the weather and time of day, so they are not reliable. Fossil-fuel and nuclear stations can supply energy at any time. Gas stations can be started quickly when demand suddenly rises.

Patterns, trends and big decisions

The trend

The UK is generating less electricity from coal and more from renewables such as wind and solar. Reasons: burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide (linked to climate change) and other pollutants, and fossil fuels will run out.

Why can’t we switch faster?

  • Building new power stations and wind farms costs a lot of money.
  • Renewables are less reliable — back-up is needed.
  • Some people object to wind turbines near their homes, or to nuclear power.
  • Changes to cars and heating systems take time and money.
Science can identify environmental problems caused by energy use, but cannot always solve them — political, social, ethical and economic factors affect what actually happens.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Uses: transport, electricity generation, heating.
  • Non-renewable: coal, oil, gas, nuclear. Renewable: wind, solar, hydro-electric, tidal, waves, geothermal, biofuel.
  • Fossil fuels: reliable, but release CO2 and will run out. Nuclear: reliable, no CO2, but radioactive waste.
  • Wind and solar: no fuel cost or CO2 while running, but unreliable. Tidal: reliable but few suitable sites.
  • Comparing resources: reliability, environmental impact, cost, and whether it is renewable.
