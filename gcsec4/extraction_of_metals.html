Walkthrough 2 · Extraction of metals and reduction

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.4.1.3 · Combined Science 5.4.1.3 · about 25 minutes

Where do metals come from?

Unreactive metals such as gold are found in the Earth as the metal itself. Most metals are found as compounds (often oxides), so a chemical reaction is needed to extract the metal.

An ore is a rock that contains enough of a metal compound to make extracting the metal worthwhile. Extracting a metal from its oxide means removing the oxygen — so it is a reduction.

Reduction with carbon

Metals less reactive than carbon can be extracted by heating their oxide with carbon. Carbon takes the oxygen away: the metal oxide is reduced and the carbon is oxidised to carbon dioxide.

Count the atoms: the number of each type of atom is the same on both sides. The coefficient (big number) in front of a formula multiplies every atom in that formula.

Choosing the extraction method

Where a metal sits compared with carbon in the reactivity series decides how it is extracted. Pick a metal oxide and try heating it with carbon.

Tap a metal

Below carbon (zinc, iron, copper, lead…): reduce the oxide by heating with carbon. Above carbon (aluminium, magnesium, sodium…): carbon cannot remove the oxygen, so electrolysis is used — this uses lots of electricity, so it is expensive. Very unreactive (gold): found as the metal.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Gold (and other very unreactive metals) are found as the metal itself.
  • Most metals are found as compounds, e.g. oxides, in ores.
  • Metals less reactive than carbon: heat the oxide with carbon. The oxide is reduced (loses oxygen); carbon is oxidised.
  • Metals more reactive than carbon: extracted by electrolysis.
