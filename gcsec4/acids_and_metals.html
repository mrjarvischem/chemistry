Walkthrough 4 · Reactions of acids with metals

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.4.2.1 · Combined Science 5.4.2.1 · about 25 minutes

Metal + acid → salt + hydrogen

Metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series react with dilute acids. The products are a salt and hydrogen gas.

Metal

Acid

metal + acid → salt + hydrogen. Test for hydrogen: a lit splint held at the mouth of the tube burns with a squeaky pop.

Naming the salt

The first part of the salt's name comes from the metal. The second part comes from the acid.

AcidFormulaSalts are called…Ion in the salt
hydrochloric acid{HCl}chlorides{Cl^-}
sulfuric acid{H2SO4}sulfates{SO4^2-}
nitric acid{HNO3}nitrates{NO3^-}
+→
It's sulfate, not "sulphide" or "sulfide". Sulfides contain only sulfur (no oxygen) — they are not made from sulfuric acid.

Balanced symbol equations

Hydrochloric acid has one H per formula, so you often need a coefficient (big number) of 2 in front of {HCl} to make the {H2} molecule.

Why is it a redox reaction? HT

Chloride and sulfate ions are spectator ions. The ionic equation shows what really happens:

{Mg(s) + 2H^+(aq) -> Mg^2+(aq) + H2(g)}

Magnesium is oxidised

{Mg -> Mg^2+ + 2e^-}

Mg atoms lose electrons.

Hydrogen ions are reduced

{2H^+ + 2e^- -> H2}

{H^+} ions gain electrons.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • metal + acid → salt + hydrogen (squeaky pop test).
  • Hydrochloric acid → chlorides; sulfuric acid → sulfates; nitric acid → nitrates.
  • {Mg + 2HCl -> MgCl2 + H2}; {Zn + H2SO4 -> ZnSO4 + H2}.
  • HT: the metal is oxidised (loses electrons); {H^+} ions are reduced (gain electrons).
