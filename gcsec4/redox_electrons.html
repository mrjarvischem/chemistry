AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.4.1.4 · Combined Science 5.4.1.4 · Higher tier only · about 25 minutes

Oxidation and reduction: electrons HT

In walkthrough 1, oxidation meant gaining oxygen. For Higher tier you also need a definition in terms of electrons. Watch magnesium displace copper from copper sulfate solution.

Oxidation Is Loss of electrons. Reduction Is Gain of electrons. — OIL RIG. Metal atoms that react are oxidised (they lose electrons to form positive ions). Metal ions that become atoms are reduced.

Ionic equations and spectator ions

In a displacement reaction some ions do not change. They are spectator ions. Tap every ion that is exactly the same on both sides to cross it out — what is left is the ionic equation.

Ionic compounds in solution are written as separate ions. Metals and elements like {Br2} are written as atoms/molecules — they are not split up.

Half equations

An ionic equation can be split into two half equations: one showing the electrons lost (oxidation) and one showing the electrons gained (reduction). Electrons are written as {e^-}.

Oxidation (loss)

{Zn -> Zn^2+ + 2e^-}

Zinc atoms lose 2 electrons. Electrons appear on the right.

Reduction (gain)

{Cu^2+ + 2e^- -> Cu}

Copper ions gain 2 electrons. Electrons appear on the left.

Check a half equation by adding up the charges on each side — they must be equal. {Cu^2+ + 2e^-}: (+2) + (−2) = 0, and Cu has charge 0 ✓

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Oxidation is loss of electrons; reduction is gain of electrons (OIL RIG).
  • Spectator ions are unchanged and are left out of the ionic equation.
  • Displacement ionic equation, e.g. {Mg + Cu^2+ -> Mg^2+ + Cu}.
  • Half equations: {Mg -> Mg^2+ + 2e^-} (oxidised) and {Cu^2+ + 2e^- -> Cu} (reduced).
