Walkthrough 8 · Titrations (chemistry only)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.4.2.5 · Required practical 2 · Chemistry only — not Combined Science · calculations are Higher tier · about 35 minutes

Doing a titration CHEMISTRY ONLY

A titration finds the exact volume of acid needed to neutralise a known volume of alkali. The flask holds 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide (from a pipette) plus a few drops of phenolphthalein (pink in alkali, colourless in acid). The burette holds 0.100 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid.

Method: rinse and fill the burette with acid; record the start reading · pipette 25.0 cm³ of alkali into a conical flask on a white tile · add indicator · add acid while swirling, then drop by drop near the end point · stop when the indicator just changes colour · record the final reading. Titre = final − start. Repeat until you have concordant results.

Reading a burette

Read from the bottom of the meniscus, with your eye level with it. Burette scales go down the tube, so the numbers get bigger as liquid runs out. Readings are recorded to 2 decimal places, ending in 0 or 5.

Concordant results and the mean titre

The first (rough) titration overshoots — it just tells you roughly where the end point is. Results within 0.10 cm³ of each other are concordant. Calculate the mean using only concordant results.

Titration calculations HT

Three steps: (1) moles of the solution you know, (2) use the balanced equation's ratio, (3) concentration of the unknown. Remember: divide cm³ by 1000 to get dm³.

moles = concentration (mol/dm³) × volume (dm³) · concentration = moles ÷ volume (dm³) · g/dm³ = mol/dm³ × Mr

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Pipette the alkali; burette the acid; indicator; swirl; drop by drop near the end point.
  • Titre = final − start reading; concordant = within 0.10 cm³; mean of concordant titres.
  • (HT) moles = c × V(dm³) → ratio from the equation → c = moles ÷ V(dm³); × Mr for g/dm³.
