Walkthrough 11 · Electrolysis of aqueous solutions (required practical 3)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.4.3.4 · Combined Science 5.4.3.4 · Required practical 3 · about 35 minutes

Solutions contain ions from water too

Water contains a small number of hydrogen ions, {H^+}, and hydroxide ions, {OH^-}. So an aqueous solution of sodium chloride contains four types of ion. At each electrode, only one type of ion is discharged.

Ions in NaCl(aq)FromGoes to
{Na^+}the saltcathode (−)
{H^+}watercathode (−)
{Cl^-}the saltanode (+)
{OH^-}wateranode (+)

Two positive ions compete at the cathode, and two negative ions compete at the anode. The rules on the next tab decide which wins.

Which ion is discharged?

At the cathode (−)

Hydrogen is produced if the metal is more reactive than hydrogen. Otherwise the metal is produced (e.g. copper, silver).

At the anode (+)

Oxygen is produced unless the solution contains halide ions ({Cl^-}, {Br^-}, {I^-}), when the halogen is produced.

Sulfate and nitrate ions are not discharged — oxygen comes from the {OH^-} ions instead.

Required practical 3

Investigate what happens when aqueous solutions are electrolysed using inert electrodes.

Safety: wear eye protection. Chlorine is toxic — use small volumes in a well-ventilated room and switch off once the gas has been tested. Use a low voltage (e.g. 4–6 V).

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Aqueous solutions also contain {H^+} and {OH^-} from water.
  • Cathode: hydrogen if the metal is more reactive than hydrogen; otherwise the metal.
  • Anode: oxygen, unless halide ions are present → the halogen.
  • Gas tests: {H2} squeaky pop · {O2} relights glowing splint · {Cl2} bleaches damp litmus paper.
