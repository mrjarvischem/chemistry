Metal oxides: oxidation and reduction

Metals react with oxygen to make metal oxides. When a substance gains oxygen it is oxidised. When a substance loses oxygen it is reduced.

Oxidation = gain of oxygen. Reduction = loss of oxygen. If one substance loses oxygen, another substance must gain it, so oxidation and reduction happen together.

How reactive is each metal?

We can compare metals by how vigorously they react with water and with dilute acid. More bubbles of hydrogen per second means a more vigorous reaction.

The reactivity series

Putting metals in order of reactivity gives the reactivity series. Carbon and hydrogen are non-metals, but they are included because they are used to compare metals.

Tap an element in the series

The reactivity of a metal is related to its tendency to form positive ions. Metals near the top lose their outer electrons most easily, so they form positive ions most easily.

Displacement reactions

A more reactive metal can displace a less reactive metal from a compound. Tap a square in the grid to drop that metal into that solution.

A metal can't displace itself, and a less reactive metal never displaces a more reactive one. Copper in magnesium sulfate solution: no reaction.

Summary

  • Oxidation = gain of oxygen; reduction = loss of oxygen.
  • Reactivity series: potassium, sodium, lithium, calcium, magnesium, (carbon), zinc, iron, (hydrogen), copper.
  • Metals above hydrogen react with dilute acids to make hydrogen; copper does not.
  • Reactivity depends on how easily a metal forms positive ions.
  • A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its compounds.
