Walkthrough 7 · pH and neutralisation

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.4.2.4 · Combined Science 5.4.2.4 · about 25 minutes

The pH scale

Acids produce hydrogen ions, {H^+}, in aqueous solution. Alkalis produce hydroxide ions, {OH^-}. The pH scale (0 to 14) measures how acidic or alkaline a solution is. Drag the slider.

pH < 7: acidic (more {H^+}). pH = 7: neutral. pH > 7: alkaline (more {OH^-}). The lower the pH, the more acidic; the higher the pH, the more alkaline.

Measuring pH

Add a few drops of universal indicator and compare the colour with a chart — or use a pH probe (pH meter) for a more precise reading.

A pH probe gives a numerical reading (e.g. pH 2.4). Universal indicator only gives an approximate whole-number pH by colour matching.

Neutralisation: {H^+ + OH^- -> H2O}

25 cm³ of dilute hydrochloric acid is in a flask with universal indicator. Add sodium hydroxide solution and watch the ions. Each {H^+} meets an {OH^-} to make a water molecule.

In every neutralisation of an acid by an alkali, hydrogen ions react with hydroxide ions to make water: {H^+(aq) + OH^-(aq) -> H2O(l)}. The {Na^+} and {Cl^-} are spectator ions — they stay in solution as the salt.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Acids make {H^+} ions in solution; alkalis make {OH^-} ions.
  • pH 0–6 acidic, 7 neutral, 8–14 alkaline. Measure with universal indicator or a pH probe.
  • Neutralisation: {H^+ + OH^- -> H2O}.
