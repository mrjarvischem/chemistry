Walkthrough 6 · Making soluble salts (required practical 1)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.4.2.3 · Combined Science 5.4.2.3 · Required practical 1 · about 25 minutes

Choosing the reactants

A soluble salt can be made by reacting an acid with a solid, insoluble substance: a metal, a metal oxide, a metal hydroxide or a metal carbonate. Choose the acid to give the second part of the name, and the solid to give the first part.

Required practical: making copper sulfate crystals

Copper oxide (black, insoluble) + dilute sulfuric acid → copper sulfate (blue, soluble) + water. Step through the method.

Safety: wear eye protection. Copper sulfate is harmful. Heat the evaporating basin gently and do not heat to dryness — hot solution can spit.

Why is each step done?

Common 6-mark question: "Describe how to make pure, dry crystals of copper sulfate from copper oxide and sulfuric acid." Include: warm the acid · add oxide until in excess · filter · heat to evaporate some water · leave to crystallise · dry the crystals.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Soluble salt = acid + insoluble metal / oxide / hydroxide / carbonate.
  • Add the solid in excess so all the acid reacts.
  • Filter off the excess solid; evaporate some water; leave to crystallise; dry.
