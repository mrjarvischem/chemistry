Lesson 1 · Fields and Newton’s law of gravitation

AQA A-level Physics 3.7.1, 3.7.2.1 – 3.7.2.2 · about 25 minutes

What is a field?

A force field is a region in which a body experiences a non-contact force. Gravitational fields act on mass; electric fields act on charge; magnetic fields act on moving charges and magnets. Fields are represented by field lines.

Gravity and electrostatics are both inverse-square laws — the same maths applies. Gravity is always attractive; electric forces can attract or repel.

Newton’s law of gravitation

F = Gm₁m₂/r²   G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻² · r = distance between the centres of mass
m₁: × 10²⁴ kg
m₂: × 10²² kg
r: × 10⁸ m
Inverse square: double r → force ÷ 4; triple r → force ÷ 9. Both bodies feel the same size force (Newton’s third law).

Gravitational field strength

g = F/m (N kg⁻¹) · in a radial field: g = GM/r². Near Earth’s surface the field is roughly uniform (parallel, equally spaced lines).

Lesson summary
