Lesson 3 · Orbits of planets and satellites

AQA A-level Physics 3.7.2.4 · about 25 minutes

Gravity provides the centripetal force

GMm/r² = mv²/r → v = √(GM/r). With v = 2πr/T: T² = (4π²/GM) r³ — Kepler’s third law, T² ∝ r³.
orbital radius r: km

As r increases: speed falls, period rises, KE falls, GPE rises (less negative), and total energy rises.

Orbital energy and satellite types

Energy in orbit
KE = GMm/2r
GPE = −GMm/r
Total = −GMm/2r
Moving to a higher orbit needs energy input.
Geostationary satellite
T = 24 h, above the equator, moves west → east with Earth. Stays above the same point (communications). r ≈ 4.2 × 10⁷ m.
Low orbit: short period, close to surface — imaging, weather.

Exam practice

G = 6.67 × 10−11 N m² kg−2; ME = 5.97 × 1024 kg.

Lesson summary

  • v = √(GM/r); T² = 4π²r³/GM.
  • Total energy −GMm/2r; higher orbit → slower, longer period, more total energy.
  • Geostationary: 24 h, equatorial, same direction as Earth’s rotation.
