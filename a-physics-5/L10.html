Lesson 10 · Alternating currents and transformers

AQA A-level Physics 3.7.5.5–3.7.5.6 · about 25 minutes

Alternating current and the oscilloscope

Vrms = V₀/√2, Irms = I₀/√2. The rms value is the DC value that gives the same mean power. Mains (UK): 230 V rms, 50 Hz → peak 325 V.
Y-gain: V div⁻¹
time-base: ms div⁻¹

Read the peak and period from the screen (8 × 10 grid). Unknown signal.

Transformers

Ns/Np = Vs/Vp. Ideal: VsIs = VpIp. Efficiency = IsVs / IpVp.

Alternating current in the primary gives a changing flux in the iron core, which links the secondary and induces an alternating emf (Faraday).

Causes of energy loss
  • Eddy currents in the core → laminated core
  • Resistance of windings (I²R) → thick, low-resistance copper
  • Hysteresis → soft iron core
  • Flux leakage → good core design
Transmission
Step up to high V → low I → power loss I²R in cables much smaller. Step down for safe use.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Vrms = V₀/√2; read peak and period from oscilloscope settings.
  • Ns/Np = Vs/Vp; efficiency = IsVs/IpVp.
  • Losses: eddy currents (laminate), I²R (thick wire), hysteresis (soft iron).
  • High-voltage transmission cuts I²R losses.
