Lesson 3 · Changes of state and internal energy

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Particle model of matter 4.3.1.2, 4.3.2.1–4.3.2.2 · about 25 minutes

Changes of state

Heat or cool a substance and it can change state. Use the buttons and watch the particles — and the balance.

mass: 100.0 g (sealed container)
Changes of state are physical changes: the substance keeps its original properties if the change is reversed. Mass is conserved — the number of particles stays the same.

Internal energy

The energy stored inside a system by its particles is its internal energy.

Internal energy = the total kinetic energy and potential energy of all the particles in the system.
Kinetic: from the particles’ movement (linked to temperature). Potential: from the forces and spacing between particles (linked to state).

Heat 1 kg of ice from −20 °C to steam

Energy supplied:
Heating changes the energy stored in a system by increasing the energy of its particles. This either raises the temperature (kinetic energy up) or changes the state (potential energy up) — not both at once.

Linking back: specific heat capacity

When heating raises the temperature, the rise depends on the mass, the material and the energy supplied. You met this in Energy lesson 5.

ΔE = m c Δθ Given on the equation sheet

c is the specific heat capacity: the energy needed to raise 1 kg by 1 °C.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Melting, freezing, boiling/evaporating, condensing, sublimation. Physical changes; mass is conserved.
  • Internal energy = total kinetic + potential energy of the particles.
  • Heating raises the temperature (KE) or changes the state (PE).
  • ΔE = m c Δθ for temperature changes (equation sheet).
