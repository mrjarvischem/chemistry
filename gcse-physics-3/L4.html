Lesson 4 · Heating curves and specific latent heat

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Particle model of matter 4.3.2.3 · about 25 minutes

Heating and cooling curves

A heater supplies energy at a steady rate to some ice. Watch the temperature over time.

What’s happening

Press Heat.
The flat sections are changes of state. Energy is still being supplied, but the temperature doesn’t change — the energy goes into breaking bonds between particles (increasing their potential energy), not making them move faster.

Specific latent heat

The specific latent heat of a substance is the amount of energy needed to change the state of 1 kg of it with no change in temperature.

Specific latent heat of fusion

Solid ⇄ liquid (melting or freezing).
Water: 334 000 J/kg

Specific latent heat of vaporisation

Liquid ⇄ gas (boiling or condensing).
Water: 2 260 000 J/kg

Vaporisation needs far more energy than fusion because the particles must be pulled completely apart, not just freed from their fixed positions.

Latent heat or heat capacity?

Temperature changes?

Use ΔE = m c Δθ (specific heat capacity).

State changes, temperature constant?

Use E = m L (specific latent heat).

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Flat sections on heating/cooling curves = changes of state; temperature constant.
  • Specific latent heat: energy to change the state of 1 kg with no temperature change.
  • E = m L (equation sheet). Fusion: solid ⇄ liquid. Vaporisation: liquid ⇄ gas.
  • Temperature changing → m c Δθ. State changing → m L.
