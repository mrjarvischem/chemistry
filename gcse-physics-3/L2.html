Lesson 2 · Required practical: measuring density

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Particle model of matter 4.3.1.1 · Required practical · about 25 minutes

Regular solids

For a block with a regular shape, measure its sides with a ruler and its mass with a balance.

Your readings

Volume of a cuboid = length × width × height. Measure in cm to get cm³, and use grams to get density in g/cm³.

Irregular solids: the displacement method

You can’t measure the sides of a stone. Instead, find its volume from the volume of water it pushes out of a eureka (displacement) can.

Method

  1. Measure the object’s mass on a balance.
  2. Fill the eureka can until water just flows out of the spout. Wait until it stops.
  3. Put a measuring cylinder under the spout.
  4. Lower the object in gently on a thread.
  5. Read the volume of water collected = volume of the object.

Liquids

Method

  1. Put an empty measuring cylinder on a balance and press zero (tare).
  2. Pour in some liquid, e.g. 50 cm³ — read the volume from the bottom of the meniscus at eye level.
  3. Read the mass from the balance.
  4. ρ = m ÷ V. Add more liquid and repeat to check.

Example

50 cm³ of salt water has a mass of 56 g.
ρ = 56 ÷ 50 = 1.12 g/cm³

Read the volume at eye level, from the bottom of the curved meniscus, to avoid parallax error.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Regular solid: measure sides → V = l × w × h; mass on a balance.
  • Irregular solid: volume = water displaced from a eureka can into a measuring cylinder.
  • Liquid: tare the balance with the empty cylinder, add a measured volume, read the mass.
  • ρ = m ÷ V. Read the meniscus at eye level.
