Lesson 6 · Pressure and volume of a gas Physics only

AQA GCSE Physics (8463 only) · Particle model of matter 4.3.3.2–4.3.3.3 · about 25 minutes

Gas pressure pushes at right angles

Particles hit the walls from every direction, but when they bounce off, the net force from the gas on any surface acts at right angles (perpendicular) to that surface. That is why a balloon inflates evenly in all directions.

Changing the volume at constant temperature

Move the piston to change the volume. The temperature is kept constant. Watch the collisions and record readings.

Volume: cm³
At constant temperature, decreasing the volume increases the pressure. The particles have less space, so they hit the walls more often — even though they are not moving any faster.

The equation

p V = constant Given on the equation sheet

For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature. p = pressure in pascals (Pa), V = volume in m³. Halve the volume → double the pressure.

Most questions are “before and after”: p₁ V₁ = p₂ V₂. Any volume unit works as long as you use the same one on both sides.

Pumping up a tyre Higher tier

Use a bicycle pump quickly and the end of the pump gets warm. Why?

  1. You push the piston in: a force moves through a distance, so you do work on the gas.
  2. Work done is an energy transfer — it goes into the gas and increases its internal energy.
  3. The particles move faster: the temperature of the gas increases.
Doing work on a gas (e.g. compressing it quickly) transfers energy to it, increasing its internal energy and so its temperature.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Gas pressure gives a net force at right angles to any surface.
  • Constant temperature: smaller volume → more frequent collisions → higher pressure.
  • p V = constant (equation sheet) for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature.
  • HT: doing work on a gas increases its internal energy and temperature.
