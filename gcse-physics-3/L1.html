Lesson 1 · States of matter and density

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Particle model of matter 4.3.1.1 · about 25 minutes

The particle model

Everything is made of tiny particles. The particle model uses simple spheres to explain how solids, liquids and gases behave. Switch between the three states.

Density

Density is the mass of each unit of volume — how much "stuff" is packed into a space.

ρ = m ÷ V Learn this — not on the equation sheet
SymbolQuantityUnit
ρ (rho)densitykilograms per metre cubed, kg/m³
mmasskilograms, kg
Vvolumemetres cubed, m³

In the lab you will often use g/cm³. To convert: 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Water is 1.0 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.

Volume of the block: cm³
Density is a property of the material, not the size of the object. A bigger block has more mass and more volume, so its density is the same. Objects less dense than water float.

Why are gases so much less dense?

Same mass, different volume

1 kg of liquid water fills about 1 litre. Boil it to steam and the same particles (the same mass) spread out to fill about 1700 litres. Same mass, far bigger volume → far lower density.

Typical densities

Substancekg/m³
Lead (solid)11 300
Aluminium (solid)2 700
Water (liquid)1 000
Air (gas)1.2
In solids and liquids the particles are close together → high density. In gases the particles are far apart → low density. Solids are usually a little denser than their liquids (water is an exception — ice floats).

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Solid: regular, touching, vibrate. Liquid: touching, random, move past each other. Gas: far apart, random, fast.
  • ρ = m ÷ V (learn it). kg/m³ or g/cm³; 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.
  • Density depends on the material, not the size of the object.
  • Gases have low density because the particles are far apart.
