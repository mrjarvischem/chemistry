Lesson 5 · Particle motion in gases: pressure and temperature

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Particle model of matter 4.3.3.1 · about 25 minutes

Gas particles and temperature

The particles in a gas are in constant random motion. Heat the gas and watch them.

Temperature: °C

Speed and energy

The temperature of a gas is related to the average kinetic energy of its particles. Higher temperature → particles move faster on average → higher average kinetic energy.

Where does gas pressure come from?

Every time a particle hits a wall it pushes on it. Billions of collisions every second add up to a steady force on the walls — the gas pressure. This container is sealed and rigid: its volume is fixed.

Temperature: °C

Pressure gauge

At constant volume, increasing the temperature of a gas increases its pressure: the particles move faster, so they hit the walls more often and harder.

Writing the explanation

Exam questions on this nearly always want the same chain of ideas. Put the steps in order.

🚗 Tyres on a hot day

The air in the tyre gets hotter, so its pressure rises. Drivers check tyre pressures when the tyres are cold.

🧴 Aerosol cans

“Do not expose to temperatures above 50 °C” — heating the sealed can raises the pressure until it could burst.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Gas particles are in constant random motion.
  • Temperature is related to the average kinetic energy of the particles.
  • Pressure is caused by particles colliding with the walls of the container.
  • Constant volume: higher temperature → faster particles → more frequent, harder collisions → higher pressure.
