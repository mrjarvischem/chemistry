Collision theory

Chemical reactions can only happen when reacting particles collide with sufficient energy. The minimum energy needed is the activation energy. A collision with at least this energy is a successful collision.

The rate of a reaction depends on: (1) the frequency of collisions (how many per second) and (2) the energy of the collisions (what fraction have at least the activation energy).
Write “more frequent collisions” or “more collisions per second”, not just “more collisions”. Over the whole reaction the total number of collisions isn't the point — it is how often they happen.

Catalysts

  • A catalyst increases the rate of a reaction but is not used up.
  • It provides a different pathway with a lower activation energy, so more collisions are successful.
  • Catalysts are not included in the overall equation.
  • Enzymes are biological catalysts.
  • Different reactions need different catalysts.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Concentration / pressure ↑ → more particles in the same volume → more frequent collisions.
  • Surface area ↑ (smaller pieces) → more particles exposed → more frequent collisions.
  • Temperature ↑ → particles move faster → more frequent collisions and more collisions with energy ≥ activation energy.
  • Catalyst → alternative pathway with lower activation energy → more successful collisions; not used up.
