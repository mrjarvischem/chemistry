Walkthrough 1 · Calculating rates of reaction

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.6.1.1 · Combined Science 5.6.1.1 · about 25 minutes

What is the rate of a reaction?

The rate of a reaction is how quickly a reactant is used up or a product is formed. Watch magnesium reacting with hydrochloric acid. The hydrogen is collected in a gas syringe.

Readings every 10 s

Mean rate of reaction = quantity of reactant used ÷ time taken
or mean rate = quantity of product formed ÷ time taken

Calculating a mean rate

Gas made

volume in cm³ ÷ time in s → cm³/s

Mass lost

mass in g ÷ time in s → g/s

Moles HT

amount in mol ÷ time in s → mol/s
Check the time unit. If the time is in minutes, the rate is per minute (e.g. cm³/min). To get per second, change minutes to seconds first (× 60).

Rate in mol/s HT

Higher tier students may need to give a rate in mol/s. Convert the mass to moles first: moles = mass ÷ Mr, then divide by the time.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Mean rate = quantity of reactant used ÷ time, or quantity of product formed ÷ time.
  • Units: g/s, cm³/s, (HT) mol/s. Watch for minutes vs seconds.
  • (HT) mol/s: convert mass to moles (mass ÷ Mr) before dividing by time.
