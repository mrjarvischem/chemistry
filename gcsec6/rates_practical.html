Walkthrough 5 · Rates of reaction (required practical 5)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.6.1.2 · Required practical 5 (Combined Science RP 11) · about 35 minutes

Method 1: measuring the volume of gas

Investigate how the concentration of hydrochloric acid affects the rate of its reaction with magnesium ribbon. Collect the hydrogen in a gas syringe (or an upturned measuring cylinder in water) and record the volume every 10 s.

Higher concentration → steeper line at the start (faster rate). The same length of magnesium (limiting) gives the same final volume of hydrogen.

Method 2: the disappearing cross

Sodium thiosulfate reacts with hydrochloric acid to make a sulfur precipitate, so the solution goes cloudy. Time how long it takes for the cross under the flask to disappear. Press Stop when you can no longer see the cross.

Rate is proportional to 1 / time. The faster the reaction, the shorter the time.

Total volume is kept the same (thiosulfate + water) so only the concentration changes. The same person should judge when the cross disappears, viewed from the same height.

Summary

  • Gas method: volume every 10 s → graph; higher concentration → steeper.
  • Cloudiness method: time for the cross to disappear; rate ∝ 1/time.
  • Independent: concentration. Dependent: volume of gas / time. Control: temperature, volume, length/mass of Mg, same cross.
