Walkthrough 7 · Changing the position of equilibrium (HT)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.6.2.4–4.6.2.7 · Combined Science 5.6.2.4–5.6.2.7 · Higher tier only · about 40 minutes

Le Chatelier's principle HT

If a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change in conditions, the position of equilibrium shifts to oppose (counteract) the change.

The method — use it every time

  1. What has changed? e.g. the temperature increases.
  2. Oppose it: the equilibrium shifts to decrease the temperature.
  3. Which direction does that? It moves in the endothermic direction.
  4. So what? Left or right? What happens to the yield?
“If T increases, the equilibrium shifts to decrease T to oppose the change, so it moves in the endothermic direction.”
{N2O4(g)} (colourless) ⇌ {2NO2(g)} (brown)   forward reaction is endothermic

Changing the temperature

Temperature increases

The equilibrium shifts to decrease the temperature, to oppose the change → moves in the endothermic direction.

Temperature decreases

The equilibrium shifts to increase the temperature, to oppose the change → moves in the exothermic direction.
The question tells you whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic. The reverse reaction is always the opposite. If the forward reaction is exothermic, the endothermic direction is to the left.

Changing the pressure (gases)

Pressure increases

The equilibrium shifts to decrease the pressure, to oppose the change → moves to the side with fewer molecules of gas.

Pressure decreases

The equilibrium shifts to increase the pressure, to oppose the change → moves to the side with more molecules of gas.
Count the molecules of gas using the coefficients (big numbers): {N2 + 3H2} → 1 + 3 = 4; {2NH3} → 2. If both sides have the same number, pressure has no effect on the position of equilibrium.

Changing the concentration

Add more of a reactant

The equilibrium shifts to decrease its concentration, to oppose the change → more products form (moves right) until equilibrium is reached again.

Add more of a product / remove a product

Adding product: shifts to decrease it → moves left. Removing product: shifts to replace it → moves right.

Mixed practice

Any reaction, any change — including catalysts. Keep going until you can get five in a row fully correct.

A catalyst does not change the position of equilibrium. It speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached faster.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • The equilibrium shifts to oppose the change.
  • T increases → shifts to decrease T → endothermic direction. T decreases → shifts to increase T → exothermic direction.
  • P increases → shifts to decrease P → side with fewer gas molecules. P decreases → more gas molecules. Equal numbers → no effect.
  • Add a reactant → shifts to use it up → right. Remove a product → shifts to replace it → right.
  • Catalyst: no change to position; equilibrium reached faster.
