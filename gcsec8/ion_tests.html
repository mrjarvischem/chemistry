Walkthrough 5 · Tests for ions (chemistry only)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.8.3.2–4.8.3.5 · Chemistry only — not Combined Science · about 35 minutes

Testing for metal ions with sodium hydroxide CHEMISTRY ONLY

Add a few drops of sodium hydroxide solution. Some metal ions form insoluble hydroxides — coloured or white precipitates. Pick a solution, add a few drops, then add NaOH in excess.

IonPrecipitate with NaOH
{Al^3+}white — dissolves in excess NaOH
{Ca^2+}white — stays in excess
{Mg^2+}white — stays in excess
{Cu^2+}blue
{Fe^2+}green
{Fe^3+}brown
Aluminium, calcium and magnesium all give white precipitates. Only aluminium hydroxide dissolves in excess NaOH. To tell calcium from magnesium, use a flame test (calcium: orange-red; magnesium: no colour).

Equations for the precipitates

{CuSO4(aq) + 2NaOH(aq) -> Cu(OH)2(s) + Na2SO4(aq)}

The metal hydroxide is the precipitate (s). The number of {OH^-} in the hydroxide equals the charge on the metal ion: {Fe(OH)2}, {Fe(OH)3}, {Al(OH)3}.

HT Ionic equation: {Cu^2+(aq) + 2OH^-(aq) -> Cu(OH)2(s)}

Testing for carbonates, halides and sulfates

IonTestResult
carbonate {CO3^2-}add dilute acid; bubble gas through limewaterfizzes; limewater turns milky
chloride {Cl^-}add dilute nitric acid, then silver nitrate solutionwhite precipitate
bromide {Br^-}cream precipitate
iodide {I^-}yellow precipitate
sulfate {SO4^2-}add dilute hydrochloric acid, then barium chloride solutionwhite precipitate
The acid is added first to remove carbonate ions, which would also form a precipitate. Use nitric acid for halides (hydrochloric acid would add {Cl^-} ions!) and hydrochloric acid for sulfates (sulfuric acid would add {SO4^2-} ions!).

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • NaOH: Al³⁺/Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ white (only Al(OH)₃ dissolves in excess); Cu²⁺ blue; Fe²⁺ green; Fe³⁺ brown.
  • Carbonates: acid → CO₂ → limewater milky.
  • Halides: nitric acid + silver nitrate → Cl⁻ white, Br⁻ cream, I⁻ yellow.
  • Sulfates: hydrochloric acid + barium chloride → white.
