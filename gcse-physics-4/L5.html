Lesson 5 · Half-life and the random nature of decay

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Atomic structure 4.4.2.4 · about 25 minutes

Random, but predictable

Each of these 400 nuclei has the same chance of decaying in each time step. You can’t predict which will decay — but you can predict how many.

The half-life of a radioactive isotope is the time it takes for the number of nuclei of the isotope in a sample to halve — or the time it takes for the count rate (or activity) from a sample to fall to half its initial level.

Half-life calculations

Halving step by step

A sample has 1600 Bq and a half-life of 3 days. After 9 days:
1600 → (3 days) 800 → (6 days) 400 → (9 days) 200 Bq. That is 3 half-lives.

Net decline as a ratio Higher tier

After n half-lives, the fraction left is (½)ⁿ. After 3 half-lives: (½)³ = 1/8 remains, so the net decline is 7/8 of the original.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Decay is random: you can’t predict which nucleus decays next, but large numbers follow a pattern.
  • Half-life: time for the number of unstable nuclei (or count rate) to halve.
  • Find it on a graph: start value → half → across → down. Check with another halving.
  • After n half-lives, (½)ⁿ remains. HT: net decline = 1 − (½)ⁿ.
