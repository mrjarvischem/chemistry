Lesson 2 · Developing the model of the atom

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Atomic structure 4.4.1.3 · about 25 minutes

How the model of the atom changed

Scientific models change when new experimental evidence doesn’t fit the old model. Step through the history.

The alpha particle scattering experiment

Alpha particles (positive) were fired at very thin gold foil. Choose a model and fire. Which model matches what Geiger and Marsden actually saw?

Results

What they saw — and what it means:
• Most alpha particles went straight through → the atom is mostly empty space.
• Some were deflected → the centre of the atom is positively charged.
• A very few bounced back → the positive charge and most of the mass are concentrated in a tiny nucleus.

Linking evidence to the model

Niels Bohr adapted the nuclear model: electrons orbit the nucleus at specific distances. His calculations agreed with experimental observations. Later experiments showed the nucleus contains smaller particles — protons — and about 20 years after the nucleus was accepted, James Chadwick provided evidence for the neutron.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Dalton: tiny spheres. Thomson: electron discovered → plum pudding model.
  • Alpha scattering: most through (empty space), some deflected (positive centre), few back (tiny, massive nucleus).
  • Nuclear model replaced plum pudding. Bohr: electrons at specific distances. Chadwick: neutrons.
  • Models change when new evidence doesn’t fit.
