Lesson 6 · Radioactive contamination and irradiation

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Atomic structure 4.4.2.5 · about 25 minutes

Irradiation vs contamination

☀ Irradiation

Exposing an object to nuclear radiation from outside. The object does not become radioactive. Once the source is removed or shielded, the exposure stops.
e.g. surgical tools or food sterilised with gamma rays.

☢ Contamination

The unwanted presence of radioactive atoms on or in other materials. The hazard comes from the decay of those atoms — and it keeps going until they are removed or have decayed.
e.g. radioactive dust on clothes, or breathing in radon gas.

Which radiation is most dangerous?

It depends on where the source is. Choose the radiation and where it is.

Outside the body: alpha is least dangerous (stopped by skin and air); beta and gamma can reach organs.
Inside the body: alpha is most dangerous — it is absorbed by nearby cells and is strongly ionising, so it does lots of damage in a small area.

Protecting people

Research on the effects of radiation must be published and checked by other scientists — peer review — so that the findings can be trusted before they are used to set safety rules.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Irradiation: exposure to radiation; the object doesn’t become radioactive.
  • Contamination: radioactive atoms on or in a material; the hazard continues as they decay.
  • Outside the body, alpha is least dangerous; inside, alpha is most dangerous.
  • Precautions: gloves and tongs, shielding, distance, short exposure times, monitoring badges. Results need peer review.
