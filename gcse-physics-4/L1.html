The nucleus is positively charged and contains almost all of the mass. An atom has no overall charge because it has the same number of electrons as protons.

The radius of an atom is about 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. The radius of the nucleus is less than 1/10 000 of the radius of the atom. If the nucleus were a pea in the centre of a football pitch, the atom would be the size of the stadium.

Electrons and electromagnetic radiation

Electrons are arranged at different distances from the nucleus — different energy levels. They can move between levels by absorbing or emitting electromagnetic radiation.

Remember

  • Absorb radiation → electron moves to a higher level, further from the nucleus.
  • Emit radiation → electron drops to a lower level, closer to the nucleus.
  • If an atom loses an outer electron completely it becomes a positive ion.

Atomic number, mass number and isotopes

Add or remove particles. The number of protons decides which element it is.

Neutrons
Electrons
Atomic number = number of protons. Mass number = protons + neutrons.
Isotopes: atoms of the same element (same number of protons) with different numbers of neutrons.
Ions: atoms that have lost or gained electrons, so they are charged.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Nucleus: protons (+1) and neutrons (0); electrons (−1) in energy levels. Atom radius ~1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m; nucleus < 1/10 000 of that.
  • Absorbing EM radiation moves an electron to a higher level; emitting it moves it lower.
  • Atomic number = protons. Mass number = protons + neutrons. Same element → same number of protons.
  • Isotopes: different numbers of neutrons. Ions: different numbers of electrons from protons.
