Newton’s three laws

First lawAn object stays at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it.
Second lawResultant force = rate of change of momentum. For constant mass: F = ma.
Third lawIf body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts an equal and opposite force on A. The forces are the same type and act on different bodies.

A book resting on a table: its weight and the normal reaction are equal and opposite — but they are not a Newton’s third law pair (they act on the same object and are different types of force). The third-law partner of the book’s weight is the book’s gravitational pull on the Earth.

Drag, lift and terminal speed

Drag (air resistance) increases with speed. A falling object accelerates until drag equals weight, then falls at constant terminal speed.

What affects drag?

Speed, cross-sectional area and shape, and the fluid’s viscosity/density. Lift is the upward force on a wing, perpendicular to the flow of the fluid.

Factors on vehicle top speed

Maximum speed is reached when the driving force equals the total resistive forces (drag + friction). More powerful engines and streamlined shapes raise the top speed.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • N1: no resultant force → constant velocity. N2: F = ma (F = Δ(mv)/Δt). N3: equal, opposite, same type, different bodies.
  • Draw free-body diagrams; resultant force = mass × acceleration.
  • Terminal speed: drag = weight, resultant force zero. Parachute → bigger drag → lower terminal speed.
