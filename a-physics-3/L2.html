The moment of a force

moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force   (N m)

Distance along spanner: m
Angle to spanner: °

The principle of moments

For an object in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments about any point = the sum of anticlockwise moments about that point. (And the resultant force is zero.)
Left mass: kg at m
Right mass: kg at m
Take moments about the point where an unknown force acts — that force then has zero moment, so it drops out of the equation.

Couples and centre of mass

Couple

A pair of equal and opposite forces that do not act along the same line. It causes rotation without translation.
moment of a couple = one force × perpendicular distance between the lines of action of the forces.
e.g. turning a steering wheel or a tap.

Centre of mass

The single point through which the whole weight of an object can be taken to act. For a uniform object it is at the geometric centre. An object topples when its centre of mass moves beyond its base, so the weight’s moment is no longer restoring.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Moment = force × perpendicular distance from pivot (N m).
  • Principle of moments: Σ clockwise = Σ anticlockwise (about any point) in equilibrium.
  • Couple: equal, opposite, non-collinear forces; moment = F × perpendicular separation.
  • Centre of mass: point where the weight acts; uniform object → centre.
