Lesson 3 · Motion along a straight line

AQA A-level Physics 3.4.1.3 · including Required Practical 3 · about 30 minutes

Displacement, velocity and acceleration graphs

velocity = rate of change of displacement, v = Δs/Δt  ·  acceleration = rate of change of velocity, a = Δv/Δt
Gradient of s–t = velocity · gradient of v–t = acceleration · area under v–t = displacement · area under a–t = change in velocity
Instantaneous velocity on a curved s–t graph = gradient of the tangent at that point. Average velocity = total displacement ÷ total time.

Equations for uniform acceleration

v = u + atno s
s = ½(u + v)tno a
s = ut + ½at²no v
v² = u² + 2asno t

Only valid for constant acceleration. Choose a positive direction and stick to it (e.g. up = +, so g = −9.81 m s⁻²).

Required practical 3: determining g by free fall

A steel ball is released from an electromagnet and falls a height h through a light gate (or onto a trapdoor) connected to a timer. From rest, h = ½gt², so a graph of h against t² is a straight line with gradient g/2.

Drop height: m
Repeat each height and average; use at least 5 heights. An electromagnet that releases slowly adds a systematic delay — it shifts the line but doesn’t change the gradient, so g from the gradient is still accurate.

Lesson summary

  • s–t gradient = v; v–t gradient = a; v–t area = displacement; a–t area = Δv.
  • SUVAT (constant a): v = u + at; s = ½(u+v)t; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as.
  • RP3: h = ½gt², gradient of h–t² graph = g/2.
