Lesson 8 · Bulk properties of solids

AQA A-level Physics 3.4.2.1 · about 25 minutes

Density and Hooke’s law

density ρ = m/V (kg m⁻³) · Hooke’s law: F = kΔL, up to the limit of proportionality (k = stiffness, N m⁻¹)
Load: N

Key points on the graph

Limit of proportionality: beyond it, F is no longer proportional to ΔL.
Elastic limit: beyond it, the material is permanently deformed — it won’t return to its original length.
Elastic deformation: returns to original shape. Plastic: permanently stretched.

Springs combined

Series: 1/k = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂ (less stiff).
Parallel: k = k₁ + k₂ (stiffer).

Stress, strain and elastic strain energy

tensile stress = F/A (Pa) · tensile strain = ΔL/L (no unit) · breaking stress = the stress at which it breaks
elastic strain energy E = ½FΔL = area under the F–ΔL graph (for the elastic region)

Energy stored = area under the force–extension graph

Loading and unloading

If a wire is stretched beyond its elastic limit, the unloading line is parallel to the loading line but doesn’t return to zero. The area between the lines is energy dissipated (work done to permanently deform it); the area under the unloading line is the elastic energy recovered.

Energy in vehicle crumple zones: work is done plastically deforming the metal, so less kinetic energy reaches the passengers. Spring energy can be transferred to kinetic energy: ½kΔL² = ½mv².

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • ρ = m/V. Hooke’s law F = kΔL up to the limit of proportionality.
  • Elastic vs plastic deformation; elastic limit.
  • Stress = F/A; strain = ΔL/L; breaking stress.
  • Elastic strain energy = ½FΔL = area under F–ΔL graph (= ½kΔL²).
  • Ductile, brittle and polymeric materials behave differently.
