The Young modulus

Young modulus E = tensile stress / tensile strain = FL / (AΔL)   unit: Pa · it is the gradient of the linear part of a stress–strain graph

E measures the stiffness of a material (not of one particular wire): steel ≈ 2.0 × 10¹¹ Pa, copper ≈ 1.2 × 10¹¹ Pa, rubber ≈ 10⁷ Pa.

Required practical 4: Young modulus of a wire

A long, thin copper wire is clamped at one end and passes over a pulley; masses are added to the other end. A marker on the wire moves along a fixed ruler as it stretches.

Added mass: kg
Length L: 2.50 m
Diameter (micrometer): 0.38 mm
Use a long, thin wire so the extension is large enough to measure. Measure the diameter in several places with a micrometer and average (A = πd²/4 — the diameter has the biggest % uncertainty because it is squared). Wear safety goggles in case the wire snaps, and keep feet clear of the masses. Plotting stress against strain, E = gradient of the linear section.
