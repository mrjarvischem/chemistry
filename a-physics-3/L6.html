Lesson 6 · Momentum and impulse

AQA A-level Physics 3.4.1.6 · about 25 minutes

Conservation of momentum

momentum p = mv (kg m s⁻¹, a vector) · In a closed system (no external forces), total momentum before = total momentum after.
Mass A: kg
Velocity A: m s⁻¹
Mass B (at rest): kg
Elastic collision: total kinetic energy is conserved. Inelastic: some KE is transferred to other forms (heat, sound, deformation). Momentum is conserved in both.

Force, impulse and force–time graphs

F = Δ(mv)/Δt  ·  impulse = FΔt = Δ(mv)  ·  impulse = area under a force–time graph (N s)
Contact time: ms

A 0.50 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m s⁻¹ and stops. The change in momentum is fixed (5.0 N s); spreading it over a longer time reduces the peak force.

Safety features — crumple zones, airbags, seat belts, cycle helmets, crash mats — all work the same way: they increase the time over which the momentum changes, so the force is smaller.

Explosions and recoil

Before an explosion the total momentum is zero, so afterwards the pieces have equal and opposite momenta: m1v1 = −m2v2. Kinetic energy is not conserved — it increases, coming from chemical (or elastic) energy.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • p = mv; momentum is conserved in a closed system.
  • Elastic: KE conserved. Inelastic: KE not conserved. Momentum always conserved.
  • F = Δ(mv)/Δt; impulse FΔt = Δ(mv) = area under F–t graph.
  • Longer impact time → smaller force (safety features). Explosions: total momentum stays zero.
