Walkthrough 3 · Reaction profiles and activation energy

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.5.1.2 · Combined Science 5.5.1.2 · about 25 minutes

Activation energy

Chemical reactions only happen when particles collide with enough energy. The minimum amount of energy that particles must have to react is the activation energy. Drag the slider to change the energy of a collision.

Activation energy = the minimum energy that particles must have to react. On a profile it is measured from the reactants up to the top of the peak.

Build a reaction profile

A reaction profile shows the energy of the reactants and products, and the energy needed to start the reaction.

Arrows have a direction. Activation energy: arrow points up from the reactants to the top of the peak. Overall energy change: arrow points from the reactants to the products — down for exothermic, up for endothermic.

Reading an exam profile

Reading values from the energy scale

Some exam questions give a scale on the energy axis. Read the energy of the reactants, the top of the peak and the products, then subtract. Each small square is 10 kJ/mol.

Activation energy = energy at the peak − energy of the reactants.
Energy released (exothermic) = reactants − products. Energy taken in (endothermic) = products − reactants.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Particles must collide with at least the activation energy to react.
  • Exothermic profile: products lower than reactants. Endothermic: products higher.
  • Activation energy: arrow up from reactants to the peak. Overall energy change: arrow from reactants to products.
  • With a scale: activation energy = peak − reactants; energy released = reactants − products.
