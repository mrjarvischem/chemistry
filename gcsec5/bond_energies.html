Walkthrough 4 · Calculating energy changes from bond energies (HT)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.5.1.3 · Combined Science 5.5.1.3 · Higher tier only · about 40 minutes

Breaking and making bonds HT

In a reaction, bonds in the reactants are broken and new bonds in the products are made. Step through hydrogen reacting with chlorine.

Breaking bonds takes in energy — it is endothermic.
Making bonds gives out energy — it is exothermic.
Exam questions often say: “The reaction releases 185 kJ/mol more energy than it absorbs.” That means energy out − energy in = 185 kJ/mol, so the reaction is exothermic.

Counting bonds — watch the coefficients

A coefficient (big number) in front of a displayed structure means there are that many whole molecules. Every bond in the structure must be counted that many times. Press Show the copies, then tap every bond to count it.

In {CH4 + 2O2}, the 2 is in front of {O2} only: there are 2 O=O bonds, but still only 4 C–H bonds. In {2H2O} there are 4 O–H bonds (2 per molecule × 2 molecules).

Calculating the energy change

If energy out > energy in: the reaction releases (out − in) kJ/mol more energy than it absorbs → exothermic.
If energy in > energy out: the reaction absorbs (in − out) kJ/mol more energy than it releases → endothermic.
Overall energy change = energy in − energy out (negative for exothermic, positive for endothermic).

Finding a missing bond energy

Now the energy change is given and one bond energy is missing. Work out the total you can calculate first, then use the energy change to find the other total.

“Releases 185 kJ more than it absorbs” → energy out = energy in + 185.
“Absorbs 185 kJ more than it releases” → energy in = energy out + 185.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Breaking bonds is endothermic (energy in); making bonds is exothermic (energy out).
  • Multiply each structure's bonds by its coefficient (big number).
  • Out > in → releases (out − in) more than it absorbs → exothermic. In > out → absorbs (in − out) more → endothermic.
  • Missing bond energy: find the total you can, use the given difference to get the other total, then subtract the known bonds and divide by the number of the unknown bond.
