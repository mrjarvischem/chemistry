AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.5.2.1 · Chemistry only — not Combined Science · about 25 minutes

Making a simple cell CHEMISTRY ONLY

A cell contains chemicals that react to produce electricity. A simple cell: two different metals dipped in an electrolyte, connected by wires. Choose the metals and electrolyte.

Electrolyte
The voltage depends on the type of electrode and the electrolyte. The bigger the difference in reactivity of the two metals, the bigger the voltage. The more reactive metal is the negative terminal: its atoms lose electrons, and the electrons flow through the wire away from the more reactive metal, towards the less reactive metal.

Batteries: cells in series

A battery is two or more cells connected together in series to give a bigger voltage.

Non-rechargeable and rechargeable

Non-rechargeable (e.g. alkaline batteries)

The chemical reactions stop when one of the reactants has been used up. The battery is then “flat” and must be replaced.

Rechargeable

The chemical reactions can be reversed when an external electrical current is supplied (the charger), so the reactants are made again.

Summary

  • Simple cell: two different metals in an electrolyte.
  • Voltage depends on the electrodes and electrolyte; bigger reactivity difference → bigger voltage.
  • Battery = cells in series. Non-rechargeable: reactants used up. Rechargeable: reactions reversed by an external current.
