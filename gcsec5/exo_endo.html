Walkthrough 1 · Exothermic and endothermic reactions

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.5.1.1 · Combined Science 5.5.1.1 · about 25 minutes

Energy is conserved

Energy is never made or destroyed in a reaction — it is transferred between the chemicals and the surroundings (the solution, the beaker, the air). Mix the chemicals and watch the thermometer.

Exothermic

Energy is transferred to the surroundings. The temperature of the surroundings increases.

Endothermic

Energy is taken in from the surroundings. The temperature of the surroundings decreases.
“The reaction gets hot” — the surroundings (the solution and thermometer) get hotter because energy was transferred out of the chemicals. Don't say the reaction "takes in heat" for an exothermic reaction.

Examples to learn

Exothermic

  • combustion (burning fuels)
  • many oxidation reactions
  • neutralisation (acid + alkali)
  • metals + acids
  • respiration

Endothermic

  • thermal decomposition (e.g. heating calcium carbonate)
  • citric acid + sodium hydrogencarbonate
  • photosynthesis
  • dissolving some salts, e.g. ammonium nitrate

Everyday uses

Hand warmers

Exothermic. Iron powder oxidises (reacts with oxygen) and releases energy. Some reusable ones use crystallisation of a salt solution.

Self-heating cans

Exothermic. Calcium oxide reacts with water in a separate compartment, heating the food or drink.

Sports injury packs

Endothermic. Squeezing breaks an inner bag; a salt such as ammonium nitrate dissolves in water and takes in energy, so the pack gets cold.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Energy is conserved; it is transferred between the chemicals and the surroundings.
  • Exothermic: energy out to surroundings; temperature rises (combustion, oxidation, neutralisation).
  • Endothermic: energy in from surroundings; temperature falls (thermal decomposition, citric acid + sodium hydrogencarbonate).
  • Uses: hand warmers and self-heating cans (exo); sports injury packs (endo).
