Four interactions and exchange particles

There are four fundamental interactions. Each one acts by particles exchanging something — an exchange particle (sometimes called a gauge boson). Exchange particles are “virtual”: they exist only briefly and can’t be detected directly.

The ball-throwing model (PS 1.2)

Throwing a heavy ball transfers momentum: thrower and catcher both recoil apart — a repulsive force. For attraction, picture them passing the ball backwards (like a boomerang) so each is pushed towards the other. Models like this help, but no everyday model fully explains attraction.

Feynman diagrams and the electromagnetic force

AQA calls these “simple diagrams”: they show the incoming and outgoing particles and the exchange particle.

Two electrons repel

Rules for drawing them

  • Particles are straight lines; exchange particles are wavy lines.
  • Time runs upwards: incoming particles at the bottom, outgoing at the top.
  • Lines meet at vertices. At every vertex, charge, baryon number and lepton number must be conserved.
  • Don’t let particle lines cross the exchange particle; the exchange particle joins the two vertices.
  • Label every line — marks are often lost for missing labels or the wrong W charge.

The weak interaction: β− and β+ decay

The weak interaction is the only interaction that can change one type of particle into another (a neutron into a proton, or vice versa). Its exchange particles, W+ and W−, are charged and very massive — which is why the weak interaction has such a short range.

β−: W− (n → p, so something negative must leave). β+: W+ (p → n, so something positive must leave). Check the charge at the first vertex and the W chooses itself.
β+ decay only happens to protons inside proton-rich nuclei — a free proton is stable because a neutron has more mass than a proton, so energy has to come from the nucleus.

Lesson summary

  • Forces are caused by exchange particles: EM → virtual photon; weak → W±; strong (between nucleons) → pion.
  • β−: n → p + W−, W− → e− + ν̄e. β+: p → n + W+, W+ → e+ + νe.
  • Electron capture: p + e− → n + νe via W+. Electron–proton collision: same reaction via W−.
  • Check charge at every vertex.
