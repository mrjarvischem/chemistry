Lesson 3 · Particles, antiparticles and photons

AQA A-level Physics 3.2.1.3 · about 25 minutes

Antiparticles

For every type of particle there is a corresponding antiparticle. A particle and its antiparticle have the same mass and rest energy but opposite charge (and opposite baryon and lepton numbers — Lesson 5).

Compare a pair

Rest energy

Mass is a form of energy. Particle physicists quote the rest energy (the energy equivalent of the mass when at rest) in MeV.

1 eV = 1.60 × 10−19 J
1 MeV = 106 eV = 1.60 × 10−13 J

Rest energies are given in the data booklet. You will not be asked to use E = mc2 in calculations.

The photon model of light

Electromagnetic radiation is emitted and absorbed in discrete packets of energy called photons. The energy of one photon depends only on its frequency.

E = hf = hcλ    h = Planck constant = 6.63 × 10−34 J s

Photon explorer

Higher frequency → shorter wavelength → more energy per photon. A gamma photon carries about a million times more energy than a photon of visible light.

Annihilation

When a particle meets its antiparticle they annihilate: both are destroyed and their mass is converted into energy, carried away as two photons.

Why two photons? If the particle and antiparticle are moving slowly, their total momentum is about zero. One photon would carry momentum away, so two photons are produced moving in opposite directions — total momentum stays zero.
For “slow” particles, ignore their kinetic energy: each photon gets one rest energy. Minimum photon energy = E0 (rest energy of one particle).

Application: the PET scanner

A patient is given a tracer that emits positrons (β+). Each positron annihilates with an electron in the body. The two gamma photons travel in opposite directions and hit a ring of detectors at the same time. Each event gives a line; the source is where the lines cross.

Pair production

The reverse of annihilation: a photon with enough energy can turn into a particle–antiparticle pair. It usually happens near a nucleus, which recoils to conserve momentum.

Extra energy

Photon energy: MeV
Minimum photon energy for pair production = 2 × rest energy of the particle. Any extra energy becomes the kinetic energy of the pair.

Exam practice

Data: h = 6.63 × 10−34 J s, c = 3.00 × 108 m s−1, 1 MeV = 1.60 × 10−13 J. Rest energies: electron 0.510999 MeV, proton 938.257 MeV.

Lesson summary

  • Antiparticles: same mass/rest energy, opposite charge. e⁺, p̄, n̄, ν̄ are the antiparticles of e⁻, p, n, ν.
  • Photon energy E = hf = hc/λ.
  • Annihilation: particle + antiparticle → 2 photons (opposite directions). Min energy of each photon = E0.
  • Pair production: photon → particle + antiparticle. Minimum photon energy = 2E0.
  • 1 MeV = 1.60 × 10−13 J.
