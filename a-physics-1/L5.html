Hadrons and leptons

Particles are sorted by the interactions they feel. Hadrons feel the strong interaction (and are made of quarks — Lesson 6). Leptons do not feel the strong interaction; they are fundamental. Tap any particle.

Baryon number and lepton number

Quantum numbers are “labels” that are conserved in interactions — they explain why some reactions happen and others never do.

Baryon number B

particleB
baryons (p, n)+1
antibaryons (p̄, n̄)−1
mesons, leptons, photons0
Baryon number is always conserved.

Lepton numbers Le and Lμ

particleLeLμ
e−, νe+10

μ−, νμ0+1

Le and Lμ are each conserved separately.
A muon number can’t be “paid” with an electron number. μ− → e− + γ conserves charge but breaks both Lμ (1 → 0) and Le (0 → 1), so it never happens.

Strange particles

Kaons (and some baryons) were called strange because they are made quickly by the strong interaction but decay slowly by the weak interaction. They are always created in pairs. To explain this, a new quantum number was introduced: strangeness, S.

Associated production

The rules

K+, K0: S = +1
K−, K̄0: S = −1
p, n, π, leptons: S = 0
Strong interaction: strangeness is conserved.
Weak interaction: strangeness can change by 0, +1 or −1.
“Strange particles are created in pairs” is the reason S was invented: starting from S = 0, a strong interaction must make one +1 and one −1.

Why do we need huge international teams?

Accelerators like the LHC at CERN cost billions, need thousands of scientists and engineers with different expertise, and generate enormous amounts of data. Results must be checked independently (e.g. by two separate detector teams) before new knowledge is accepted. This collaboration is how discoveries like the Higgs boson (2012) are validated.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Hadrons (baryons + mesons) feel the strong interaction; leptons don’t.
  • B: baryons +1, antibaryons −1, others 0 — always conserved.
  • Le and Lμ conserved separately.
  • Proton = only stable baryon. Pion = strong-force exchange particle. Kaon → pions. Muon → electron.
  • Strange particles: made in pairs (strong), decay by weak. S conserved in strong; ΔS = 0, ±1 in weak.
