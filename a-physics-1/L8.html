The electron volt

Energies of electrons and photons are tiny in joules, so we often use the electron volt (eV).

1 eV is the energy gained by an electron when it is accelerated through a potential difference of 1 V.
1 eV = 1.60 × 10−19 J    (eV → J: × 1.60×10−19  ·  J → eV: ÷ 1.60×10−19)

Excitation and ionisation

Electrons in atoms can only have certain discrete energies — the energy levels. The lowest is the ground state. Energies are negative: 0 eV is the energy of a free electron (ionisation).

Energy: eV

Excitation

An atomic electron moves up to a higher energy level. Needs exactly the energy difference ΔE. A colliding electron can give part of its KE (it carries on with the rest). A photon must have exactly ΔE to be absorbed.

Ionisation

An electron is removed completely from the atom, leaving an ion. Ionisation energy = the energy needed to remove an electron from the ground state (13.6 eV for hydrogen).

De-excitation and line spectra

An excited electron soon falls back to a lower level, emitting a photon. The photon’s energy is exactly the difference between the levels:

hf = E1 − E2    (E1 = higher level, E2 = lower level)

Hydrogen emission spectrum (visible part)

Because energy levels are discrete, only certain photon energies — so only certain wavelengths — can be emitted. That is why a gas gives a line spectrum, and why line spectra are evidence for discrete energy levels. (An absorption spectrum shows dark lines at the same wavelengths.) In the lab, line spectra are viewed using a diffraction grating (AT j).

The fluorescent tube

This is a classic 4–6 mark question. Use the words accelerated, collide, excite, de-excite, UV photons, coating (phosphor), absorbs, visible photons — in that order.

Lesson summary

  • 1 eV = 1.60 × 10−19 J — the energy gained by an electron accelerated through 1 V.
  • Excitation: electron moves up a level. Ionisation: electron removed. Colliding electrons can give part of their KE; photons must match ΔE exactly.
  • De-excitation: hf = E1 − E2. Discrete levels → line spectra.
  • Fluorescent tube: electrons excite Hg → UV photons → coating absorbs UV → coating de-excites in smaller steps → visible light.
