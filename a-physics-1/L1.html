Inside the atom

An atom has a tiny, dense nucleus of protons and neutrons (together called nucleons), surrounded by electrons. The atom is about 10−10 m across; the nucleus is about 10−15 m (1 fm), so it is roughly 100 000 times smaller.

Atom builder

The number of protons decides which element it is. Changing the neutrons makes a different isotope; changing the electrons makes an ion.

Reading nuclide notation

neutron number N = A − Z

Isotope explorer

Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same proton number) with different numbers of neutrons (different nucleon numbers). They have the same number of electrons, so the same chemical properties — but different masses and often different nuclear stability.

Using isotopic data

1. Relative atomic mass. Chlorine is a mixture of 35Cl and 37Cl. Slide to change the abundance of chlorine-35 and see the mean mass.

2. Radioactive dating. Living things keep a constant ratio of radioactive carbon-14 to carbon-12. After death, the C-14 decays (half-life 5730 years) and is not replaced. Measuring the C-14 : C-12 ratio tells us how long ago the organism died.

Specific charge

The specific charge of a particle is its charge divided by its mass.

specific charge = charge ÷ mass    unit: C kg−1

Specific charge calculator

Compare (log scale — each gridline is ×10)

For a nucleus the charge is Z × e, not just e. For an ion, the charge is the net charge (extra or missing electrons × e), and the mass includes the electrons — though their mass makes very little difference.
The electron has by far the largest specific charge (about 1800 × the proton’s) because its mass is so small. A neutron’s specific charge is zero.

Lesson summary

  • Proton +1, neutron 0, electron −1 (relative charge); masses 1, 1, 0.0005 (relative).
  • AZX: A = nucleon number, Z = proton number, N = A − Z.
  • Isotopes: same Z, different N. Same chemistry, different mass and stability.
  • Specific charge = charge ÷ mass (C kg−1). Nucleus: Ze ÷ (Zmp + Nmn). Electron has the largest.
