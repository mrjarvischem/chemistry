Lesson 9 · Wave–particle duality

AQA A-level Physics 3.2.2.4 · about 25 minutes

Waves or particles?

Light and electrons both show wave behaviour in some experiments and particle behaviour in others. Tap each card.

Light behaving as a WAVE
Diffraction and interference (e.g. Young’s double slit, diffraction gratings). Only waves superpose to give bright and dark fringes.
Light behaving as a PARTICLE
The photoelectric effect: threshold frequency and instant emission can only be explained if light comes in packets (photons), each interacting with one electron.
Electrons behaving as PARTICLES
They have a definite mass and charge (specific charge), are deflected by fields, and collide with atoms (excitation, ionisation).
Electrons behaving as a WAVE
Electron diffraction: a beam of electrons passing through a thin crystal (e.g. graphite) produces a ring pattern — a diffraction pattern.
Electron diffraction suggests particles have wave properties; the photoelectric effect suggests electromagnetic waves have a particle nature. Neither model alone explains everything — this is wave–particle duality.

The de Broglie wavelength

In 1924 Louis de Broglie suggested that if light waves can behave like particles, then particles should behave like waves. A moving particle has a wavelength:

λ = hmv    where mv is the momentum p

de Broglie calculator

Speed: m s−1
Diffraction is only noticeable when the wavelength is similar to the size of the gap or object. Atoms in a crystal are about 10−10 m apart, so electrons with λ ≈ 10−10 m diffract. A tennis ball’s wavelength is ~10−34 m — far too small for any diffraction to be seen.

Electron diffraction

Electrons are accelerated through a p.d. and pass through thin graphite. The rings on the screen are a diffraction pattern. Change the accelerating voltage.

Accelerating p.d.: kV
How is λ calculated from the p.d.?KE gained: ½mv² = eV → momentum p = mv = √(2meV)
λ = h/p = h / √(2meV)
So λ ∝ 1/√V — doubling V does not halve λ.
Higher p.d. → electrons faster → greater momentum → shorter de Broglie wavelength → less diffraction → rings get smaller (closer to the centre).

    How scientific knowledge changes

    Our model of light and matter has changed many times. Tap the timeline. Blue = evidence for waves; red = evidence for particles.

    New ideas (like photons, or de Broglie’s matter waves) are accepted only after they are tested by experiment, published, checked through peer review and validated by other scientists repeating the work. Davisson–Germer and G. P. Thomson independently confirmed electron diffraction in 1927, three years after de Broglie’s prediction.

    Lesson summary — and the end of section 3.2!

    • Electron diffraction → particles have wave properties. Photoelectric effect → EM waves have particle properties.
    • de Broglie wavelength λ = h/mv = h/p.
    • Greater momentum → shorter λ → less diffraction (smaller rings).
    • Diffraction is significant when λ is similar to the gap/spacing.
    • Scientific models change when new evidence appears; new ideas are validated by peer review and repeat experiments.
