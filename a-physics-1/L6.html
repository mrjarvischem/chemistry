Quarks, antiquarks and hadrons

Hadrons are made of quarks. You need the up (u), down (d) and strange (s) quarks and their antiquarks. Antiquarks have the opposite charge, baryon number and strangeness.

baryon number B+⅓+⅓+⅓−⅓−⅓−⅓
strangeness S00−100+1
Baryons = 3 quarks. Antibaryons = 3 antiquarks. Mesons = 1 quark + 1 antiquark.

Kaons: “K+ = us”. A strange quark has S = −1, so K+ (with an anti-strange) has S = +1. Students often get this sign backwards.

Change of quark character in β decay

In β decay the weak interaction changes the flavour (character) of a quark.

β−: d → u + W− (udd → uud).   β+: u → d + W+ (uud → udd).

Applying the conservation laws

The method

  1. Check charge Q, baryon number B, and lepton numbers Le and Lμ separately — all must balance in every interaction.
  2. Check strangeness S: must balance for a strong interaction; may change by ±1 in a weak one.
  3. Any neutrino involved → it must be weak.
  4. Energy and momentum are also always conserved (data for any unfamiliar particle will be given).

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • u: +⅔, d: −⅓, s: −⅓ (S = −1). Antiquarks: everything opposite. Each quark B = +⅓.
  • p = uud, n = udd, π⁺ = ud, π⁻ = du, π⁰ = uu/dd, K⁺ = us, K⁻ = su, K⁰ = ds, K̄⁰ = sd.
  • β⁻: d → u; β⁺: u → d.
  • Always conserved: Q, B, Le, Lμ (and energy, momentum). S: conserved in strong, can change by ±1 in weak.
