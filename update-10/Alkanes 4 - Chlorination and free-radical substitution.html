Chlorination of Alkanes – Free-Radical Substitution

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.3.2 Alkanes · walkthrough 4

1. Alkanes, chlorine and free radicals

Alkanes are quite unreactive: C–C and C–H bonds are strong and non-polar, so nothing is attracted to them.

But in ultraviolet (UV) light, alkanes react with chlorine (or bromine) by free-radical substitution: an H atom is swapped for a halogen atom.

CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl

A free radical is a species with an unpaired electron, shown with a dot: Cl•, •CH₃. Radicals are very reactive.

UV light provides the energy to break the Cl–Cl bond by homolytic fission: the bond breaks evenly and each atom takes one electron.

CHECK 1AQA AS Paper 2, 2020, Q01.3 (part)

1-Chloropropane can be produced by the reaction between propane and chlorine in the presence of ultraviolet light. State why ultraviolet light is needed. [1 mark]

To provide the energy to break the Cl–Cl bond (homolytically), forming chlorine free radicals.

2. The mechanism: initiation, propagation, termination

The chain reaction

cycles: 0

The Cl• used in propagation step 1 is made again in step 2 – so one Cl• from initiation can make thousands of CH₃Cl molecules before it's removed by termination.

How to spot each step: initiation – radicals made from a molecule (radicals only on the right). Propagation – one radical in, one radical out. Termination – two radicals in, none out.

3. Watch it happen – and why you get a mixture

Cl• radicals can also attack the product: Cl• + CH₃Cl → •CH₂Cl + HCl, then •CH₂Cl + Cl₂ → CH₂Cl₂ + Cl•. So you get a mixture – CH₃Cl, CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃, CCl₄ (and some C₂H₆ from termination).

  • Excess methane → mainly CH₃Cl (a Cl• is more likely to meet CH₄ than CH₃Cl).
  • Excess chlorine → substitution keeps going → mainly CCl₄: CH₄ + 4Cl₂ → CCl₄ + 4HCl.

For bigger alkanes, Cl can go on different carbons too (1-chloropropane and 2-chloropropane), which adds to the mixture.

4. Writing propagation steps for any molecule

The same two propagation steps work every time. If the reactant is R–H and the halogen is X₂:

Step 1:   X• + R–H → R• + H–X
Step 2:   R• + X₂ → R–X + X•

Work out where the X ends up in the product – the radical R• has its dot on that carbon.

Step 1 always makes HCl and a carbon radical. It never makes H• – that's a common wrong answer.

5. Exam questions

EXAM 1AQA AS Paper 2, 2020, Q01.3

Give an equation for each propagation step in the formation of 1-chloropropane from propane. [2 marks]

Cl• + CH₃CH₂CH₃ → •CH₂CH₂CH₃ + HCl
•CH₂CH₂CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl + Cl•
The dot must be on the end carbon to make 1-chloropropane.

CH₃CH₂•CHCH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃CH₂CHClCH₃ + Cl• (M2)
Dot anywhere on the second carbon. Extra initiation/termination steps are ignored.

•CCl₂F + Cl₂ → CCl₃F + •Cl (M2)
