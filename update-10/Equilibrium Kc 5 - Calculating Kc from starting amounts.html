Equilibrium (Kc) 5 – Calculating Kc from Starting Amounts

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibria walkthrough

1. Putting it together: from starting amounts to Kc

  1. ICE table in moles: find every equilibrium amount (Walkthrough 4).
  2. Divide by the volume in dm³ to get concentrations (Walkthrough 3).
  3. Write the Kc expression with square brackets.
  4. Substitute and calculate.
  5. Work out the units (Walkthrough 2).

A 6-mark question usually gives one mark for each of these, plus one for the ratio in the ICE table. Show every step: if you slip early, you still get the later marks.

Worked example with the mark scheme

June 2025 AS Paper 1 Q7.3 (6 marks)

CO₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g) + H₂O(g)
2.50 mol CO₂ is mixed with 6.00 mol H₂ in a sealed container of volume 4.60 dm³. At equilibrium, 1.30 mol CO₂ remains. Give the Kc expression, calculate Kc and give its units.

  1. M1 Kc = [CH₃OH] [H₂O][CO₂] [H₂]³
  2. M2 CO₂ reacted = 2.50 − 1.30 = 1.20 mol
  3. M3 ICE table:
    CO₂3H₂CH₃OHH₂O
    I / mol2.506.0000
    C / mol−1.20−3.60+1.20+1.20
    E / mol1.302.401.201.20
  4. M4 Divide by 4.60: [CO₂] = 0.283, [H₂] = 0.522, [CH₃OH] = [H₂O] = 0.261 mol dm⁻³
  5. M5 Kc = 0.261 × 0.2610.283 × 0.522³ = 1.70 (1.69–1.72 allowed)
  6. M6 n = 2 − 4 = −2 → mol⁻² dm⁶

Less CO₂ left means more reaction, more product, and a bigger Kc. In real life, Kc is fixed at a given temperature, so only one of these mixtures is the true equilibrium.

2. Mixing solutions: use the total volume

When two solutions are mixed, everything ends up in one mixture. The volume for Kc is the total volume.

Vtotal = V1 + V2 (+ any water added)

Animation: mixing

QUICK CHECK 1 June 2024 Paper 2 Q2.2 (4 marks)

2P(aq) + Q(aq) ⇌ R(aq) + 3S(aq). 25.0 cm³ of P solution is added to 20.0 cm³ of Q solution. At equilibrium: P = 0.0145 mol, Q = 0.0275 mol, R = 0.0115 mol, S = 0.0345 mol. Calculate Kc.

Units:
V = 25.0 + 20.0 = 45.0 cm³ = 0.0450 dm³
Kc = [R] [S]³[P]² [Q] = (0.0115/0.045)(0.0345/0.045)³(0.0145/0.045)²(0.0275/0.045) = 0.256 × 0.767³0.322² × 0.611 = 1.81 mol dm⁻³
Mark scheme: if the volume isn't used you get 0.0817 and lose a mark. Here the volume does matter because the powers don't balance (4 on top, 3 on the bottom).

3. Past-paper practice

QUICK CHECK 2 June 2022 Paper 2 Q2.1–2.3

2CHClF₂(g) ⇌ C₂F₄(g) + 2HCl(g). A 2.00 mol sample of CHClF₂ is heated in a container of volume 23.2 dm³. At equilibrium the mixture contains 0.270 mol CHClF₂. Calculate Kc.

Reacted = 1.73 mol → C₂F₄ = 0.865 mol, HCl = 1.73 mol
[CHClF₂] = 0.0116, [C₂F₄] = 0.0373, [HCl] = 0.0746 mol dm⁻³
Kc = 0.0373 × 0.0746²0.0116² = 1.53 mol dm⁻³
QUICK CHECK 3 June 2017 Paper 2 Q5.2

2CH₃COOH(l) + HOCH₂CH₂OH(l) ⇌ C₆H₁₀O₄(l) + 2H₂O(l). A mixture of 0.470 mol ethanoic acid and 0.205 mol ethane-1,2-diol reaches equilibrium. At equilibrium 0.180 mol ethanoic acid remains.

(a) Find the equilibrium amounts of the diol, the diester and water.

diol: diester: water:

(b) No volume is given. Calculate Kc anyway, using the amounts in moles.

Acid used = 0.470 − 0.180 = 0.290 mol → diester = 0.145, water = 0.290, diol = 0.205 − 0.145 = 0.060 mol
Kc = [diester] [H₂O]²[acid]² [diol] = 0.145 × 0.290²0.180² × 0.060 = 6.27 (no units)
Why does this work without a volume? 3 powers on top and 3 on the bottom, so V cancels. That's the subject of Walkthrough 6.
