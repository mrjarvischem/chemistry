Weak acids, Ka and pKa

Writing Ka expressions, using pKa to compare acid strength, and calculating the pH of a weak acid.

The acid dissociation constant, Ka

A weak acid only partially dissociates, so the solution is an equilibrium mixture:

CH3COOH ⇌ CH3COO− + H+

For ethanoic acid

Ka = [CH3COO−][H+][CH3COOH]

For any weak acid HA

HA ⇌ H+ + A−

Ka = [H+][A−][HA]

  • Ka is the acid dissociation constant. Units: mol dm−3.
  • Square brackets are essential: they mean concentration in mol dm−3.
  • Ka only changes with temperature.
  • Water is not included, because [H2O] is effectively constant.

CHECK 3

What are the units of Ka?

(mol dm−3 × mol dm−3) ÷ mol dm−3 = mol dm−3

pKa and acid strength

From Ka to pKa

pKa = −log10Ka

From pKa to Ka

Ka = 10−pKa

What the numbers tell you

  • Larger Ka: the equilibrium lies further to the right, more H+, stronger acid.
  • Smaller pKa: stronger acid.
AcidKa / mol dm−3pKa
methanoic acid1.78 × 10−43.75
benzoic acid6.31 × 10−54.20
ethanoic acid1.74 × 10−54.76
propanoic acid1.35 × 10−54.87

Methanoic acid has a Ka about 10 times larger than ethanoic acid, so its pKa is about 1 unit smaller.

Calculating the pH of a weak acid

Two assumptions

  1. Each HA that dissociates gives one H+ and one A−, so [H+] = [A−]. (H+ from water is ignored.)
  2. So little HA dissociates that [HA] at equilibrium = [HA] at the start.

With those two assumptions the Ka expression simplifies:

Ka = [H+]2[HA]    so    [H+] = √(Ka × [HA])

Worked example

Calculate the pH of 0.103 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid. Ka = 1.78 × 10−5 mol dm−3

1
Ka = [CH3COO−][H+][CH3COOH] = [H+]2[CH3COOH]
2
[H+]2 = Ka × [HA] = 1.78 × 10−5 × 0.103 = 1.83 × 10−6
3
[H+] = √(1.83 × 10−6) = 1.35 × 10−3 mol dm−3
4
pH = −log(1.35 × 10−3)

pH = 2.87

Working backwards: finding Ka or a concentration

Ka from pH

A 0.150 mol dm−3 solution of a weak acid HA has a pH of 2.95. Calculate Ka.

1
[H+] = 10−2.95 = 1.12 × 10−3 mol dm−3
2
Ka = [H+]2[HA] = (1.12 × 10−3)20.150
3

Ka = 8.39 × 10−6 mol dm−3

CHECK 12

A 0.200 mol dm−3 solution of a weak acid HA has a pH of 3.89. Calculate Ka.

[H+] = 10−3.89 = 1.29 × 10−4 mol dm−3
Ka = (1.29 × 10−4)2 ÷ 0.200 = 8.30 × 10−8 mol dm−3

CHECK 13

A 0.0850 mol dm−3 solution of a weak acid has a pH of 3.20. Calculate pKa.

[H+] = 6.31 × 10−4 mol dm−3
Ka = (6.31 × 10−4)2 ÷ 0.0850 = 4.68 × 10−6 mol dm−3
pKa = 5.33

CHECK 14

A solution of propanoic acid has a pH of 3.10. Calculate its concentration. Ka = 1.35 × 10−5 mol dm−3

[H+] = 10−3.10 = 7.94 × 10−4 mol dm−3
[HA] = [H+]2 ÷ Ka = (7.94 × 10−4)2 ÷ (1.35 × 10−5) = 0.0467 mol dm−3

Dilutions, and strong against weak

Diluting a weak acid

25.0 cm3 of 0.500 mol dm−3 propanoic acid is diluted to a total volume of 100.0 cm3. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.35 × 10−5 mol dm−3

1
New [HA] = 0.500 × (25.0 ÷ 100.0) = 0.125 mol dm−3
2
[H+] = √(1.35 × 10−5 × 0.125) = 1.30 × 10−3 mol dm−3
3

pH = 2.89

Both would need the same amount of NaOH to neutralise them. The weak acid just has far fewer H+ ions free in solution at any moment.

CHECK 15

10.0 cm3 of 0.400 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid is made up to 250 cm3. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.74 × 10−5 mol dm−3

New [HA] = 0.400 × (10.0 ÷ 250) = 0.0160 mol dm−3
[H+] = √(1.74 × 10−5 × 0.0160) = 5.28 × 10−4 mol dm−3
pH = 3.28

CHECK 16

A strong acid is diluted ×100 and its pH rises by 2.00. By how much does the pH of a weak acid rise when it is diluted ×100?

[H+] = √(Ka × [HA]). If [HA] falls by a factor of 100, [H+] only falls by a factor of √100 = 10, so the pH rises by 1.00.
