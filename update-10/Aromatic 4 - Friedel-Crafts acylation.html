Aromatic 4 – Friedel–Crafts Acylation

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.3.10 Aromatic chemistry · walkthrough 4

1. Friedel–Crafts acylation: adding C=O to the ring

Reagents: an acyl chloride RCOCl with an aluminium chloride (AlCl₃) catalyst, under anhydrous conditions, heated under reflux.

Product: a phenyl ketone (an aromatic ketone) + HCl.

C₆H₆ + CH₃COCl → C₆H₅COCH₃ + HCl

Why it matters: it makes a new C–C bond to the ring – an important step in synthesis (the ketone can then be reduced, etc.).

2. Making the electrophile, RCO⁺ (an acylium ion)

AlCl₃ has an Al atom with only six outer electrons – it accepts a lone pair from Cl (it is a Lewis acid / halogen carrier).

RCOCl + AlCl₃ → RCO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻

at the end: H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl    (catalyst regenerated)

Mark schemes allow the + on C or O in the equation, but in the mechanism it must be on the C that bonds to the ring.

4. Naming the products

Name the acyl part as the ketone, with phenyl as a substituent: C₆H₅COCH₃ = phenylethanone; C₆H₅COCH₂CH₃ = 1-phenylpropan-1-one. With HCOCl (methanoyl chloride) you get an aldehyde: C₆H₅CHO, benzaldehyde.

5. What happens to the ketone next?

Click a step.

Reducing the ketone with NaBH₄ (nucleophilic addition) gives a secondary alcohol, e.g. 1-phenylethanol. That alcohol can be dehydrated to phenylethene (the monomer for poly(phenylethene)).
