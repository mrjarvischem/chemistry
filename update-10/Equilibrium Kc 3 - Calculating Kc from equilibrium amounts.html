Equilibrium (Kc) 3 – Calculating Kc from Equilibrium Amounts

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibria walkthrough

1. Calculating Kc from equilibrium concentrations

If you're given the equilibrium concentrations, it's just substitution:

  1. Write the Kc expression.
  2. Put each equilibrium concentration in its square bracket. Apply the powers.
  3. Work out the value (calculator: use brackets!) and the units.

Worked example

H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). At equilibrium [H₂] = 0.0115, [I₂] = 0.0115 and [HI] = 0.0770 mol dm⁻³. Calculate Kc.

  1. Kc = [HI]²[H₂] [I₂]
  2. Kc = (0.0770)²(0.0115) × (0.0115) = 5.929 × 10⁻³1.3225 × 10⁻⁴
  3. Kc = 44.8
  4. Units: 2 − 2 = 0 → no units
QUICK CHECK 1

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g). At equilibrium [N₂] = 0.50, [H₂] = 1.20 and [NH₃] = 0.30 mol dm⁻³. Calculate Kc.

Kc = (0.30)²(0.50) × (1.20)³ = 0.090.864 = 0.104 mol⁻² dm⁶
Common slip: cubing 1.20 but forgetting to square 0.30.

2. From moles: divide by the volume first

Exam questions usually give you moles, not concentrations. Kc needs concentrations, so:

concentration (mol dm⁻³) = molesvolume in dm³

Volume in cm³? Divide by 1000 first. 250 cm³ = 0.250 dm³.

Animation: moles → concentration

The same moles in a smaller container are more crowded, so the concentration is higher. Change the volume.

Worked example

June 2018 AS Paper 2 Q8.3 (3 marks)

CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g). An equilibrium mixture in a container of volume 250 cm³ contains 0.340 mol CO, 0.190 mol H₂ and 0.0610 mol CH₃OH. Calculate Kc.

  1. Volume = 250 ÷ 1000 = 0.250 dm³
  2. [CO] = 0.340 ÷ 0.250 = 1.36   [H₂] = 0.190 ÷ 0.250 = 0.760   [CH₃OH] = 0.0610 ÷ 0.250 = 0.244 mol dm⁻³
  3. Kc = [CH₃OH][CO] [H₂]² = 0.2441.36 × 0.760² = 0.2440.7855
  4. Kc = 0.311 mol⁻² dm⁶

V = 0.750 dm³
[C₂H₅OH] = 0.100, [H₂O] = 0.208, [CO] = 0.1467, [H₂] = 0.2933 mol dm⁻³

QUICK CHECK 3 June 2024 AS Paper 2 Q7.7 (4 marks)

C₂H₄(g) + CO(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CH₃CH₂COOH(g). The equilibrium mixture in a 1200 cm³ container contains 0.078 mol C₂H₄, 0.062 mol CO, 0.062 mol H₂O and 0.420 mol CH₃CH₂COOH. Calculate Kc.

V = 1.2 dm³ → [CH₃CH₂COOH] = 0.350, [C₂H₄] = 0.065, [CO] = [H₂O] = 0.0517 mol dm⁻³
Kc = 0.3500.065 × 0.0517 × 0.0517 = 2.02 × 10³ mol⁻² dm⁶ (mark scheme allows 1990–2020)
If you forget the volume you get 1401: that scores only 2 of the 4 marks.
QUICK CHECK 4 June 2020 Paper 2 Q9.2 (4 marks)

A(aq) + 2B(aq) ⇌ C(aq). An equilibrium mixture contains 0.30 mol A, 0.25 mol B and 0.020 mol C in 350 cm³ of solution. Calculate Kc and deduce its units.

Units:
Kc = [C][A] [B]² = (0.020/0.35)(0.30/0.35) × (0.25/0.35)² = 0.13 mol⁻² dm⁶

4. What went wrong?

These are real wrong answers that AQA mark schemes list for June 2020 Paper 2 Q9.2 (correct answer Kc = 0.13 mol⁻² dm⁶). Diagnose each one.

Your checklist

  1. Volume converted to dm³?
  2. Every amount divided by the volume?
  3. Products on top?
  4. Powers applied to the whole concentration, e.g. (0.25/0.35)², not 0.25²/0.35?
  5. Units worked out from your expression?
