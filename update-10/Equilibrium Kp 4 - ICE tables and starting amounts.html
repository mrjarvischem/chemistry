Equilibrium (Kp) 4 – ICE tables and starting amounts

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibrium constant Kp walkthrough

1. From starting amounts: ICE table, then partial pressures

  1. ICE table in moles: Initial, Change (use the balancing numbers), Equilibrium.
  2. Total moles of gas at equilibrium. Add up the E row: it is usually not the same as the starting total.
  3. Mole fraction of each gas = E moles ÷ total.
  4. Partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure.
  5. Kp expression, substitute, units.

The only difference from Kc is steps 2–4: you divide by the total moles instead of by a volume.

Worked example with the mark scheme

November 2021 Paper 1 Q5.2 (4 marks)

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). 0.460 mol SO₂ is mixed with 0.250 mol O₂. At equilibrium the pressure is 215 kPa and the mixture contains 0.180 mol SO₃. Calculate the partial pressure of SO₂.

  1. ICE table:
    2SO₂O₂2SO₃
    I / mol0.4600.2500
    C / mol−0.180−0.090+0.180
    E / mol0.2800.1600.180
  2. M1 SO₂ = 0.460 − 0.180 = 0.280 mol   M2 O₂ = 0.250 − 0.090 = 0.160 mol
  3. M3 Total = 0.280 + 0.160 + 0.180 = 0.620 mol (it started at 0.710)
  4. M4 p(SO₂) = 0.2800.620 × 215 = 97.1 kPa

CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g). 0.430 mol CO is mixed with 0.860 mol H₂. At equilibrium the total pressure is 250 kPa and the mixture contains 0.110 mol CH₃OH. Calculate the amount of CO at equilibrium and its partial pressure.

n(CO) = mol
p(CO) = kPa
M1 n(CO) = 0.430 − 0.110 = 0.320 mol; H₂ = 0.860 − 0.220 = 0.640 mol
M2 Total = 0.320 + 0.640 + 0.110 = 1.07 mol
M3 p(CO) = 0.320/1.07 × 250 = 74.8 kPa

2 mol of gas → 2 mol of gas, so the total stays at 4.00 mol. Mole fraction = 0.92 ÷ 4.00 = 0.23.
The 20 dm³ volume is not needed.

3. Questions that give a percentage or fraction reacted

Some questions say that a fraction of a gas has dissociated (split up), for example "40% of the PCl₅ has dissociated". Use that fraction to fill in the C row.

Worked example (practice question)

PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g). 1.00 mol of PCl₅ is heated. At equilibrium 40.0% has dissociated and the total pressure is 200 kPa. Calculate Kp.

  1. PCl₅ reacted = 0.400 mol → E row: PCl₅ 0.600, PCl₃ 0.400, Cl₂ 0.400 mol
  2. Total = 1.400 mol
  3. p(PCl₅) = 0.600/1.40 × 200 = 85.7 kPa; p(PCl₃) = p(Cl₂) = 0.400/1.40 × 200 = 57.1 kPa
  4. Kp = 57.1 × 57.185.7 = 38.1 kPa
