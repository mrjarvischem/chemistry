Autocatalysis by Mn²⁺

Autocatalysis: Mn²⁺ in the manganate(VII)–ethanedioate reaction

An autocatalyst is a product of the reaction that catalyses the reaction itself. The reaction starts slowly, speeds up as the catalyst builds up, then slows as the reactants run out.

Why it starts slowly

Both reactants are negative ions. They repel each other, so the activation energy of the direct reaction is high.

Collision view

How Mn²⁺ provides a route with lower activation energy

Manganese has variable oxidation states. Mn²⁺ is oxidised to Mn³⁺ by MnO₄⁻, then Mn³⁺ is reduced back to Mn²⁺ by C₂O₄²⁻. Each step is between oppositely charged ions.

  1. Step 1: Mn²⁺ is oxidised to Mn³⁺
    4Mn2+ + MnO4− + 8H+ → 5Mn3+ + 4H2O

    Positive Mn²⁺ attracts negative MnO₄⁻.

  2. Step 2: Mn³⁺ is reduced back to Mn²⁺
    2Mn3+ + C2O42− → 2Mn2+ + 2CO2

    Positive Mn³⁺ attracts negative C₂O₄²⁻. Mn²⁺ is regenerated, so it is a catalyst.

  3. Why it is autocatalysis

    Mn²⁺ is also a product of the overall reaction. As more forms, there is more catalyst, so the reaction speeds up.
