Alkenes 2 – Electrophilic addition

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.3.4 Alkenes · walkthrough 2

1. Electrophiles and the C=C bond

  • An electrophile is an electron-pair acceptor. It is attracted to regions of high electron density.
  • The π bond in C=C is a region of high electron density, so it attacks electrophiles.
  • The reaction is electrophilic addition: the double bond opens and two atoms or groups add on. Two reactants make one product.

H–Br and H–OSO₃H

These are polar: Br and O are more electronegative than H, so the H is δ+. The δ+ H is the electrophile.

Br–Br

Br₂ is non-polar, but as it approaches the C=C, the π electrons repel the electrons in Br–Br. This induces a dipole: the nearer Br becomes δ+ and acts as the electrophile.

Curly-arrow rules for this mechanism (all double-headed):
  • Arrow 1 starts from the C=C bond and goes to the δ+ atom of the electrophile.
  • Arrow 2 starts from the H–Br (or Br–Br) bond and goes to the δ− atom.
  • Draw the carbocation with a + on the correct carbon (no bond drawn to the +).
  • Arrow 3 starts from a lone pair on the negative ion and goes to the C⁺.

2. Electrophilic addition, step by step

Making an alcohol

The product with concentrated sulfuric acid is an alkyl hydrogensulfate. Warming it with water hydrolyses it to an alcohol and regenerates the acid, so H₂SO₄ acts as a catalyst overall:

CH₃CH₂OSO₃H + H₂O → CH₃CH₂OH + H₂SO₄
