Empirical and Molecular Formulae

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.1.2 Amount of substance walkthrough

1. Empirical and molecular formulae

Empirical formula – the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.

Molecular formula – the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule of a compound.

The molecular formula is always a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula. Sometimes the multiple is 1, so they are the same.

Find the repeating unit

Pick a molecule. Its atoms are split into the biggest number of identical groups possible. One group is the empirical formula.

2. Empirical formula from percentages or masses

Use a table, one column per element:

  1. Write the % or mass of each element. If you have percentages, imagine 100 g, so % = grams.
  2. Divide each by its Ar to get moles.
  3. Divide by the smallest number of moles.
  4. Write the whole-number ratio as a formula.

If one element isn't given, it's the rest: % = 100 − (the others), or mass = total − (the others).

3. When the ratio isn't a whole number

After dividing by the smallest, you might get numbers like 1.5 or 1.33. Don't round these! Multiply every number by the same factor until they are all whole numbers.

Decimal ends in…Multiply byExample
.5× 21 : 1.5 → 2 : 3
.33 or .67× 31 : 1.33 → 3 : 4
.25 or .75× 41 : 2.25 → 4 : 9
.2, .4, .6 or .8× 51 : 1.6 → 5 : 8

Only round when a value is within about 0.1 of a whole number (e.g. 2.98 → 3 or 1.03 → 1). That small difference comes from rounding in the data.

Worked example

5.0 g of an oxide contains 4.0 g of molybdenum. Calculate the empirical formula of the oxide.

mass O = 5.0 − 4.0 = 1.0 g

4. Using Mr to find the molecular formula

n = Mr of compoundMr of empirical formula   →   molecular formula = (empirical formula) × n

The Mr is usually given, or comes from the molecular ion peak (the peak with the largest m/z) in a mass spectrum.

Stack the empirical units

Keep adding empirical formula units until their total mass matches the Mr.

Worked example

Compound R contains 61.0% carbon and 11.9% hydrogen by mass. The rest is oxygen. The mass spectrum of R has a molecular ion peak at m/z = 118. Find the empirical and molecular formulae.

O = 100 − 61.0 − 11.9 = 27.1%
C 61.0 ÷ 12.0 = 5.08 · H 11.9 ÷ 1.0 = 11.9 · O 27.1 ÷ 16.0 = 1.69
÷ 1.69 → 3.00 : 7.03 : 1 → empirical formula C₃H₇O
Mr(C₃H₇O) = 36 + 7 + 16 = 59.0  ·  n = 118 ÷ 59.0 = 2
molecular formula = (C₃H₇O) × 2 = C₆H₁₄O₂Multiply every subscript by 2.
