Atom Economy and Percentage Yield

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.1.2 Amount of substance walkthrough

1. Two ways to judge a reaction

Percentage yield

How much product you actually got, compared with the maximum possible.

Found by experiment. It depends on how well the reaction was carried out.

Atom economy

How much of the mass of the reactants ends up in the desired product.

Found from the balanced equation only. It measures the waste built into the reaction.

See the difference

Titanium is extracted from TiCl₄ using sodium: TiCl₄ + 4Na → Ti + 4NaCl. Change the yield and watch what does (and doesn't) change.

Even at 100% yield, 83% of the mass of the reactants becomes NaCl. Only the equation can change the atom economy. A better technique only improves the yield.

2. Percentage atom economy

% atom economy = Mr of desired productsum of Mr of all reactants × 100
  • Multiply each Mr by its coefficient in the balanced equation.
  • Addition reactions have an atom economy of 100% – there is only one product.
  • Atom economy does not depend on the masses used or on the yield.

Calculate the percentage atom economy for the formation of titanium: TiCl₄ + 4Na → Ti + 4NaCl (from your C2.10 lesson).

reactants: TiCl₄ = 47.9 + (4 × 35.5) = 189.9  ·  4Na = 4 × 23.0 = 92.0  ·  total = 281.9Remember the coefficient: 4Na means 4 × 23.0.
desired product: Ti = 47.9
% atom economy = 47.9281.9 × 100 = 17.0%
check: products = 47.9 + (4 × 58.5) = 281.9 ✓Mass is conserved, so the products must add up to the same total as the reactants.

CHECK 1AQA AS Paper 1, June 2025, Q01.1–01.2

The first stage in the extraction of titanium: TiO₂(s) + 2C(s) + 2Cl₂(g) → TiCl₄(l) + 2CO(g)

(a) Calculate the percentage atom economy to form TiCl₄. [2 marks]   %

reactants = 79.9 + (2 × 12.0) + (2 × 71.0) = 245.9 · TiCl₄ = 189.9
(189.9 ÷ 245.9) × 100 = 77.2%

(b) Suggest one economic advantage of using a process with a high atom economy. [1 mark]

More of the mass of the reactants ends up in the useful product / less waste (by-product) to dispose of. Just saying "it costs less" doesn't get the mark unless you say why.

3. Percentage yield

% yield = actual mass of producttheoretical mass of product × 100   or   actual moles of producttheoretical moles of product × 100

The theoretical (maximum) amount is calculated from the equation, using the limiting reactant.

Where does the product go?

Step through the reasons why you never get 100%.

In a laboratory simulation of the blast furnace, 40.0 g of iron(III) oxide produced 21.5 g of iron. Calculate the percentage yield. Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ (from your C2.10 lesson)

n(Fe₂O₃) = 40.0 ÷ 159.6 = 0.2506 mol
Fe₂O₃ : Fe = 1 : 2 → n(Fe) = 0.5013 mol
theoretical mass = 0.5013 × 55.8 = 27.97 g
% yield = 21.527.97 × 100 = 76.9%
CHECK 3AQA AS Paper 2, June 2020 (sat November 2020), Q03

A student makes methylpropanal (Mr = 72.0) by two methods, each starting from 1.00 g of organic starting material.

Method 1: (CH₃)₂CHCOCOOH (Mr = 116.0) → (CH₃)₂CHCHO + CO₂  ·  yield of methylpropanal = 552 mg
Method 2: (CH₃)₂CHCH₂OH (Mr = 74.0) + H₂O₂ → (CH₃)₂CHCHO + 2H₂O

(a) Calculate the percentage yield for Method 1.   %

(b) Calculate the percentage atom economy for Method 2.   %

(a) reactant = 1.00 ÷ 116.0 = 0.00862 mol · product = 0.552 ÷ 72.0 = 0.00767 mol · 1 : 1 → (0.00767 ÷ 0.00862) × 100 = 88.9%
(b) 72.0 ÷ (74.0 + 34.0) × 100 = 72.0 ÷ 108.0 × 100 = 66.7%

(c) State the importance of each when choosing a method.

% yield: you want to get as much product as possible – efficient conversion of reactants into products.
% atom economy: you want as much of the mass of the reactants as possible to end up in the desired product – fewer by-products.

4. Liquids, and using a yield

Liquids: mass (g) = volume (cm³) × density (g cm⁻³)

Using a yield forwards: actual mass = theoretical mass × % yield100

Working backwards to find how much reactant you need: divide by the yield (you need more reactant).

20.0 cm³ of cyclohexanol (Mr = 100.0, density 0.962 g cm⁻³) produced 9.85 g of cyclohexene (Mr = 82.0). C₆H₁₁OH → C₆H₁₀ + H₂O. Calculate the percentage yield. (From your C2.10 lesson.)

mass = 20.0 × 0.962 = 19.24 g → n = 19.24 ÷ 100.0 = 0.1924 mol
n(cyclohexene) = 9.85 ÷ 82.0 = 0.1201 mol
1 : 1 → % yield = 0.12010.1924 × 100 = 62.4%For a 1 : 1 reaction you can compare moles directly.

A student added an excess of concentrated phosphoric acid to 14.4 g of cyclohexanol (Mr = 100.0) and obtained 4.15 cm³ of cyclohexene (Mr = 82.0, density = 0.810 g cm⁻³). Calculate the percentage yield. Give your answer to the appropriate number of significant figures. [5 marks]

%

Using a yield

TiCl₄ + 4Na → Ti + 4NaCl. The percentage yield is 85.0%. Calculate the mass of titanium produced from 1.00 kg of sodium. (From your C2.10 lesson.)

n(Na) = 1000 ÷ 23.0 = 43.48 mol
Na : Ti = 4 : 1 → n(Ti) = 10.87 mol → theoretical mass = 10.87 × 47.9 = 520.7 g
actual = 520.7 × 0.850 = 443 g

Multi-step syntheses

When a product is made in several steps, the overall yield is the yields multiplied together. It drops fast!
