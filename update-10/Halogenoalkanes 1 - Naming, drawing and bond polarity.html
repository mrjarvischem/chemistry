Halogenoalkanes – Naming, Drawing and Bond Polarity

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.3.3 Halogenoalkanes · walkthrough 1

1. Naming halogenoalkanes

Halogenoalkanes are alkanes with one or more H atoms replaced by a halogen. The halogen is a prefix:

F → fluoro Cl → chloro Br → bromo I → iodo

  • Same rules as alkanes: longest chain, lowest numbers, di/tri, and all prefixes in alphabetical order (bromo, chloro, ethyl, fluoro, iodo, methyl).
  • The halogen is just another prefix – it doesn't get priority over a methyl when numbering. Lowest set of numbers wins; if it's a tie, the first in the alphabet gets the lower number.
  • One carbon or two: no number needed for a single halogen – chloromethane, bromoethane.

Worked examples

CHECK 1AQA AS Paper 2, June 2024, Q03.1

CFCl₃ used to be the propellant in most aerosol cans. Use IUPAC rules to name CFCl₃. [1 mark]

trichlorofluoromethane – chloro before fluoro alphabetically (ignore the "tri").
CHECK 2AQA A-level Paper 2, June 2018, Q02.1

Give the IUPAC name for CH₂(OH)CH(CH₃)CH₂Br. [1 mark]

3-bromo-2-methylpropan-1-ol. The –OH sets the ending (-ol) and gets the lowest number, so C1 is the CH₂OH carbon. Bromo and methyl are prefixes in alphabetical order.

4. The polar C–X bond

Halogens are more electronegative than carbon, so the C–X bond is polar: Cδ+–Xδ−

The δ+ carbon is electron-deficient, so it is attacked by nucleophiles – species with a lone pair of electrons (like OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃) that they donate to form a new bond.

Going down the group, electronegativity decreases, so the bond gets less polar – but the bond also gets longer and weaker.

Exam favourite: C–F is the most polar bond but fluoroalkanes are the least reactive. Reactivity is controlled by bond enthalpy, not polarity – the weaker C–I bond breaks most easily (walkthrough 2).
CHECK 3AQA AS Paper 2, June 2025, Q01.1

Halogenoalkanes undergo substitution reactions with nucleophiles such as CN⁻, NH₃ and OH⁻. Explain why halogenoalkanes react with nucleophiles. [2 marks]

The halogen is more electronegative than carbon, so the C–X bond is polar / the carbon is δ+ (M1).
The nucleophile donates a lone pair of electrons to / is attracted to the δ+ carbon (M2).
CHECK 4AQA A-level Paper 2, June 2018, Q02.2

CH₂(OH)CH(CH₃)CH₂Br reacts with NaOH by nucleophilic substitution. Explain why the halogenoalkane is attacked by the nucleophile. [3 marks]

Br is more electronegative than C (M1)
so the C–Br bond is polar and the C is δ+ / electron deficient (M2)
the OH⁻ nucleophile has a lone pair which it donates to / is attracted to the δ+ C (M3)
