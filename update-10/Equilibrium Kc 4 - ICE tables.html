Equilibrium (Kc) 4 – Equilibrium Amounts and ICE Tables

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibria walkthrough

1. Finding equilibrium amounts with an ICE table

Usually you're told the starting amounts and only one equilibrium amount. You work out the rest with an ICE table:

IInitial moles: what you start with
CChange: how many moles react or form. These must follow the ratio in the equation.
EEquilibrium moles = Initial + Change
  • Reactants go down (−), products go up (+). Assume the reaction goes forwards unless you're told otherwise.
  • Work in moles, not concentrations. The volume comes later (Walkthrough 5).
  • Find the change for the substance you know both numbers for first. Then use the ratio.

Animated worked example

June 2019 AS Paper 1 Q5.1 (2 marks)

2NO(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ 2NOCl(g). 1.50 mol NO is mixed with 1.00 mol Cl₂. At equilibrium there is 0.350 mol NOCl. Find the amounts of NO and Cl₂ at equilibrium.

2. Practice ICE tables

Fill in the Change and Equilibrium rows, then press Check. Use + for amounts formed and − for amounts used up. A new random problem appears every time.

Common errors: ignoring the ratio (e.g. taking 0.350 mol of Cl₂ away when only 0.175 mol reacted); adding the change to a reactant instead of subtracting; forgetting that a product already present at the start must be added on to.

QUICK CHECK 3 June 2022 Paper 2 Q2.1 (2 marks)

2CHClF₂(g) ⇌ C₂F₄(g) + 2HCl(g). 2.00 mol CHClF₂ is heated. At equilibrium 0.270 mol CHClF₂ is left. Find the amounts of C₂F₄ and HCl.

HCl: mol
CHClF₂ reacted = 2.00 − 0.270 = 1.73 mol
C₂F₄ = 1.73 ÷ 2 = 0.865 mol   HCl = 1.73 × 2/2 = 1.73 mol
QUICK CHECK 4 June 2022 AS Paper 2 Q19 (1 mark)

CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g). 2.0 mol CO is mixed with 3.0 mol H₂. The equilibrium mixture contains 0.6 mol methanol. What is the total amount of gas at equilibrium?

CO = 2.0 − 0.6 = 1.4   H₂ = 3.0 − 1.2 = 1.8   CH₃OH = 0.6
Total = 1.4 + 1.8 + 0.6 = 3.8 mol

4. Harder ICE tables

A product is there at the start

June 2018 Paper 2 Q4.1 (5 marks)

2A + B ⇌ 3C + D. A beaker contained 40 cm³ of 0.16 mol dm⁻³ A. Then 9.5 × 10⁻³ mol B and 2.8 × 10⁻² mol C were added. At equilibrium there was 3.9 × 10⁻³ mol A. Find the equilibrium amounts of B, C and D.

  1. Initial A = 0.040 × 0.16 = 6.4 × 10⁻³ mol (moles = conc × volume in dm³)
  2. A used = 6.4 × 10⁻³ − 3.9 × 10⁻³ = 2.5 × 10⁻³ mol. The equation has 2A, so "one lot" of reaction, x = 2.5 × 10⁻³ ÷ 2 = 1.25 × 10⁻³
  3. 2AB3CD
    I6.4 × 10⁻³9.5 × 10⁻³2.8 × 10⁻²0
    C−2x−x+3x+x
    E3.9 × 10⁻³8.25 × 10⁻³0.03181.25 × 10⁻³
  4. C started at 2.8 × 10⁻², so 3x is added on: 0.028 + 0.00375 = 0.0318 mol.

Using x

Calling one "lot" of reaction x makes the ratio automatic: each change is just the balancing number × x.

QUICK CHECK 5 June 2017 AS Paper 1 Q4.1 (1 mark)

P(g) + Q(g) ⇌ 2R(g). 1.0 mol P and 1.0 mol Q are mixed. At equilibrium x mol of P has reacted, leaving (1 − x) mol of P and of Q. How many moles of R are there?

QUICK CHECK 6 – working backwards June 2024 Paper 2 Q2.1 (2 marks)

2P(aq) + Q(aq) ⇌ R(aq) + 3S(aq). A 25.0 cm³ sample of P solution is added to 20.0 cm³ of Q solution. At equilibrium: P = 0.0145 mol, Q = 0.0275 mol, R = 0.0115 mol, S = 0.0345 mol.
Calculate the amount of P before the reaction, and the concentration of the original P solution.

Initial P: mol
[P] in the 25.0 cm³ sample: mol dm⁻³
0.0115 mol R formed, so 2 × 0.0115 = 0.0230 mol P was used.
Initial P = 0.0145 + 0.0230 = 0.0375 mol
[P] = 0.0375 ÷ 0.0250 = 1.50 mol dm⁻³ (use the volume of the P sample only)
