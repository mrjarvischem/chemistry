Rate Equations 5 – The Arrhenius Equation: Calculations

AQA A-level Chemistry · Kinetics walkthrough

1. The Arrhenius equation

The rate constant k changes with temperature and activation energy. The Arrhenius equation links them:

k = A e−Ea / RT
SymbolMeaningUnits
krate constantdepends on the overall order
AArrhenius constant (pre-exponential factor). Linked to how often particles collide with the right orientationsame units as k
Eaactivation energyJ mol⁻¹ in the equation (not kJ!)
Rgas constant = 8.31J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹
TtemperatureK (add 273 to °C)

The part e−Ea/RT is the fraction of collisions with energy ≥ Ea. A is the total frequency of collisions with the right orientation. So k ≈ how often collisions happen × the fraction that succeed.

The equation is given on the exam paper. You don't need to remember it, but you do need to be able to rearrange it.

Interactive: what controls k?

The graph shows how k changes with temperature, compared with its value at 298 K, for the Ea you choose. Try a 10 K rise with Ea ≈ 50 kJ mol⁻¹: the rate roughly doubles. Then try a bigger Ea.

What the equation shows

  • Increase T → −Ea/RT becomes less negative → e−Ea/RT gets bigger → k increases. The increase is exponential, not proportional.
  • Lower Ea (a catalyst) → e−Ea/RT gets bigger → k increases.
  • Changing concentration doesn't appear anywhere in the equation, so it doesn't change k.

2. Rearranging the equation

Take natural logs (ln) of both sides. This is the form most questions use:

ln k = ln A − EaRT
To find…Use
kk = A × e−Ea/RT
EaEa = RT (ln A − ln k) = RT ln(A/k)
AA = k ÷ e−Ea/RT = k × eEa/RT
TT = Ea ÷ (R × ln(A/k))

Worked example

Rate Equations 4 Q1

A = 3.62 × 10⁷ s⁻¹ and k = 8.92 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ at 298 K. Calculate Ea.

  1. Ea = RT ln(A/k)
  2. A/k = 3.62 × 10⁷ ÷ 8.92 × 10⁻³ = 4.058 × 10⁹ → ln = 22.12
  3. Ea = 8.31 × 298 × 22.12 = 54 788 J mol⁻¹
  4. ÷ 1000 → 54.8 kJ mol⁻¹
QUICK CHECK 1 June 2017 Paper 2 Q3.2 (4 marks)

ln k = −Ea/RT + ln A. At 25 °C k = 2.8 × 10⁻² mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹ and ln A = 16.9. Calculate Ea in kJ mol⁻¹.

M1 ln k = ln(2.8 × 10⁻²) = −3.58
M2 Ea = RT(ln A − ln k)
M3 Ea = 8.31 × 298 × (16.9 + 3.58) = 50 716 J mol⁻¹
M4 51 kJ mol⁻¹

3. Comparing rates: temperature and catalysts

"How many times faster?" questions: work out k for both conditions, then divide. A cancels, so you can also go straight to the ratio:

k₂k₁ = e−Ea/RT₂ ÷ e−Ea/RT₁

With all concentrations the same, the ratio of rates = the ratio of rate constants.

4. Two temperatures, and avoiding common errors

If you know k at two temperatures, you can find Ea without knowing A. Subtracting the two ln forms gives:

lnk₁k₂ = EaR(1T₂ − 1T₁)

The exam gives you this equation when it's needed.

June 2022 Paper 2 Q1.7 (5 marks)

T₁ = 303 K with k₁ = 1.55 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹, and T₂ = 333 K with k₂ = 1.70 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹. Calculate Ea in kJ mol⁻¹.

  1. ln(k₁/k₂) = ln(1.55 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ 1.70 × 10⁻⁴) = ln(0.0912) = −2.395
  2. 1/T₂ − 1/T₁ = 1/333 − 1/303 = −2.973 × 10⁻⁴
  3. Ea/R = −2.395 ÷ −2.973 × 10⁻⁴ = 8055
  4. Ea = 8055 × 8.31 = 66 940 J mol⁻¹ = 66.9 kJ mol⁻¹

Spot the error

Each "student answer" below has one mistake. Click to reveal it.

Ea = 8.31 × 25 × ln(A/k) … Temperature in °C. It must be in kelvin (298 K).
k = A e−54.8/(8.31 × 298) … Ea in kJ. It must be 54 800 J mol⁻¹ to match R in J.
Ea = −52.4 kJ mol⁻¹ … Negative Ea. Activation energy is always positive. Check the order of the ln terms.
ln A = 16.9, so I used ln(16.9) = 2.83 … Already a log. 16.9 is ln A, so use it directly.
Units of A: J mol⁻¹ … Wrong units. A has the same units as k.

Calculator tips

  • ex is usually SHIFT + ln. Put the whole power in brackets: e^(−54800 ÷ (8.31 × 298)).
  • Work out −Ea/RT first and write it down. It's usually worth a method mark.
  • Don't round in the middle of a calculation. Keep the full calculator value until the end.
