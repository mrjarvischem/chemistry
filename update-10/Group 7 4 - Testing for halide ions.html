Group 7 – Testing for Halide Ions

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.2.3 Group 7, the halogens · walkthrough 4

1. The silver nitrate test

  1. Add dilute nitric acid to the solution.
  2. Add silver nitrate solution, AgNO₃(aq).
  3. Look at the colour of any precipitate: Ag+(aq) + X−(aq) → AgX(s)
IonF−Cl−Br−I−
Precipitatenone (AgF is soluble)white AgClcream AgBryellow AgI
Why add nitric acid first? To remove other ions, such as carbonate (CO₃2−) or hydroxide, which would also form precipitates with Ag+ and give a false positive:
CO₃2− + 2H+ → CO₂ + H₂O
Don't use HCl (it adds Cl− ions) or H₂SO₄.

2. Telling the precipitates apart with ammonia

White, cream and yellow can look similar – so add ammonia solution to the precipitate:

dilute NH₃concentrated NH₃
AgCl (white)dissolvesdissolves
AgBr (cream)doesn't dissolvedissolves
AgI (yellow)doesn't dissolvedoesn't dissolve

The precipitate dissolves by forming a colourless complex ion: AgCl + 2NH₃ → [Ag(NH₃)₂]+ + Cl−

Solubility in ammonia decreases down the group – this is how silver halides of a mixture can be separated.

4. Exam questions

EXAM 1AQA A-level Paper 1, 2020, Q09.1

State what is observed when silver nitrate solution is added to sodium fluoride solution. [1 mark]

No visible change / stays colourless – AgF is soluble.
EXAM 2AQA A-level Paper 1, June 2019, Q05.5

Solution Y contains two different negative ions. A student adds silver nitrate solution → cream precipitate containing compounds D and E; then excess dilute nitric acid → cream precipitate D remains and bubbles of gas F; then excess concentrated ammonia → colourless solution containing complex ion G. Give the formulas of D, E and F, an ionic equation for the formation of E, and an equation for D → G. [6 marks]

D = AgBr · E = Ag₂CO₃ · F = CO₂
2Ag+ + CO₃2− → Ag₂CO₃
AgBr + 2NH₃ → [Ag(NH₃)₂]+ + Br−
This is exactly why you acidify first – the carbonate also gave a precipitate.
EXAM 3AQA A-level Paper 1, June 2022, Q06.5

A student adds acidified silver nitrate solution to a solution of an unknown impure sodium halide and sees bubbles of gas and a colourless solution. The gas turns calcium hydroxide solution (limewater) cloudy. Deduce the sodium halide, the gas, and give an ionic equation for the formation of the gas from the impurity. [3 marks]

Sodium fluoride (no precipitate) · carbon dioxide · CO₃2− + 2H+ → CO₂ + H₂O
EXAM 4AQA AS Paper 1, 2020, Q07.3–07.4

600 mg of a mixture of NaCl and NaI is dissolved in water. An excess of aqueous silver nitrate is added, then excess dilute ammonia, which dissolves the silver chloride. The silver iodide is filtered, washed and dried: 315 mg. (a) Suggest why an excess of silver nitrate is used. (b) Calculate the amount, in moles, of AgI. (Mr(AgI) = 234.8) [2 marks]

(b) mol

(a) To make sure all the halide ions are precipitated.
(b) 0.315 ÷ 234.8 = 1.34 × 10−³ mol

Going further (practice): mol NaI = 1.34 × 10−³ → mass NaI = 1.342 × 10−³ × 149.9 = 0.201 g → NaCl = 0.600 − 0.201 = 0.399 g → 66.5% NaCl by mass.
