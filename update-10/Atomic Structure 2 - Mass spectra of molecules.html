Atomic Structure 2 – Mass Spectra of Molecules

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.1.1 Atomic structure walkthrough · Cl₂, Br₂ and molecules containing Cl or Br

1. Quick recap – what a TOF spectrometer measures

  1. Ionisation. With electron impact, a high-energy electron knocks an electron off each particle. A molecule loses one electron to form a molecular ion, M⁺:
    Cl₂(g) + e⁻ → Cl₂⁺(g) + 2e⁻
  2. Acceleration. All the ions are accelerated by an electric field so they have the same kinetic energy.
  3. Ion drift. KE = ½mv², so lighter ions move faster and reach the detector first. Heavier ions have a longer time of flight.
  4. Detection. Ions hit the detector and gain an electron, which makes a current. The size of the current is proportional to the abundance of that ion.

The spectrum plots abundance (y-axis) against m/z (x-axis). For a 1+ ion, m/z is just the mass of the ion.

2. Why does Cl₂ give three molecular ion peaks?

Chlorine has two isotopes: 35Cl and 37Cl in a 3 : 1 ratio (75% and 25%).

A Cl₂ molecule is made of two chlorine atoms, and each one can be either isotope. So there are three possible molecules:

MoleculeMass of Cl₂⁺ (m/z)
35Cl–35Cl35 + 35 = 70
35Cl–37Cl  (or 37Cl–35Cl)35 + 37 = 72
37Cl–37Cl37 + 37 = 74

The question is: how tall is each peak?

The 4 × 4 grid

Imagine a bag of 4 chlorine atoms that matches the 3 : 1 ratio: three 35Cl and one 37Cl. Pick the first atom (row) and the second atom (column). Every square is one equally likely molecule. Hover over or tap a square, or click a peak button to count its squares.

The key idea: the m/z 72 molecule can be made two ways – 35 then 37, or 37 then 35. That's why it gets 6 squares, not 3.

3. Calculating the peak ratio

The quick way is to multiply the ratio numbers (or fractions) of each isotope. For an element with two isotopes in the ratio a : b:

X₂ peaks:   a × a  :  2 × a × b  :  b × b

The middle peak is doubled because the mixed molecule can be made two ways.

Chlorine, 3 : 1

m/z 70:  3 × 3 = 9³⁵Cl–³⁵Cl
m/z 72:  2 × 3 × 1 = 6³⁵Cl–³⁷Cl two ways
m/z 74:  1 × 1 = 1³⁷Cl–³⁷Cl
Ratio 70 : 72 : 74 = 9 : 6 : 1Check: 9 + 6 + 1 = 16 = 4 × 4 squares in the grid.

Bromine, 1 : 1

79Br and 81Br are in (almost exactly) equal abundance.

m/z 158:  1 × 1 = 1⁷⁹Br–⁷⁹Br
m/z 160:  2 × 1 × 1 = 2⁷⁹Br–⁸¹Br two ways
m/z 162:  1 × 1 = 1⁸¹Br–⁸¹Br
Ratio 158 : 160 : 162 = 1 : 2 : 1

4. Molecules containing Cl or Br atoms

The same idea works for any molecule. Assume the other atoms are their main isotopes (12C, 1H, 16O). Then only the halogen atoms give different masses.

Halogen atoms in the moleculeMolecular ion peaksRatioExample
one ClM and M+23 : 1CH₃Cl: 50 and 52
one BrM and M+21 : 1CH₃Br: 94 and 96
two ClM, M+2 and M+49 : 6 : 1CH₂Cl₂: 84, 86 and 88
two BrM, M+2 and M+41 : 2 : 1CH₂Br₂: 172, 174 and 176

"M" here means the molecular ion containing only the lighter halogen isotope.

Worked example – one chlorine atom

AQA AS Paper 2, June 2018, Q06.2

Compound A (3-chloro-2,4-dimethylpentane, C₇H₁₅Cl) has Mr = 134.5. The main isotopes of hydrogen and carbon are 1H and 12C. Chlorine is 75% 35Cl and 25% 37Cl. Draw the peaks for the main molecular ions. [2 marks]

C₇H₁₅ part = (7 × 12) + (15 × 1) = 99Use whole-number masses of the main isotopes.
with 35Cl: 99 + 35 = 134  ·  with 37Cl: 99 + 37 = 136134.5 is the Mr using Ar(Cl) = 35.5. No ion actually has a mass of 134.5!
Only one Cl atom → peak heights 134 : 136 = 3 : 1Mark scheme: lines at 134 and 136 (M1), and the line at 134 three times as high as the one at 136 (M2).
With one halogen atom there are only two peaks. Students often draw the Cl₂ pattern (three peaks, 9 : 6 : 1) by mistake. Count the halogen atoms first.

Working backwards

A Cl₂ spectrum from a different sample has peaks at 70, 72 and 74. The peaks at 70 and 74 are in the ratio 16 : 1. Find the ratio of 35Cl : 37Cl, and the relative height of the 72 peak.

70 peak = a × a = 16 → a = 4  ·  74 peak = b × b = 1 → b = 1The outside peaks are squares, so take the square root.
35Cl : 37Cl = 4 : 1
72 peak = 2 × 4 × 1 = 8  →  ratio 70 : 72 : 74 = 16 : 8 : 1
