Kinetics 1 – Calculating Rates of Reaction

AQA A-level Chemistry · Kinetics walkthrough

1. What is the rate of a reaction?

rate of reaction = change in amount of a reactant or producttime taken
  • The "amount" can be anything you can measure: volume of gas, mass, moles, concentration, even colour.
  • A reactant goes down, a product goes up. Rate is always given as a positive number.
  • The units are always: (units of what you measured) ÷ (units of time).

Interactive: build the units

AQA wants units written with negative powers: cm³ s⁻¹, not cm³/s. The June 2020 Paper 2 mark scheme says "NOT cm³/s", and June 2023 says "penalise g/min".

Mean rate

A mean (average) rate uses the total change over a period of time.

Magnesium reacts with acid. 45.0 cm³ of hydrogen is collected in the first 30 s.

  1. change = 45.0 − 0 = 45.0 cm³   time = 30 s
  2. mean rate = 45.0 ÷ 30 = 1.50 cm³ s⁻¹
  3. In cm³ min⁻¹: 45.0 cm³ in 0.5 min → 45.0 ÷ 0.5 = 90.0 cm³ min⁻¹ (the same rate, × 60)

The concentration of a reactant falls from 0.80 to 0.50 mol dm⁻³ in 2.0 minutes. Calculate the mean rate in mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹.

rate = 0.30 ÷ 120 = 2.5 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

Exam example

June 2023 Paper 2 Q1.2 (mark scheme)

A tangent to a mass-loss graph (mass in g, time in minutes) has a gradient of 0.50. The units are g min⁻¹. A student who converted to seconds got full marks too:

0.50 g min⁻¹ ÷ 60 = 8.3 × 10⁻³ g s⁻¹

Either is fine, as long as the number matches the units. "0.50 g s⁻¹" would be wrong.

3. From measurements to mol s⁻¹ and mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

To compare different reactions, convert your measurement into moles:

  • Mass of gas lost: n = m ÷ Mr
  • Volume of gas: n = pV ÷ RT (p in Pa, V in m³, T in K, R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹)
  • Concentration rate: divide the mol s⁻¹ by the volume of solution in dm³ → mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

Worked example 1: mass loss

CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g). The flask loses 0.66 g in the first 60 s. The acid volume is 50.0 cm³.

  1. The mass lost is CO₂ escaping. n(CO₂) = 0.66 ÷ 44.0 = 0.0150 mol
  2. rate of CO₂ formation = 0.0150 ÷ 60 = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ mol s⁻¹
  3. 2 HCl react per CO₂, so HCl is used up at 2 × 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol s⁻¹
  4. In 0.0500 dm³: rate = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0500 = 1.00 × 10⁻² mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

Worked example 2: gas volume with pV = nRT

48.0 cm³ of H₂ is collected in 40 s at 100 kPa and 298 K. Calculate the mean rate of H₂ formation in mol s⁻¹.

  1. Convert: p = 100 × 10³ Pa,   V = 48.0 × 10⁻⁶ m³ (cm³ → m³ is ÷ 10⁶)
  2. n = pVRT = 100 000 × 48.0 × 10⁻⁶8.31 × 298 = 1.94 × 10⁻³ mol
  3. rate = 1.94 × 10⁻³ ÷ 40 = 4.85 × 10⁻⁵ mol s⁻¹

4. Rates of different substances: use the equation

Different substances in the same reaction change at different rates. The ratio is the balancing numbers.

2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 10CO₂

Every time 2 MnO₄⁻ react, 5 C₂O₄²⁻ react and 10 CO₂ form. So CO₂ forms 5 times faster than MnO₄⁻ is used up.

rate for X = rate for Y × balancing number of Xbalancing number of Y
