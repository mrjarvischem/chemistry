Common mistakes

Each line contains one mistake. Find it before you reveal the answer.

pH of 0.10 mol dm−3 H2SO4 = −log(0.10) = 1.00

H2SO4 is diprotic: [H+] = 0.20, pH = 0.70.

pH of 0.10 mol dm−3 NaOH = −log(0.10) = 1.00

0.10 is [OH−]. [H+] = 10−14 ÷ 0.10 = 10−13, pH = 13.00.

Excess H+ = 1.0 × 10−3 mol after mixing 25 cm3 and 15 cm3: [H+] = 1.0 × 10−3 ÷ 0.025

Divide by the total volume: 0.040 dm3.

pH of pure water at 50 °C = −log(5.47 × 10−14)

That is −log Kw. [H+] = √Kw first, then take −log.

Water at 60 °C has pH 6.5, so it is acidic

It is neutral, because [H+] = [OH−].

Weak acid with some NaOH added: [H+] = √(Ka × [HA])

The mixture contains HA and A−, so it is a buffer: [H+] = Ka × mol HA ÷ mol A−.

HCl added to a buffer: mol HA decreases, mol A− increases

The other way round. A− + H+ → HA, so HA increases and A− decreases.

Ethanoic acid with NaOH: use methyl orange

The vertical section is about pH 7 to 11, so use phenolphthalein.

The explain questions

The written answers that come up most often. Say each one out loud, then check.

CHECK 1

Define a Brønsted–Lowry acid.

A proton (H+) donor.

CHECK 2

What is meant by a weak acid?

An acid that is only partially dissociated in water.

CHECK 3

Explain why Kw increases as temperature increases.

The dissociation of water is endothermic, so the equilibrium shifts to the right as temperature rises. [H+] and [OH−] increase.

CHECK 4

Explain why pure water at 50 °C is neutral even though its pH is below 7.

[H+] = [OH−].

CHECK 5

State the two assumptions made when calculating the pH of a weak acid.

[H+] = [A−], and [HA] at equilibrium = [HA] at the start.

CHECK 6

Define a buffer solution.

A solution that resists changes in pH when small amounts of acid or alkali are added.

CHECK 7

Explain how a buffer of HA and A− resists a change in pH when a small amount of alkali is added.

HA + OH− → A− + H2O (or OH− reacts with H+ and the equilibrium HA ⇌ H+ + A− shifts right). The ratio [HA] : [A−] stays almost constant.

CHECK 8

Explain how the choice of indicator is made for a titration.

The pH range over which the indicator changes colour must lie completely within the vertical section of the pH curve.
