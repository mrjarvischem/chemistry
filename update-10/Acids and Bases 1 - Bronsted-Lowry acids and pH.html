Brønsted–Lowry acids and pH

What acids and bases are, the difference between strong and weak, and how to move between pH and [H+] for strong acids.

Acids donate protons, bases accept them

Brønsted–Lowry acid

A proton (H+) donor

Brønsted–Lowry base

A proton (H+) acceptor

An acid–base reaction is a proton transfer. To find the acid, look for the species that has lost an H+ by the end of the reaction. The base is the species that has gained one.

Example

HCl + H2O → H3O+ + Cl−

  • HCl becomes Cl−. It has lost H+, so HCl is the acid.
  • H2O becomes H3O+. It has gained H+, so H2O is the base.

H3O+ is the oxonium (hydroxonium) ion. In calculations H+(aq) and H3O+(aq) mean the same thing, so the equation is usually shortened to HCl → H+ + Cl−.

CHECK 1

Identify the acid and the base in the forward reaction: NH3 + H2O ⇌ NH4+ + OH−

Acid: H2O (it loses H+ to become OH−). Base: NH3 (it gains H+ to become NH4+). Water can act as an acid or as a base: it depends on what it reacts with.

CHECK 2

Identify the acid and the base in the forward reaction: CH3COOH + NH3 ⇌ CH3COO− + NH4+

Acid: CH3COOH. Base: NH3.

CHECK 3

Identify the acid and the base in the forward reaction: H2SO4 + HNO3 ⇌ HSO4− + H2NO3+

Acid: H2SO4 (loses H+). Base: HNO3 (gains H+ to become H2NO3+). Nitric acid is acting as a base here because sulfuric acid is the stronger acid.

CHECK 4

Identify the acid and the base in the forward reaction: HSO4− + H3O+ ⇌ H2SO4 + H2O

Acid: H3O+ (loses H+ to become H2O). Base: HSO4− (gains H+).

Strong and weak acids

Strong acid

Fully dissociated in water.

HCl → H+ + Cl−

Every molecule has dissociated, so [H+] = [HCl].

Strong acids to know: HCl, HNO3, H2SO4.

Weak acid

Partially dissociated in water.

CH3COOH ⇌ CH3COO− + H+

The ⇌ shows an equilibrium that lies well to the left.

Weak acids to know: carboxylic acids such as ethanoic acid.

Try it: what is in the beaker?

Strong does not mean concentrated. Strong and weak describe how much of the acid dissociates. Concentrated and dilute describe how much acid is dissolved in each dm3.

pH and hydrogen ion concentration

From [H+] to pH

pH = −log10[H+]

Calculator: (−) log, then the concentration.

From pH to [H+]

[H+] = 10−pH

Calculator: SHIFT log gives 10^x, then (−) and the pH.

  • [H+] is in mol dm−3.
  • Always give pH to 2 decimal places.
  • pH is a log scale: 1 pH unit is a ×10 change in [H+].

pH of a strong acid

A strong acid is fully dissociated, so the dissociation equation gives [H+] directly.

Monoprotic: HCl

Calculate the pH of 0.171 mol dm−3 hydrochloric acid.

1
Dissociation: HCl → H+ + Cl−
2
1 mol HCl gives 1 mol H+, so [H+] = 0.171 mol dm−3
3
pH = −log(0.171)

pH = 0.77

Diprotic: H2SO4

Calculate the pH of 0.054 mol dm−3 sulfuric acid. Assume both protons fully dissociate.

1
Dissociation: H2SO4 → 2H+ + SO42−
2
1 mol H2SO4 gives 2 mol H+, so [H+] = 2 × 0.054 = 0.108 mol dm−3
3
pH = −log(0.108)

pH = 0.97

Working backwards

From a pH, find [H+] first, then use the equation to find the acid concentration.

  • HCl with pH 1.07: [H+] = 10−1.07 = 0.0851, so [HCl] = 0.0851 mol dm−3
  • H2SO4 with pH 1.89: [H+] = 10−1.89 = 0.0129, so [H2SO4] = [H+] ÷ 2 = 6.44 × 10−3 mol dm−3

Diluting a strong acid

When an acid is diluted the moles of H+ stay the same but the volume increases. Find the new concentration first, then the pH.

new concentration = old concentration × old volumenew (total) volume

Worked example

25.0 cm3 of 0.070 mol dm−3 HCl is made up to 100.0 cm3 with deionised water. Calculate the pH of the diluted solution.

1
New concentration = 0.070 × (25.0 ÷ 100.0) = 0.0175 mol dm−3
2
Strong acid, so [H+] = 0.0175 mol dm−3
3
pH = −log(0.0175)

pH = 1.76

Read the question carefully. "Made up to 250 cm3" means the total volume is 250 cm3. "250 cm3 of water is added" means the total is the original volume + 250 cm3.

CHECK 16

10.0 cm3 of 0.200 mol dm−3 HCl is made up to 250 cm3. Calculate the pH.

New concentration = 0.200 × (10.0 ÷ 250) = 8.00 × 10−3 mol dm−3
pH = 2.10

CHECK 17

50.0 cm3 of 0.0600 mol dm−3 H2SO4 is diluted to a total volume of 200 cm3. Calculate the pH.

[H2SO4] = 0.0600 × (50.0 ÷ 200) = 0.0150 mol dm−3
[H+] = 2 × 0.0150 = 0.0300 mol dm−3
pH = 1.52

CHECK 18

Nitric acid has a pH of 1.20. What volume of water must be added to 25.0 cm3 of this acid to give a solution with a pH of 2.00?

Original [H+] = 10−1.20 = 0.0631 mol dm−3. Final [H+] = 0.0100 mol dm−3.
Final volume = 25.0 × (0.0631 ÷ 0.0100) = 157.7 cm3
Water added = 157.7 − 25.0 = 132.7 cm3
