Section 1

Why transition metals have variable oxidation states

Transition metals lose their 4s electrons first, then a variable number of 3d electrons. The 4s and 3d sub-shells are close in energy, so it takes a similar amount of energy to remove different numbers of electrons. Each transition metal therefore forms ions with several different oxidation states.

Common oxidation states, Sc to Zn (tap an element)

Manganese reaches +7 – it can use all of its 4s and 3d electrons (4s² 3d⁵).

Remember. Sc and Zn only form one ion each (Sc3+ 3d0 and Zn2+ 3d10), so they are not transition metals.
Section 2

Vanadium: four oxidation states, four colours

Vanadium species in oxidation states +4, +3 and +2 are formed by the reduction of vanadate(V) ions by zinc in acidic solution. Each oxidation state has its own colour.

Zinc and acid with ammonium vanadate(V) solution
The first green is not V3+. It is a mixture of yellow VO2+ and blue VO2+. Keep air out at the end: V2+ is easily oxidised back to V3+.

Why can zinc reduce vanadium all the way to +2? (links to electrode potentials)

E⦵(Zn2+/Zn) = −0.76 V is more negative than every vanadium couple, including E⦵(V3+/V2+) = −0.26 V. So zinc is a strong enough reducing agent for every step.

Section 4

Redox potential: the effect of pH and the ligand

The redox potential tells us how easily a transition metal ion changes from a higher to a lower oxidation state. It is influenced by pH and by the ligand.

pH: drag to change the H⁺ concentration
{MnO4^- + 8H^+ + 5e^- -> Mn^2+ + 4H2O}

Why pH matters. H+ is a reactant in the reduction half-equation. More H+ pushes the equilibrium to the right, so MnO4− is reduced more easily: it is a stronger oxidising agent in acid. In alkaline solution the reverse is true – metal ions are oxidised more easily (e.g. Fe(OH)2 darkens to Fe(OH)3 in air).

Tollens’ reagent

Tollens’ reagent contains the complex [Ag(NH3)2]+, with silver in the +1 oxidation state. Aldehydes reduce it to metallic silver; ketones do not. This distinguishes aldehydes from ketones.
