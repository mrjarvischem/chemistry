Equilibrium (Kp) 2 – Writing Kp expressions and units

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibrium constant Kp walkthrough

1. Writing a Kp expression

Kp is written just like Kc, but with partial pressures in place of concentrations.

aA(g) + bB(g) ⇌ cC(g) + dD(g)   →   Kp = p(C)c × p(D)dp(A)a × p(B)b
  • Products on top, reactants on the bottom. Balancing numbers become powers.
  • Write p(X) or pX. Never use square brackets: they mean concentration, and the mark scheme penalises them.
  • Only gases appear in Kp.

2. Units of Kp

Every partial pressure has units of pressure (kPa or Pa). Count the powers:

Δn = (total powers on top) − (total powers on the bottom)  →  units = kPaΔn
  • Δn = 0 → no units (they cancel)
  • Δn = 1 → kPa; Δn = −1 → kPa⁻¹; Δn = −2 → kPa⁻² …
  • If the pressures are in Pa, the units are PaΔn, and the number is different (1 kPa = 1000 Pa).

Changing kPa to Pa

June 2017 Paper 1 Q9.3 gave Kp = 9.41 × 10⁻⁷ kPa⁻² for CO + 2H₂ ⇌ CH₃OH. The mark scheme also accepts 9.41 × 10⁻¹³ Pa⁻², if every partial pressure is converted to Pa first.

Each pressure × 1000 → Kp × 1000Δn = 9.41 × 10⁻⁷ × 1000⁻² = 9.41 × 10⁻¹³ Pa⁻²
QUICK CHECK 2 June 2024 Paper 1 Q4.2 (1 mark)

H₂O(g) + CO(g) ⇌ H₂(g) + CO₂(g). State why Kp for this reaction has no units.

The amount in moles of products is the same as reactants (2 = 2), so the units of partial pressure cancel out.

3. Reversed and halved equations

  • Reverse the equation → the expression turns upside down → new Kp = 1Kp and the units flip sign.
  • Halve every balancing number → every power halves → new Kp = √Kp and the units are square-rooted.
  • Double every balancing number → new Kp = Kp².
QUICK CHECK 3 June 2022 Paper 1 Q1.6 (2 marks)

For CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g) at temperature T, Kp = 1.15 × 10⁻⁶ kPa⁻². Calculate Kp for CH₃OH(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 2H₂(g) and give its units.

Units:
Kp = 1 ÷ 1.15 × 10⁻⁶ = 8.70 × 10⁵ kPa²
QUICK CHECK 4 June 2019 Paper 1 Q7.4 (2 marks)

For 2SO₃(g) ⇌ 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) at 500 K, Kp = 3.94 × 10⁴ Pa. Calculate Kp at 500 K for SO₃(g) ⇌ SO₂(g) + ½O₂(g) and deduce its units.

Units:
Every balancing number is halved, so Kp = √(3.94 × 10⁴) = 198.5 Pa½ (allow 198–198.5)
