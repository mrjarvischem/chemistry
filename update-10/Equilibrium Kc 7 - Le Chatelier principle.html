Equilibrium (Kc) 7 – Le Chatelier's Principle

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibria walkthrough

1. Le Chatelier's principle

If a factor affecting an equilibrium is changed, the position of equilibrium shifts in the direction that opposes the change.

  • Position of equilibrium = how much product there is compared with reactant.
  • "Shifts to the right" (forwards) → more products, higher yield.
  • "Shifts to the left" (backwards) → more reactants, lower yield.

How to write the answer (3 marks)

  1. Effect: e.g. "the yield of HI increases".
  2. Direction: "the equilibrium shifts to the right / in the endothermic direction".
  3. Why: "to oppose the increase in temperature".

AQA marks each point separately. "Favours the forward reaction" is usually ignored, so say where the equilibrium shifts.

Changing concentration

A + B ⇌ C. The graph shows the concentrations as time goes on. Make a change and watch the system respond.

[A][B][C]
  • Add a reactant → equilibrium shifts right to use it up.
  • Remove a product → shifts right to replace it. (Industry does this to increase yield.)
  • Add a product → shifts left.
  • The system only partly cancels the change. After adding A, there is still more A than before.

2. Changing pressure (gases only)

  • Increase pressure → shifts to the side with fewer moles of gas, to reduce the pressure.
  • Decrease pressure → shifts to the side with more moles of gas.
  • Same number of gas moles on both sides → pressure has no effect on the position.

Count the gas moles using the balancing numbers. Ignore solids and liquids.

QUICK CHECK 2 June 2022 Paper 1 Q1.2 (1 mark)

A gas-phase reaction is at equilibrium. When the pressure is increased, the yield of product decreases. What can you deduce about the equation?

There are more moles of gas on the product (right-hand) side than on the reactant side. Increasing pressure shifts the equilibrium to the side with fewer gas moles, which is the reactant side.

3. Changing temperature, and adding a catalyst

The ΔH given is for the forward reaction. The reverse reaction has the opposite sign.

  • Increase temperature → shifts in the endothermic direction, to take in heat and lower the temperature.
  • Decrease temperature → shifts in the exothermic direction, to give out heat.
Forward reactionHigher TLower T
exothermic (ΔH −)shifts left, yield ↓shifts right, yield ↑
endothermic (ΔH +)shifts right, yield ↑shifts left, yield ↓

Catalysts

A catalyst speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally. So equilibrium is reached faster, but the position does not change and the yield is the same.

QUICK CHECK 4 June 2022 AS Paper 1 Q7.1–7.3

(a) How does the graph show that the forward reaction is exothermic?

At any pressure the yield is lower at the higher temperature (600 K). Raising T shifts the equilibrium in the endothermic direction, which here is to the left. So the forward reaction must be exothermic.

(b) Does the forward reaction increase, decrease or not change the number of moles of gas?

Yield falls as pressure rises → equilibrium shifts left at high pressure → the left has fewer gas moles → the forward reaction increases the moles of gas.

(c) Why does a catalyst have no effect on the percentage yield?

It increases the rates of the forward and reverse reactions equally, so the position of equilibrium doesn't change.

5. Industrial conditions: the compromise

Industry wants a good yield, a fast rate and a low cost. Le Chatelier alone isn't enough:

ConditionBest for yield (exothermic, fewer gas moles on the right)But…
Temperaturelowrate too slow → a compromise temperature is used

Catalystno effect on yieldfaster rate, so a lower temperature can be used → saves cost
