Equilibrium (Kc) 6 – Finding Missing Values and When Volume Cancels

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibria walkthrough

1. Finding a missing concentration from Kc

If you know Kc and every equilibrium concentration except one, you can find the missing one.

  1. Write the Kc expression.
  2. Rearrange so the unknown term is on its own.
  3. Substitute the numbers you know.
  4. If the unknown is squared, square root at the end (cubed → cube root).

Interactive: rearranging machine

Pick an equation and the substance you want to find.

Rule: whatever is on the bottom with the unknown moves to the top on the other side, and Kc moves to the bottom.

Worked example

June 2023 AS Paper 1 Q8.4 (3 marks)

A(aq) + 2B(aq) ⇌ C(aq). At equilibrium [A] = 0.48 mol dm⁻³ and [C] = 0.62 mol dm⁻³. Kc = 7.8 mol⁻² dm⁶. Calculate [B], to an appropriate number of significant figures.

  1. Kc = [C][A] [B]²   →   [B]² = [C][A] × Kc
  2. [B]² = 0.620.48 × 7.8 = 0.1656
  3. [B] = √0.1656 = 0.4069…
  4. The data are to 2 s.f., so [B] = 0.41 mol dm⁻³

2. Finding a missing amount in moles

When the question gives moles and a volume and asks for an amount, there's one extra step at the end:

moles → ÷ V → concentrations → rearrange Kc → missing concentration → × V → missing moles

The commonest lost mark is forgetting to multiply back by the volume.

Worked example

November 2021 AS Paper 1 Q10.2 (3 marks)

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). An equilibrium mixture in a 1800 cm³ container contains 0.176 mol SO₂ and 0.461 mol SO₃. Kc = 15.0 mol⁻¹ dm³. Calculate the amount of oxygen.

  1. M1 V = 1.80 dm³ → [SO₂] = 0.0978, [SO₃] = 0.256 mol dm⁻³
  2. M2 [O₂] = [SO₃]²[SO₂]² × Kc = 0.256²0.0978² × 15.0 = 0.457 mol dm⁻³
  3. M3 n(O₂) = 0.457 × 1.80 = 0.823 mol
QUICK CHECK 1 June 2019 AS Paper 1 Q5.3 (4 marks)

2NO(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ 2NOCl(g). In a flask of volume 800 cm³ an equilibrium mixture contains 0.850 mol NO and 0.458 mol Cl₂. Kc = 1.32 × 10⁻² mol⁻¹ dm³. Find the amount of NOCl in moles.

mol
[NOCl]² = Kc × [NO]² × [Cl₂] = 1.32 × 10⁻² × (0.850/0.800)² × (0.458/0.800) = 8.53 × 10⁻³
[NOCl] = 0.0924 mol dm⁻³ → n = 0.0924 × 0.800 = 0.0739 mol
Without the volume you'd get 0.0529 mol (max 3 marks).
QUICK CHECK 2 June 2022 AS Paper 1 Q7.4 (5 marks)

3H₂(g) + N₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), Kc = 0.118 mol⁻² dm⁶ at 745 K. 0.150 dm³ of an equilibrium mixture contains 0.0285 mol H₂ and 0.0870 mol N₂. Calculate the amount of ammonia.

mol
[H₂] = 0.190, [N₂] = 0.580 mol dm⁻³
[NH₃]² = 0.118 × 0.190³ × 0.580 = 4.69 × 10⁻⁴ → [NH₃] = 0.0217 mol dm⁻³
n(NH₃) = 0.0217 × 0.150 = 3.25 × 10⁻³ mol
QUICK CHECK 3 June 2018 Paper 2 Q4.3 (3 marks)

2A + B ⇌ 3C + D, Kc = 116 mol dm⁻³. A different equilibrium mixture with a total volume of 500 cm³ contains 0.21 mol B, 1.05 mol C and 0.076 mol D. Calculate [A] to 2 s.f.

mol dm⁻³
[A]² = [C]³ [D]Kc [B] = (1.05/0.5)³ × (0.076/0.5)116 × (0.21/0.5) = 0.0289
[A] = 0.17 mol dm⁻³

3. Which volume do I use?

SituationVolume to use
Gases in a sealed containerthe volume of the container
Two (or more) solutions mixedthe sum of all the volumes
Water added to an equilibrium mixturethe new total, including the water
"Assume the total volume stays at 100 cm³"just use 100 cm³
Concentration of an original solution before mixingthe volume of that solution only

4. When the volume doesn't matter

Every concentration is n ÷ V. Put that into Kc and the V's collect together:

Kc = (ratio of moles) × V−n    where n = (powers on top) − (powers on bottom)

If n = 0, i.e. the total number of moles on each side of the equation is the same, every V cancels:

  • you don't need the volume. Use moles straight in.
  • Kc has no units (the same test!).

2CH₃COOH(l) + HOCH₂CH₂OH(l) ⇌ C₆H₁₀O₄(l) + 2H₂O(l)

(a) The total volume of the mixture does not need to be measured to calculate Kc. Justify this statement.

The volume cancels out in the Kc expression or there are equal numbers of moles on each side of the equation.

(b) At a different temperature Kc = 6.45. A new equilibrium mixture contains 0.264 mol diol, 0.802 mol diester and 1.15 mol water. Calculate the amount of ethanoic acid, to 3 s.f.

mol
n(acid)² = n(diester) × n(H₂O)²Kc × n(diol) = 0.802 × 1.15²6.45 × 0.264 = 0.623
n(acid) = √0.623 = 0.789 mol (must be 3 s.f.)
Upside-down rearrangement gives 1.27 (1 mark only).

5. Stretch: finding the extent of reaction, x

Sometimes you only know the starting amounts and Kc. Call the amount that reacts x, write every equilibrium amount in terms of x, and solve. At A-level these are set up so that you can square root both sides.

June 2017 AS Paper 1 Q4.2 (3 marks)

P(g) + Q(g) ⇌ 2R(g). 1.0 mol P and 1.0 mol Q are mixed in a 1.0 dm³ container. Kc = 3.6 at 298 K. Calculate [R] at equilibrium.

  1. At equilibrium: P = (1 − x), Q = (1 − x), R = 2x. V = 1.0 dm³, so these are also the concentrations.
  2. Kc = (2x)²(1 − x)(1 − x) = (2x)²(1 − x)² = 3.6
  3. Both sides are squares, so square root: 2x1 − x = √3.6 = 1.897
  4. 2x = 1.897 − 1.897x → 3.897x = 1.897 → x = 0.487
  5. [R] = 2x = 0.97 mol dm⁻³
