Mixing strong acids and strong bases

Finding the pH after a strong acid and a strong base are mixed: work out which is in excess, then divide by the total volume.

Whatever is left over sets the pH

H+ + OH− → H2O

H+ and OH− always react 1 : 1. Whichever one there is more of is left over (in excess), and that is what decides the pH.

The method

  1. Calculate the moles of H+ and the moles of OH−.
  2. Subtract to find the moles in excess.
  3. Divide by the total volume (in dm3) to get a concentration.
  4. H+ in excess: pH = −log[H+].
    OH− in excess: [H+] = Kw ÷ [OH−], then pH = −log[H+].

1 mol H2SO4 gives 2 mol H+. 1 mol Ba(OH)2 gives 2 mol OH−.

CHECK 1

Set both solutions to 0.100 mol dm−3 HCl and NaOH, with 25.0 cm3 of acid. Slide the base from 24.5 to 25.5 cm3. What happens to the pH, and why is the change so large?

The pH jumps from about 3 to about 11. Near the end almost no H+ is left, so one drop of NaOH removes the rest and leaves OH− in excess. [H+] changes by a factor of about 100 million.

Step 1 and 2: which is in excess?

1 : 1 example

23.59 cm3 of 0.110 mol dm−3 HCl is mixed with 15.78 cm3 of 0.074 mol dm−3 NaOH.

1
mol H+ = 0.02359 × 0.110 = 2.59 × 10−3 mol
2
mol OH− = 0.01578 × 0.074 = 1.17 × 10−3 mol
3
H+ is in excess: 2.59 × 10−3 − 1.17 × 10−3

excess H+ = 1.43 × 10−3 mol

Diprotic acid

24.00 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 H2SO4 is mixed with 14.60 cm3 of 0.045 mol dm−3 NaOH.

1
mol H2SO4 = 0.02400 × 0.100 = 2.40 × 10−3 mol
2
× 2: mol H+ = 4.80 × 10−3 mol
3
mol OH− = 0.01460 × 0.045 = 6.57 × 10−4 mol
4

excess H+ = 4.14 × 10−3 mol

CHECK 3

30.0 cm3 of 0.120 mol dm−3 NaOH is mixed with 31.25 cm3 of 0.059 mol dm−3 H2SO4. Which is in excess, H+ or OH−?

mol OH− = 3.60 × 10−3   mol H+ = 2 × (0.03125 × 0.059) = 3.688 × 10−3
H+ is in excess (by 8.8 × 10−5 mol). Without doubling for H2SO4 you would get the wrong answer.

Acid in excess

Worked example

Calculate the pH when 23.00 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 HCl is mixed with 20.10 cm3 of 0.098 mol dm−3 NaOH.

1
mol H+ = 0.02300 × 0.100 = 2.30 × 10−3   mol OH− = 0.02010 × 0.098 = 1.97 × 10−3
2
excess H+ = 2.30 × 10−3 − 1.97 × 10−3 = 3.30 × 10−4 mol
3
Total volume = 23.00 + 20.10 = 43.10 cm3 = 0.04310 dm3
4
[H+] = 3.30 × 10−4 ÷ 0.04310 = 7.66 × 10−3 mol dm−3
5

pH = 2.12

[H+] = 3.30 × 10−4 ÷ 0.02300

That is only the volume of the acid. The excess H+ is spread through the total volume, 43.10 cm3.

Base in excess

The first three steps are the same. There is one extra step at the end: the excess is OH−, so use Kw to get [H+].

Worked example

Calculate the pH when 25.0 cm3 of 0.110 mol dm−3 NaOH is added to 50.0 cm3 of 0.040 mol dm−3 HCl.

1
mol OH− = 0.0250 × 0.110 = 2.75 × 10−3   mol H+ = 0.0500 × 0.040 = 2.00 × 10−3
2
excess OH− = 7.50 × 10−4 mol
3
Total volume = 75.0 cm3. [OH−] = 7.50 × 10−4 ÷ 0.0750 = 0.0100 mol dm−3
4
Extra step: [H+] = (1.00 × 10−14) ÷ 0.0100 = 1.00 × 10−12 mol dm−3
5

pH = 12.00

CHECK 8

Calculate the pH when 25.0 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 HCl is mixed with 28.54 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 NaOH.

mol H+ = 2.50 × 10−3   mol OH− = 2.85 × 10−3
excess OH− = 3.54 × 10−4 mol in 53.54 cm3
[OH−] = 3.54 × 10−4 ÷ 0.0535 = 6.61 × 10−3 mol dm−3

pH = 11.82

Spot the error, then exam-style questions

Each line contains one mistake. Find it.

25.0 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 H2SO4: mol H+ = 0.0250 × 0.100

H2SO4 is diprotic. mol H+ = 2 × 0.0250 × 0.100 = 5.00 × 10−3.

excess OH− = 5.0 × 10−4 mol in 50 cm3: pH = −log(0.010) = 2.00

The excess is OH−, so 0.010 is [OH−]. [H+] = 10−14 ÷ 0.010 = 10−12, pH = 12.00.

25.0 cm3 acid + 30.0 cm3 alkali: volume = 55.0 dm3

Convert to dm3: 55.0 cm3 = 0.0550 dm3.

At 30 °C: [H+] = (1.00 × 10−14) ÷ [OH−]

Kw is only 1.00 × 10−14 at 25 °C. At another temperature use the Kw given in the question.
