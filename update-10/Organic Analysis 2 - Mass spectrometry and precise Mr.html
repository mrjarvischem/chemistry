Organic Analysis 2 – Mass spectrometry and precise Mr

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.3.6 Organic analysis · walkthrough 2

1. The molecular ion

  • In a mass spectrum of an organic compound, the peak with the highest m/z (ignoring small isotope peaks) is usually the molecular ion, M⁺. Its m/z gives the Mr.
  • Chlorine has isotopes ³⁵Cl : ³⁷Cl ≈ 3 : 1, so a compound with one Cl shows two molecular-ion peaks, M and M+2, in a 3 : 1 ratio.
  • Bromine: ⁷⁹Br : ⁸¹Br ≈ 1 : 1, so M and M+2 are equal in height.

M1 lines at m/z = 134 and 136 · M2 the line at 134 is three times higher than the line at 136.

2. High-resolution mass spectrometry

Low-resolution Mr values are whole numbers, so different molecular formulae can share the same value (e.g. C₃H₆O and C₄H₁₀ are both 58). High-resolution MS measures Mr to 4 or 5 decimal places. Because precise atomic masses are not whole numbers, compounds with different molecular formulae have different precise Mr.

Isomers (same molecular formula) have the same precise Mr, so high-resolution MS can't tell them apart.

3. Exam questions

EXAM 2AQA AS Paper 2, June 2023, Q01.3

Precise Mr values from high-resolution MS: C₅H₁₂ = 72.1416; C₅H₁₀ = 70.1260. Use these data to find the Ar of hydrogen and carbon (4 decimal places). Use them to find the Mr of C₆H₆ (4 d.p.). [3 marks]

Ar(H) = Ar(C) = Mr(C₆H₆) =
M1 the difference is 2H: (72.1416 − 70.1260) ÷ 2 = 1.0078 · M2 C = (72.1416 − 12 × 1.0078) ÷ 5 = 12.0096 · M3 C₆H₆ = 6 × 12.0096 + 6 × 1.0078 = 78.1044
EXAM 3AQA AS Paper 2, June 2025, Q05.1

Propanone, prop-2-en-1-ol and butane each have Mr = 58.0. Give the reason why high-resolution mass spectrometry can distinguish between propanone and butane, but cannot distinguish between propanone and prop-2-en-1-ol. [2 marks]

M1 butane and propanone have different Mr to several decimal places · M2 prop-2-en-1-ol and propanone have the same molecular formula (C₃H₆O) / are isomers.
