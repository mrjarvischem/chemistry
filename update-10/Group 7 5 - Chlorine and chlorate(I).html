1. Chlorine and water

Chlorine reacts with water in a reversible reaction:

Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO

HClO is chloric(I) acid. Chlorine goes from 0 to −1 (in HCl) and to +1 (in HClO) – it is both reduced and oxidised. This is a disproportionation reaction.

In sunlight, chlorine water decomposes and gives off oxygen:

2Cl₂ + 2H₂O → 4HCl + O₂

2. Chlorine in water treatment

Chlorine is added to drinking water and swimming pools to kill bacteria (and other microorganisms), preventing diseases such as cholera. It also stops algae growing and keeps killing bacteria as the water travels through pipes.

Benefits

  • Kills disease-causing microorganisms
  • Long-lasting effect in the pipes
  • Cheap and effective

Risks

  • Chlorine is a toxic gas – the amount added must be controlled
  • It can react with organic compounds in water to form chlorinated hydrocarbons, which may cause cancer
Why use it if it's toxic? The benefit to health (preventing deadly waterborne diseases) outweighs the risk, because only very low concentrations are used.

3. Chlorine and sodium hydroxide – bleach

Chlorine reacts with cold, dilute sodium hydroxide to make sodium chlorate(I), NaClO – the active ingredient in household bleach:

Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O

Another disproportionation: Cl goes from 0 to −1 (NaCl) and +1 (NaClO). Bleach is used for cleaning, disinfecting and bleaching paper and textiles.

4. How much NaClO is in bleach?

EXAMAQA A-level Paper 1, 2020, Q08.1

A student dilutes a 10.0 cm³ sample of bleach to 100 cm³. 25.0 cm³ of the diluted bleach is acidified and excess KI added, forming I₂. The I₂ is titrated with 0.100 mol dm−³ Na₂S₂O₃: 33.50 cm³ is needed. The density of the original bleach is 1.20 g cm−³.

ClO− + 2H+ + 2I− → Cl− + H₂O + I₂    2S₂O₃2− + I₂ → 2I− + S₄O₆2−

Calculate the percentage by mass of NaClO in the original bleach, to 3 significant figures. [7 marks]

%

mol S₂O₃2− = 33.50/1000 × 0.100 = 3.35 × 10−³
mol I₂ = 3.35 × 10−³ ÷ 2 = 1.675 × 10−³ = mol ClO− in 25.0 cm³
mol ClO− in 100 cm³ (= in the 10.0 cm³ original) = 1.675 × 10−³ × 4 = 6.70 × 10−³
mass NaClO = 6.70 × 10−³ × 74.5 = 0.499 g
mass of 10.0 cm³ bleach = 10.0 × 1.20 = 12.0 g
% NaClO = 0.499 ÷ 12.0 × 100 = 4.16%
