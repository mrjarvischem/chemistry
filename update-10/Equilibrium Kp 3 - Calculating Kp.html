Equilibrium (Kp) 3 – Calculating Kp

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibrium constant Kp walkthrough

1. Calculating Kp from partial pressures

If you are given the partial pressures, there are three steps:

  1. Write the Kp expression.
  2. Substitute the partial pressures, with their powers.
  3. Work out the units from the powers.

Worked example with the mark scheme

June 2018 Paper 1 Q2.3 (2 marks)

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g). Partial pressures: N₂ = 1.20 × 10² kPa, H₂ = 1.50 × 10² kPa, NH₃ = 1.10 × 10³ kPa. Calculate Kp and give its units.

  1. Kp = p(NH₃)²p(N₂) × p(H₂)³
  2. = (1.10 × 10³)²(1.20 × 10²) × (1.50 × 10²)³ = 1.21 × 10⁶4.05 × 10⁸
  3. M1 Kp = 2.99 × 10⁻³ (allow 2.9 to 3.0 × 10⁻³)
  4. M2 2 − 4 = −2 → kPa⁻²

Interactive: see how each pressure affects Kp

For N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃. Notice that p(H₂) is cubed, so it has the biggest effect.

QUICK CHECK 1 November 2021 Paper 1 Q5.3 (3 marks)

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). Partial pressures: SO₂ = 1.67 × 10² kPa, O₂ = 1.02 × 10² kPa, SO₃ = 1.85 × 10² kPa. Calculate Kp and give its units.

Units:
Kp = p(SO₃)²p(SO₂)² × p(O₂) = 185²167² × 102 = 1.20 × 10⁻² kPa⁻¹
The mark scheme penalises square brackets in the expression.

2. Calculating Kp from mole fractions

If you are given mole fractions and the total pressure, first turn each mole fraction into a partial pressure:

p(A) = x(A) × Ptotal

Then substitute into Kp as before.

QUICK CHECK 2 June 2024 Paper 1 Q4.4 (4 marks)

C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g) at a total pressure of 6000 kPa.

GasMole fraction
Ethene0.645
Steam0.323
Ethanol0.0321

Give an expression for Kp. Calculate the value of Kp and state its units.

Units:
M1 Kp = p(C₂H₅OH)p(C₂H₄) × p(H₂O)
M2 = 0.0321 × 6000(0.645 × 6000) × (0.323 × 6000) = 192.63870 × 1938
M3 Kp = 2.56 × 10⁻⁵ (allow 2.57 × 10⁻⁵)   M4 kPa⁻¹

A shortcut you can use to check

Because every p = x × P, you can pull out the total pressure:

Kp = (expression in mole fractions) × PΔn
Ethanol example: 0.03210.645 × 0.323 × 6000⁻¹ = 0.154 × 1.667 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.57 × 10⁻⁵ kPa⁻¹ ✔

You will meet this again in Walkthrough 6: it explains why changing the pressure moves the equilibrium but leaves Kp unchanged.

3. The full calculation: from equilibrium moles

  1. Add up the total moles of gas.
  2. Find each mole fraction.
  3. Multiply by the total pressure to get each partial pressure.
  4. Write Kp, substitute and calculate.
  5. Give the units.

Worked example with the mark scheme

June 2017 Paper 1 Q9.3 (6 marks)

CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g). At equilibrium at 600 K the mixture contained 2.76 mol CO, 4.51 mol H₂ and 0.360 mol CH₃OH. The total pressure was 630 kPa. Calculate Kp and state its units.

  1. Total moles = 2.76 + 4.51 + 0.360 = 7.63 mol
  2. p(CO) = 2.767.63 × 630 = 228 kPa
  3. p(H₂) = 4.517.63 × 630 = 372 kPa
  4. p(CH₃OH) = 0.3607.63 × 630 = 29.7 kPa (all three partial pressures = 2 marks; two correct = 1 mark)
  5. Kp = p(CH₃OH)p(CO) × p(H₂)² = 29.7228 × 372² = 9.41 × 10⁻⁷ (allow 9.39–9.50 × 10⁻⁷)
  6. Units: 1 − 3 = −2 → kPa⁻² (or 9.41 × 10⁻¹³ Pa⁻² if you used 630 000 Pa)
Common mistakes: putting moles straight into Kp; dividing by a volume (Kp never needs one); forgetting the square on p(H₂).
