Equilibrium (Kc) 8 – Effect of Conditions on Kc and Position

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibria walkthrough

1. Position of equilibrium v the value of Kc

Only a change in temperature changes the value of Kc.

ChangePosition of equilibriumValue of Kc
Concentrationshiftsno change
Pressure (or volume)shifts if gas moles differno change
Catalystno shiftno change
Temperatureshiftschanges

So the position can move while Kc stays the same. How? The mixture changes its amounts until the Kc expression gives the same number again.

2. Why changing concentration doesn't change Kc

A + B ⇌ C    Kc = [C][A] [B] = 5.0 mol⁻¹ dm³

The lower graph shows the value of the fraction [C] / ([A][B]) at every moment. Add some A and watch it.

[A][B][C]
  1. Adding A makes the bottom of the fraction bigger, so the fraction drops below Kc.
  2. The mixture is no longer at equilibrium. The forward reaction is now faster, making more C and using up A and B.
  3. This continues until the fraction is back to 5.0. The position has moved right, but Kc is the same.

3. Why changing pressure or volume doesn't change Kc

Write each concentration as n / V (Walkthrough 6):

Kc = (ratio of moles) × 1Vn    n = (powers on top) − (powers on bottom)

If V changes, Kc can't, so the ratio of moles must change to make up for it. That is the shift in position.

QUICK CHECK 2 June 2024 Paper 2 Q2.3 (2 marks)

2P(aq) + Q(aq) ⇌ R(aq) + 3S(aq). Explain why the amount of S increases when water is added to the equilibrium mixture.

Either (Le Chatelier):
M1 the equilibrium shifts to the side with more moles (4 on the right v 3 on the left)
M2 to oppose the decrease in concentration of all the reactants and products (dilution)

Or (Kc):
M1 Kc = (nR nS³ / nP² nQ) × 1/V, since concentration = amount / volume
M2 if V increases, the amounts of R and S must increase to keep Kc constant
QUICK CHECK 3 November 2020 AS Paper 2 Q8.3 (final mark)

C₂H₅OH(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ 2CO(g) + 4H₂(g). The pressure is increased at constant temperature. Predict the effect on the value of Kc.

The yield of H₂ falls, but Kc doesn't change. The temperature is constant.

4. Temperature: the one thing that changes Kc

A temperature change shifts the position, and the new equilibrium mixture gives a different value of Kc.

  • Shift to the right → more products on top → Kc increases.
  • Shift to the left → Kc decreases.
Forward reactionIncrease TDecrease T
exothermic (ΔH −)Kc decreasesKc increases
endothermic (ΔH +)Kc increasesKc decreases
