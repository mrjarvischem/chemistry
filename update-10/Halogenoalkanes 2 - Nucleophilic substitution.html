Halogenoalkanes – Nucleophilic Substitution

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.3.3 Halogenoalkanes · walkthrough 2

1. Nucleophilic substitution

A nucleophile is an electron pair donor. It has a lone pair (and often a negative charge): OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃.

The nucleophile attacks the δ+ carbon and replaces (substitutes) the halogen, which leaves as a halide ion X⁻.

Curly arrow rules (AQA penalises these):
  • An arrow shows a pair of electrons moving. It starts at a lone pair or a bond, and ends at an atom (or where a new bond forms).
  • Arrow 1 starts on the lone pair of the nucleophile – show the lone pair and the charge.
  • Arrow 2 goes from the middle of the C–X bond to X.
  • Show δ+ and δ− correctly – never a full + or − on the C or the X of the C–X bond.

2. OH⁻, CN⁻ and NH₃

OH⁻NaOH or KOH, aqueous, warm (reflux)alcoholCH₃CH₂Br + OH⁻ → CH₃CH₂OH + Br⁻
CN⁻KCN in aqueous ethanol, refluxnitrile (chain gets one C longer)CH₃CH₂Br + CN⁻ → CH₃CH₂CN + Br⁻ (propanenitrile)
NH₃excess ammonia in ethanol, heated in a sealed tubeprimary amineCH₃CH₂Br + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Br

With ammonia, a second NH₃ removes H⁺ from the intermediate – so 2 NH₃ in the overall equation. Excess NH₃ stops the amine reacting further.

Naming nitriles: count all the carbons including the C of C≡N. CH₃CH₂CN has 3 C → propanenitrile.

4. Which halogenoalkane reacts fastest?

The rate depends on the C–X bond enthalpy, not the polarity. The C–I bond is the weakest, so it breaks most easily → iodoalkanes react fastest, fluoroalkanes slowest.

The silver nitrate test

Equal amounts of 1-chlorobutane, 1-bromobutane and 1-iodobutane are warmed with aqueous silver nitrate in ethanol. Water is the nucleophile; the halide ion released forms a precipitate: Ag⁺(aq) + X⁻(aq) → AgX(s).

Identical concentrations of 1-bromobutane and 1-iodobutane were reacted separately with NaOH under the same conditions, and the concentration of halide ions was monitored. The graph for 1-iodobutane is steeper at the start. State how the graph shows that 1-iodobutane reacts faster, and state why the rates are different. [2 marks]

The gradient (at the start) is steeper for 1-iodobutane / the halide concentration rises faster (M1).
The C–I bond is weaker / has a lower bond enthalpy than C–Br, so it breaks more easily (M2).
