Hydrolysis of Esters, Fats and Oils

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.3.9.1 Carboxylic acids and esters · walkthrough

1. What hydrolysis does to an ester

Every ester has the same link in the middle: R–COO–R′.

  • The R–C=O part came from a carboxylic acid.
  • The O–R′ part came from an alcohol. Circle it: this is the bit that leaves as the alcohol.

Hydrolysis = using water to split a molecule.

The bond that breaks is the C–O single bond between the C=O carbon and the O–R′ oxygen. Water adds back on: the OH goes to the C=O carbon, the H goes to the O–R′.

2. The two conditions: acid or alkali

Both are done by heating under reflux. Either way, it's the water that splits the ester. The acid or alkali just sets the conditions.

Dilute acid, e.g. HCl(aq)

  • H⁺ is a catalyst.
  • Products: carboxylic acid + alcohol.
  • Reversible (⇌): it's the reverse of esterification, so it never goes to completion.

Alkali, e.g. NaOH(aq)

  • OH⁻ is used up, so it's a reagent.
  • Products: carboxylate salt + alcohol.
  • Goes to completion (→): the carboxylate ion can't react with the alcohol, so it's better: higher yield.

To get the carboxylic acid after alkaline hydrolysis, add a strong acid: RCOONa + HCl → RCOOH + NaCl.

QUICK CHECK 3

Why does alkaline hydrolysis give a higher yield than acid hydrolysis?

Acid hydrolysis is reversible, so an equilibrium forms. With alkali the acid is turned into a carboxylate ion/salt, which can't react with the alcohol to re-form the ester, so the reaction goes to completion.

3. Fats and oils are triesters

  • Fats and vegetable oils are esters of propane-1,2,3-triol (glycerol), which has three OH groups.
  • Each OH is joined to a long-chain carboxylic acid (a fatty acid), so there are three ester links.
  • Hydrolysis splits all three links. You always get glycerol back, plus three chain products, and you need 3 moles of the reagent.

4. The three chains don't have to be the same

Big exam trap. In a real fat or oil, the three fatty acid chains are often different. Each chain comes off with its own formula, so you can get 2 of one product and 1 of another, or 3 different ones. Always split each ester link separately.

Build a triester, then split it

Chains with the same formula are drawn in the same colour.

Working out Mr of a triester

The core (3 × COO plus C₃H₅) is always C₆H₅O₆ = 173. Then add the three chains:

Mr(triester) = 173 + Mr(R₁) + Mr(R₂) + Mr(R₃)

For an alkyl chain CH₃(CH₂)ₙ, Mr = 15 + 14n.

Coconut oil contains a triester with three identical R groups. This triester reacts with potassium hydroxide.

(RCOO)₃C₃H₅  + 3KOH ⟶ 3RCOOK +  ? 

1.1 Complete the equation by drawing the structure of the other product. Name the type of compound shown by the formula RCOOK. Give one use for this type of compound. [3 marks]

M1 CH₂OHCH(OH)CH₂OH
M2 (potassium) carboxylate salt (allow fatty acid salt / salt of a carboxylic acid)
M3 soap (allow detergent / surfactant)

1.2 The triester has Mr = 638.0. R can be written as CH₃(CH₂)ₙ. Deduce the value of n. Show your working. [3 marks]

M1 Mr of the ester core = 173
M2 638 = 173 + 3(15 + 14n), so 3(15 + 14n) = 465 and 15 + 14n = 155
M3 14n = 140, so n = 10 (n must be an integer)

EXAM QUESTION 5 – the practical June 2017 Paper 3 Q3.2–3.6 (6 marks)

Ethyl benzoate is hydrolysed in alkaline conditions. A student adds 5.0 cm³ of ethyl benzoate (density = 1.05 g cm⁻³, Mr = 150) to 30.0 cm³ of 2 mol dm⁻³ NaOH(aq), heats under reflux for half an hour, cools, then adds 50.0 cm³ of 2 mol dm⁻³ HCl. Benzoic acid precipitates.

3.2 Show, by calculation, that an excess of sodium hydroxide is used. [2 marks]  

M1 mass of ester = 1.05 × 5.0 = 5.25 g; amount = 5.25 ÷ 150 = 0.0350 mol
M2 amount of NaOH = 30.0 × 2 ÷ 1000 = 0.0600 mol, which is more than 0.0350 mol

3.3 Suggest why an excess of sodium hydroxide is used. [1 mark]  

To make sure all the ester is hydrolysed / reacts.

3.4 Suggest why an electric heater is used rather than a Bunsen burner. [1 mark]  

The ester / ethanol (organic compounds) are flammable.

3.5 State why reflux is used. [1 mark]  

Vapours of the reactants condense and return to the flask, so none escape while it's heated.

3.6 Write an equation for the reaction between sodium benzoate and hydrochloric acid. [1 mark]  

C₆H₅COONa + HCl → C₆H₅COOH + NaCl (ionic equation allowed)
