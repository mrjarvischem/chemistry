Buffer calculations

Calculating the pH of a buffer made from an acid and its salt or from an acid and NaOH, and the new pH after acid or alkali is added.

One equation for every buffer question

Ka = [H+][A−][HA]    rearranges to    [H+] = Ka × [HA][A−]

  • [HA] is the weak acid. [A−] comes from the salt.
  • HA and A− are in the same solution, so the volume cancels. You can use moles in place of concentrations:

[H+] = Ka × mol HAmol A−

Do not use [H+] = √(Ka × [HA]) for a buffer. That only works for a weak acid on its own, where [H+] = [A−]. In a buffer most of the A− comes from the salt.

Every question follows the same plan

  1. Find the moles of HA and the moles of A− in the final mixture.
  2. Put them into [H+] = Ka × mol HA ÷ mol A−.
  3. pH = −log[H+].

The only thing that changes between question types is how you find the two mole values.

CHECK 1

A buffer contains 0.200 mol of ethanoic acid and 0.100 mol of sodium ethanoate. Calculate its pH. Ka = 1.74 × 10−5 mol dm−3

[H+] = 1.74 × 10−5 × (0.200 ÷ 0.100) = 3.48 × 10−5 mol dm−3
pH = 4.46

CHECK 2

A buffer is made by mixing 25 cm3 of HA with 50 cm3 of NaA. Both solutions have the same concentration. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.60 × 10−5 mol dm−3

Same concentration, so mol HA : mol A− = 25 : 50 = 1 : 2
[H+] = 1.60 × 10−5 × ½ = 8.00 × 10−6 mol dm−3
pH = 5.10

Type 1: weak acid + its salt

Both mole values come straight from the question: n = c × V for solutions, or n = mass ÷ Mr for a solid salt.

Worked example

25.0 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid is mixed with 30.0 cm3 of 0.080 mol dm−3 sodium ethanoate. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.76 × 10−5 mol dm−3

1
mol HA = 0.0250 × 0.100 = 2.50 × 10−3
2
mol A− = 0.0300 × 0.080 = 2.40 × 10−3
3
[H+] = 1.76 × 10−5 × 2.50 × 10−32.40 × 10−3 = 1.83 × 10−5 mol dm−3
4

pH = 4.74

CHECK 3

30.0 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 propanoic acid is mixed with 25.0 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 sodium propanoate. Calculate the pH. pKa = 4.88

Ka = 10−4.88 = 1.32 × 10−5 mol dm−3
mol HA = 3.00 × 10−3   mol A− = 2.50 × 10−3
[H+] = 1.32 × 10−5 × (3.00 ÷ 2.50) = 1.58 × 10−5 mol dm−3
pH = 4.80

CHECK 4

0.580 g of sodium ethanoate (Mr = 82.0) is dissolved in 50.0 cm3 of 0.080 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.76 × 10−5 mol dm−3

mol A− = 0.580 ÷ 82.0 = 7.07 × 10−3
mol HA = 0.0500 × 0.080 = 4.00 × 10−3
[H+] = 1.76 × 10−5 × (4.00 × 10−3 ÷ 7.07 × 10−3) = 9.95 × 10−6 mol dm−3
pH = 5.00

CHECK 5

1.50 g of sodium propanoate (Mr = 96.0) is dissolved in 1.00 dm3 of 0.200 mol dm−3 propanoic acid. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.34 × 10−5 mol dm−3

mol A− = 1.50 ÷ 96.0 = 0.0156   mol HA = 0.200
[H+] = 1.34 × 10−5 × (0.200 ÷ 0.0156) = 1.72 × 10−4 mol dm−3
pH = 3.77

Type 2: excess weak acid + NaOH

HA + NaOH → NaA + H2O

  • All of the NaOH reacts (the acid is in excess).
  • mol A− formed = mol NaOH added
  • mol HA left = mol HA at start − mol NaOH added

Worked example

25.0 cm3 of 0.050 mol dm−3 NaOH is added to 25.0 cm3 of 0.080 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.76 × 10−5 mol dm−3

1
mol HA at start = 0.0250 × 0.080 = 2.00 × 10−3
2
mol NaOH = 0.0250 × 0.050 = 1.25 × 10−3
3
mol A− formed = 1.25 × 10−3
4
mol HA left = 2.00 × 10−3 − 1.25 × 10−3 = 7.5 × 10−4
5
[H+] = 1.76 × 10−5 × 7.5 × 10−41.25 × 10−3 = 1.06 × 10−5 mol dm−3
6

pH = 4.98

mol HA left = 7.5 × 10−4 in 50 cm3, so [HA] = 0.015 and [H+] = √(Ka × 0.015)

This treats the mixture as a weak acid on its own. It also contains 1.25 × 10−3 mol of A−, so it is a buffer. Use [H+] = Ka × mol HA ÷ mol A−. (The wrong method gives pH 3.29. The correct answer is 4.98.)

CHECK 6

25.0 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 NaOH is added to 100.0 cm3 of 0.110 mol dm−3 propanoic acid. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.34 × 10−5 mol dm−3

mol HA at start = 0.0110   mol NaOH = 2.50 × 10−3 = mol A−
mol HA left = 0.0110 − 0.0025 = 0.0085
[H+] = 1.34 × 10−5 × (0.0085 ÷ 0.0025) = 4.56 × 10−5 mol dm−3
pH = 4.34

CHECK 7

40.0 cm3 of 0.125 mol dm−3 NaOH is added to 100.0 cm3 of 0.150 mol dm−3 methanoic acid. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.78 × 10−4 mol dm−3

mol HA at start = 0.0150   mol NaOH = 5.00 × 10−3 = mol A−
mol HA left = 0.0100
[H+] = 1.78 × 10−4 × (0.0100 ÷ 0.00500) = 3.56 × 10−4 mol dm−3
pH = 3.45

CHECK 8

20.0 cm3 of 0.250 mol dm−3 NaOH is added to 50.0 cm3 of 0.200 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.74 × 10−5 mol dm−3

mol HA at start = 0.0100   mol NaOH = 5.00 × 10−3
mol HA left = mol A− = 5.00 × 10−3 (half-neutralised)
[H+] = Ka, so pH = pKa = 4.76

Adding acid or alkali to a buffer

Acid added

A− + H+ → HA

mol HA goes up and mol A− goes down by the moles of H+ added.

Remember: acid adds to acid.

Alkali added

HA + OH− → A− + H2O

mol HA goes down and mol A− goes up by the moles of OH− added.

Worked example: adding acid

50.0 cm3 of 0.120 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid is mixed with 45.0 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 sodium ethanoate. Then 5.00 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 HCl is added. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.76 × 10−5 mol dm−3

pH = 4.54

CHECK 9

A buffer contains 0.0500 mol of ethanoic acid and 0.0400 mol of sodium ethanoate. Calculate its pH. Ka = 1.74 × 10−5 mol dm−3

[H+] = 1.74 × 10−5 × (0.0500 ÷ 0.0400) = 2.18 × 10−5   pH = 4.66

CHECK 10

2.00 × 10−3 mol of HCl is added to that buffer. Calculate the new pH.

mol HA = 0.0520   mol A− = 0.0380
[H+] = 1.74 × 10−5 × (0.0520 ÷ 0.0380) = 2.38 × 10−5   pH = 4.62

CHECK 11

Instead, 2.00 × 10−3 mol of NaOH is added to the original buffer. Calculate the new pH.

mol HA = 0.0480   mol A− = 0.0420
[H+] = 1.74 × 10−5 × (0.0480 ÷ 0.0420) = 1.99 × 10−5   pH = 4.70

CHECK 12

A buffer is made by adding 0.0136 mol of the salt KX to 100 cm3 of 0.500 mol dm−3 HX. Then 3.00 × 10−4 mol of KOH is added. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.41 × 10−5 mol dm−3

Start: mol HX = 0.0500   mol X− = 0.0136
After KOH: mol HX = 0.0497   mol X− = 0.0139
[H+] = 1.41 × 10−5 × (0.0497 ÷ 0.0139) = 5.04 × 10−5 mol dm−3
pH = 4.30

CHECK 13

A buffer contains 0.0250 mol of sodium ethanoate in 500 cm3 of 0.0700 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid. 5.00 cm3 of 2.00 mol dm−3 HCl is added. Calculate the pH. Ka = 1.76 × 10−5 mol dm−3

Start: mol HA = 0.0350   mol A− = 0.0250   mol HCl = 0.0100
After HCl: mol HA = 0.0450   mol A− = 0.0150
[H+] = 1.76 × 10−5 × (0.0450 ÷ 0.0150) = 5.28 × 10−5 mol dm−3
pH = 4.28

Working backwards from a pH

If the pH is given, use it to find the ratio first: mol HA ÷ mol A− = [H+] ÷ Ka.

Finding a concentration

A buffer with a pH of 3.87 contains ethanoic acid and sodium ethanoate. [CH3COO−] = 0.136 mol dm−3. Calculate [CH3COOH]. Ka = 1.74 × 10−5 mol dm−3

1
[H+] = 10−3.87 = 1.35 × 10−4 mol dm−3
2
Rearrange Ka:   [HA] = [H+][A−]Ka
3
[HA] = (1.35 × 10−4 × 0.136) ÷ (1.74 × 10−5)

[HA] = 1.05 mol dm−3

CHECK 14

Calculate the mass of sodium ethanoate (Mr = 82.0) that must be dissolved in 500 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid to make a buffer with a pH of 4.50. Ka = 1.74 × 10−5 mol dm−3

[H+] = 10−4.50 = 3.16 × 10−5 mol dm−3
mol HA ÷ mol A− = [H+] ÷ Ka = 1.82
mol HA = 0.0500, so mol A− = 0.0500 ÷ 1.82 = 0.0275
mass = 0.0275 × 82.0 = 2.26 g

CHECK 15

Calculate the volume of 0.100 mol dm−3 NaOH that must be added to 25.0 cm3 of 0.200 mol dm−3 ethanoic acid to make a buffer with a pH of 5.00. Ka = 1.74 × 10−5 mol dm−3

[H+] = 1.00 × 10−5, so mol HA ÷ mol A− = 0.575
Let mol NaOH = x. Then mol A− = x and mol HA = 5.00 × 10−3 − x
(5.00 × 10−3 − x) ÷ x = 0.575, so x = 5.00 × 10−3 ÷ 1.57 = 3.18 × 10−3 mol
volume = 3.18 × 10−3 ÷ 0.100 = 31.8 cm3
