pH of strong bases

Using Kw to find the pH of a strong base from a concentration or a mass, and working backwards from a pH.

Strong bases and the three-step method

Strong base

A base that is fully dissociated in water

One OH− per formula

NaOH → Na+ + OH−

[OH−] = [NaOH]

Also KOH and LiOH.

Two OH− per formula

Ba(OH)2 → Ba2+ + 2OH−

[OH−] = 2 × [Ba(OH)2]

Also Ca(OH)2 and Sr(OH)2.

The method

  1. Find [OH−] from the concentration of the base.
  2. Use Kw to find [H+]:   [H+] = Kw ÷ [OH−]
  3. pH = −log[H+]

Use Kw = 1.00 × 10−14 mol2 dm−6 unless the question gives a different value.

CHECK 1

What is [OH−] in 0.10 mol dm−3 NaOH?

NaOH is fully dissociated, 1 OH− each: 0.10 mol dm−3

CHECK 2

What is [OH−] in 0.10 mol dm−3 Ba(OH)2?

Each Ba(OH)2 gives 2 OH−: 2 × 0.10 = 0.20 mol dm−3

pH from a concentration

Worked example

Calculate the pH of 0.330 mol dm−3 NaOH at 25 °C.

1
NaOH is fully dissociated, so [OH−] = 0.330 mol dm−3
2
[H+] = Kw ÷ [OH−] = (1.00 × 10−14) ÷ 0.330 = 3.03 × 10−14 mol dm−3
3
pH = −log(3.03 × 10−14)

pH = 13.52

pH of 0.330 mol dm−3 NaOH = −log(0.330) = 0.48

0.330 is [OH−], not [H+]. A pH of 0.48 would be a strong acid. Use Kw to convert first.

CHECK 3

[OH−] = 0.120   [H+] = 8.33 × 10−14 mol dm−3
pH = 13.08

pH = 13.34

pH from a mass

If the question gives a mass and a volume, find the concentration of the base first: n = mass ÷ Mr, then c = n ÷ V (in dm3).

Worked example: LiOH

0.54 g of LiOH (Mr = 23.9) is dissolved to make 500 cm3 of solution. Calculate the pH.

1
n = 0.54 ÷ 23.9 = 0.0226 mol
2
[OH−] = 0.0226 ÷ 0.500 = 0.0452 mol dm−3
3
[H+] = (1.00 × 10−14) ÷ 0.0452 = 2.21 × 10−13 mol dm−3
4

pH = 12.66

Worked example: Ba(OH)2

1.185 g of Ba(OH)2 (Mr = 171.3) is dissolved to make 280 cm3 of solution. Calculate the pH.

1
n = 1.185 ÷ 171.3 = 6.92 × 10−3 mol
2
[Ba(OH)2] = 6.92 × 10−3 ÷ 0.280 = 0.0247 mol dm−3
3
× 2: [OH−] = 0.0494 mol dm−3
4
[H+] = 2.02 × 10−13 mol dm−3

pH = 12.69

CHECK 7

n = 0.0267 mol   [OH−] = 0.134 mol dm−3
[H+] = 7.48 × 10−14 mol dm−3   pH = 13.13

1.89 g of KOH (Mr = 56.1) is dissolved to make 250 cm3 of solution. Calculate the pH.

n = 0.0337 mol   [OH−] = 0.135 mol dm−3
pH = 13.13

n = 9.28 × 10−3 mol   [Ba(OH)2] = 0.0928 mol dm−3
[OH−] = 0.186 mol dm−3   pH = 13.27

Working backwards from a pH

Run the method in reverse: pH → [H+] → [OH−] → concentration of base → moles → mass.

Worked example

Calculate the mass of NaOH needed to make 0.50 dm3 of solution with a pH of 12.89 at 293 K. Kw = 6.80 × 10−15 mol2 dm−6

1
[H+] = 10−12.89 = 1.288 × 10−13 mol dm−3
2
[OH−] = Kw ÷ [H+] = (6.80 × 10−15) ÷ (1.288 × 10−13) = 0.0528 mol dm−3
3
n(NaOH) = 0.0528 × 0.50 = 0.0264 mol
4
mass = 0.0264 × 40.0

mass = 1.06 g

For Ba(OH)2, Ca(OH)2 and Sr(OH)2 remember to halve [OH−] to get the concentration of the base.

CHECK 10

A solution of NaOH has a pH of 12.70 at 25 °C. Calculate its concentration.

[H+] = 10−12.70 = 1.995 × 10−13 mol dm−3
[OH−] = (1.00 × 10−14) ÷ (1.995 × 10−13) = 0.0501 mol dm−3 = [NaOH]

CHECK 11

Calculate the mass of Ba(OH)2 (Mr = 171.3) needed to make 200 cm3 of solution with a pH of 13.50 at 293 K. Kw = 6.80 × 10−15 mol2 dm−6

[H+] = 3.162 × 10−14   [OH−] = 0.215 mol dm−3
[Ba(OH)2] = 0.215 ÷ 2 = 0.108 mol dm−3
n = 0.108 × 0.200 = 0.0215 mol
mass = 0.0215 × 171.3 = 3.68 g

Other temperatures and dilutions

A different Kw

A saturated solution of Ca(OH)2 (Mr = 74.1) contains 0.400 g dm−3 at 293 K. Calculate its pH. Kw = 6.80 × 10−15 mol2 dm−6

1
[Ca(OH)2] = 0.400 ÷ 74.1 = 5.40 × 10−3 mol dm−3
2
[OH−] = 2 × 5.40 × 10−3 = 0.0108 mol dm−3
3
Use the Kw given: [H+] = (6.80 × 10−15) ÷ 0.0108 = 6.30 × 10−13 mol dm−3
4

pH = 12.20

Diluting a base

20.0 cm3 of 0.180 mol dm−3 Sr(OH)2 is made up to 250 cm3 with distilled water. Calculate the pH at 25 °C.

1
[Sr(OH)2] = 0.180 × (20.0 ÷ 250) = 0.0144 mol dm−3
2
[OH−] = 2 × 0.0144 = 0.0288 mol dm−3
3
[H+] = 3.47 × 10−13 mol dm−3
4

pH = 12.46

CHECK 12

At 50 °C, Kw = 5.47 × 10−14 mol2 dm−6. Calculate the pH of 0.0250 mol dm−3 Ba(OH)2 at 50 °C.

[OH−] = 0.0500   [H+] = (5.47 × 10−14) ÷ 0.0500 = 1.09 × 10−12 mol dm−3
pH = 11.96

CHECK 13

10.0 cm3 of 0.150 mol dm−3 NaOH is made up to 250 cm3. Calculate the pH at 25 °C.

[OH−] = 0.150 × (10.0 ÷ 250) = 6.00 × 10−3 mol dm−3
[H+] = 1.67 × 10−12 mol dm−3   pH = 11.78
