Section 1

What is an oxidation state?

Oxidation state: the charge an atom would have if all of its bonds were ionic.

In a covalent bond the electrons are shared. To find oxidation states we pretend every bond is ionic. The more electronegative atom takes both electrons from each bond it makes. Each electron it takes counts −1; each electron an atom gives away counts +1.

If both atoms are the same element, neither wins: the pair is split, one electron each, so there is no charge.

Pretend the bonds are ionic
Write the sign first for an oxidation state: +2, −3. Ion charges are written the other way round: Mg2+.
Section 2

The rules

You don't need to draw the bonds every time. Four rules and a short list of fixed values do the job.

① Uncombined element = 0

Na, O2, Cl2 and S8 are all 0. No bonds to a different element, so nothing to give away.

② Simple ion = its charge

Mg2+ is +2, Cl− is −1, Fe3+ is +3.

③ They add up to the overall charge

Compound: total = 0. H2O: 2(+1) + (−2) = 0.
Ion: total = its charge. OH−: (−2) + (+1) = −1.

④ More electronegative = negative

In HCl, Cl is −1 and H is +1. Cl would win the bonding electrons.

Working it out: compounds

Use the same four steps every time. For a neutral compound the total is zero.

Section 4

Inside a compound ion

For an ion such as SO42−, the oxidation states add up to the charge on the ion, not zero. You can see why by making the bonds ionic: every bond to oxygen takes electrons from the central atom, and a double bond counts twice.

Count the bonds to oxygen
Step through a worked example
The total is the charge on the ion. Setting SO42− equal to zero gives S = +8, which is impossible: sulfur only has six outer electrons.
Section 5

Exceptions: peroxides, hydrides and fluorine

Every exception comes straight from electronegativity. Make the bonds ionic and the odd values appear on their own.

Make the bonds ionic

Peroxides: O = −1

The O–O bond is shared equally, so each O only gains one electron (from H). Also Na2O2.

Metal hydrides: H = −1

H is more electronegative than a reactive metal, so it gains an electron: Na+ H−. NaH: (+1) + x = 0, so H = −1. Also CaH2.

With fluorine: O = +2

F always wins, so it is −1. OF2: x + 2(−1) = 0, so O = +2. Likewise Cl is +1 in ClF.

Naming with Roman numerals
