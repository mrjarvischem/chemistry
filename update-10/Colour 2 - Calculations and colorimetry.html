Section 1

Ground state and excited state

In a complex, the ligands split the d orbitals into two energy levels. The electrons sit in the lower level: this is the ground state. When a d electron absorbs a photon of visible light with exactly the right energy, it moves up to the higher level: an excited state.

Ti³⁺ (3d¹) in an octahedral complex: absorb a photon
The electron is in the ground state.
The four-mark answer. ① Ligands split the d orbitals into two energy levels. ② A d electron absorbs a photon of visible light. ③ It moves from the ground state to an excited state; the energy absorbed is ΔE = hν. ④ The remaining wavelengths are transmitted or reflected, and this is the colour we see.
Don’t write that electrons emit light (that’s flame tests), or that the complex absorbs its own colour.

Identifying ions by colour

Transition metal ions can be identified by their colour. The colour we see is the complementary colour of the light that is absorbed: the colour opposite it on the colour wheel.

ΔE = hν = hc/λ

The energy gap ΔE equals the energy of the photon absorbed. A bigger gap means a higher frequency (shorter wavelength) of light is absorbed.

Section 5

Colorimetry

The absorption of visible light is used in spectroscopy. A simple colorimeter measures how much light of one colour a solution absorbs, to find the concentration of a coloured ion.

Choose a filter and a concentration of copper(II) solution
Choosing the filter. Use the colour of light the solution absorbs – the complementary colour of the solution. Blue copper(II) solutions absorb orange/red, so an orange or red filter gives the largest, most sensitive reading.
Calibration curve: find the unknown
  1. Make standard solutions of known concentration.
  2. Measure the absorbance of each and plot absorbance against concentration.
  3. Measure the absorbance of the unknown and read its concentration off the graph.

Pale solutions give small readings. Adding a ligand that gives a deeper colour (e.g. NH3 with Cu2+) makes the measurement more sensitive.
