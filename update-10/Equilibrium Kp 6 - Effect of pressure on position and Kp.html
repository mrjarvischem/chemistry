Equilibrium (Kp) 6 – Effect of pressure on position and Kp

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibrium constant Kp walkthrough

1. Changing the total pressure

  • Increasing the pressure (for example, by squashing into a smaller volume) shifts the equilibrium towards the side with fewer moles of gas.
  • Decreasing the pressure shifts it towards the side with more moles of gas.
  • The value of Kp does not change. Only temperature changes Kp.

2. Why the position moves but Kp doesn't

Write each partial pressure as mole fraction × P:

Kp = p(NO₂)²p(N₂O₄) = x(NO₂)²x(N₂O₄) × P

Kp is a constant at a fixed temperature. If P is doubled, the mole fraction part must halve to keep Kp the same. That means less NO₂ and more N₂O₄: the equilibrium has shifted to the side with fewer moles of gas, exactly as Le Chatelier predicts.

3. Adding or removing one of the gases

If you add more of a reactant at constant temperature:

  1. Straight away, the bottom of the Kp expression gets bigger, so the ratio is now smaller than Kp.
  2. The reaction moves forward (to the right) until the ratio equals Kp again.
  3. More product forms, but the value of Kp is unchanged.
