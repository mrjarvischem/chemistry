RP4: tests for ions

Learning the tests as a list isn't enough. Exam questions come at them from every direction: test → result, result → ion, ionic equation → test, and "how would you tell these two apart?" This run-through drills all of them, then gives you unknown solutions to identify yourself.

Section 1

The tests at a glance

RP4 covers the cations Group 2 and NH₄⁺, and the anions halides, OH⁻, CO₃²⁻ and SO₄²⁻. For every ion you need four things: the test, the result, the ionic equation and why each step is done.

Section 2

Every way round: the drill

Each question gives you one part of a test and asks for another. Choose a direction or mix them all. Questions are generated, so you can keep going until you score ten in a row.

    Halide ions: silver nitrate, then ammonia

    This test belongs to Group 7 as well. Acidify with dilute nitric acid, then add silver nitrate solution. The three precipitate colours are hard to tell apart, so ammonia solution is used to confirm which one you have.

    Why do we…? The explain marks

    These are the 1–2 mark "explain" questions that students lose. Say your answer out loud, then open the card to check it.

    Section 6

    Telling two compounds apart

    A favourite question: "Describe a test to distinguish between A and B. State what you would observe in each case." You need one reagent and an observation for both compounds. "No visible change" counts as an observation.
