Equilibrium (Kp) 5 – Missing values and when pressure cancels

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibrium constant Kp walkthrough

1. Using Kp to find a missing partial pressure

  1. Write the Kp expression.
  2. Rearrange it to make the unknown partial pressure the subject. This is a mark on its own.
  3. Substitute and calculate.
  4. If the unknown is squared, take the square root at the end.

Worked example with the mark scheme

June 2022 Paper 1 Q1.5 (3 marks)

CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g) at temperature T. p(CO) = 125 kPa, p(CH₃OH) = 5.45 kPa, Kp = 1.15 × 10⁻⁶ kPa⁻². Calculate the partial pressure of hydrogen.

  1. Kp = p(CH₃OH)p(CO) × p(H₂)²
  2. M1 rearrange: p(H₂)² = p(CH₃OH)Kp × p(CO)
  3. M2 p(H₂)² = 5.451.15 × 10⁻⁶ × 125 = 37 913
  4. M3 p(H₂) = √37 913 = 194.7 kPa (allow 195)

2. Finding the total pressure

If you know Kp and the mole fractions, write each partial pressure as x × P. The P terms collect together:

Kp = (expression in mole fractions) × PΔn

Rearrange for P. If Δn = 1 (as in 2NO₂ ⇌ 2NO + O₂), P appears to the power 1 and you can solve directly.

Worked example with the mark scheme

June 2023 Paper 1 Q4.3 (3 marks)

2NO₂(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) + O₂(g). Mole fractions: NO₂ 0.310, NO 0.460, O₂ 0.230. Kp = 59.7 kPa. Give an expression for Kp and calculate the total pressure.

  1. M1 Kp = p(NO)² × p(O₂)p(NO₂)² (no square brackets)
  2. Each p = x × P, so Kp = (0.460P)² × 0.230P(0.310P)² = 0.460² × 0.2300.310² × P
  3. M2 P = Kp × 0.310²0.460² × 0.230 = 59.7 × 0.09610.04867
  4. M3 P = 117.9 kPa (or 118 kPa)

3. When the total pressure cancels

If the number of moles of gas is the same on both sides (Δn = 0), the P terms cancel completely:

H₂O + CO ⇌ H₂ + CO₂:   Kp = x(H₂)P × x(CO₂)Px(H₂O)P × x(CO)P = x(H₂) × x(CO₂)x(H₂O) × x(CO)

So you can calculate Kp from mole fractions (or even from moles) without knowing the total pressure. Kp has no units. In this case you cannot find P from Kp.
