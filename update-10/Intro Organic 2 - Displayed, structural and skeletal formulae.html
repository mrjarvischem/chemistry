Displayed, Structural and Skeletal Formulae

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.3.1 Introduction to organic chemistry · walkthrough 2

1. From displayed to skeletal, one step at a time

Don't try to jump straight from a displayed formula to a skeletal formula. Do it in small steps:

  1. Start with the displayed formula.
  2. Find the longest carbon chain. Write down just those carbons: C   C   C   C
  3. Add the hydrogens on each of those carbons: CH₃   CH   CH   CH₃
  4. Add the branches and groups in brackets after the carbon they're on. That's the structural formula.
  5. Put a dot for each carbon in the chain, in a zigzag.
  6. Join the dots with lines.
  7. Add the branches and functional groups. That's the skeletal formula.

2. Displayed formula → structural formula

A structural formula shows the minimum detail needed to show how the atoms are arranged, without drawing the bonds.

  • Go along the chain carbon by carbon. Write each carbon with its hydrogens: CH₃, CH₂, CH or C.
  • Put a branch or group in brackets straight after the carbon it's attached to: CH(CH₃), CH(OH), CH(Cl).
  • Two identical branches on one carbon: C(CH₃)₂.
  • Groups on an end carbon don't need brackets: CH₂Cl, CH₂OH, CHO, COOH.
  • Show a C=C double bond: CH₂=CHCH₃.
  • You can start from either end. CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ and CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₃ are the same molecule.

3. Structural formula → skeletal formula

  • Every corner and every end of a line is a carbon atom.
  • Hydrogens on carbon are not drawn.
  • Draw the chain as a zigzag (about 120° angles), not a straight line.
  • Branches are lines coming off a corner. Other atoms (Cl, Br, O, N) are written in, with their H if they have any: OH, not just O.
  • C=C is a double line.
AQA mark schemes say the H of an OH group must be shown in a skeletal formula. (AQA AS Paper 2, June 2019, Q07.1 mark scheme)
Common mistakes: drawing a straight line (one long line is only 2 carbons!), forgetting that the ends of lines are carbons, and drawing the H atoms on carbon.

4. Reading a skeletal formula

Go backwards with the same steps:

  1. Dot every carbon: each corner, each end and the end of every branch.
  2. Each carbon makes 4 bonds. H on a carbon = 4 − number of bonds you can see (a double line counts as 2).
  3. Write it out as a structural formula, or add up the atoms for the molecular formula.
