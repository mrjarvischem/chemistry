Amines 4 – Amines as Nucleophiles

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.3.11 Amines · walkthrough 4

1. The lone pair makes amines nucleophiles

A nucleophile is an electron-pair donor that attacks a δ+ carbon. The lone pair on N lets amines (and ammonia) attack:

Reacts withMechanismProduct
halogenoalkanes R′Xnucleophilic substitutiona more substituted amine (or quaternary salt)
acyl chlorides R′COClnucleophilic addition–eliminationN-substituted amide + HCl
acid anhydrides (R′CO)₂Onucleophilic addition–eliminationN-substituted amide + R′COOH
Same lone pair, two jobs: attacking H+ = acting as a base; attacking a δ+ carbon = acting as a nucleophile.

2. With halogenoalkanes: the substitution ladder

Start: Halogenoalkane:

Each new amine still has a lone pair, so it keeps reacting. The quaternary ammonium ion R₄N+ has no lone pair (it's used in the fourth bond) – so the ladder stops there.

Each step really forms an ammonium salt first, e.g. (CH₃CH₂)₂NH₂+Br−; a base (excess amine/NH₃) then removes the H+.

3. Quaternary ammonium salts as cationic surfactants

Quaternary ammonium salts with at least one long hydrocarbon chain, e.g. [CH₃(CH₂)₁₇N(CH₃)₃]+Cl−, are cationic surfactants.

  • The N+ head is attracted to negatively charged surfaces – wet fibres and hair carry a negative charge.
  • The long hydrocarbon tail faces outwards, leaving a smooth, water-repelling layer that reduces static.
  • Uses: fabric softeners and hair conditioners.

4. With acyl chlorides: nucleophilic addition–elimination

Nucleophile: Acyl chloride:

Five marks to collect: (1) arrow from N lone pair to C=O carbon; (2) arrow from C=O bond to O; (3) correct intermediate (O−, N+); (4) arrow from O− lone pair reforming C=O and arrow from C–Cl bond to Cl; (5) arrow from N–H bond to N+.

The HCl formed reacts with more amine, so the overall equation uses 2 amines: CH₃COCl + 2CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + CH₃NH₃+Cl−

5. Anhydrides and naming amides

Acid anhydride

(CH₃CO)₂O + CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + CH₃COOH

Same mechanism, but the leaving group is CH₃COO− and the by-product is a carboxylic acid, not HCl. Anhydrides are cheaper, less corrosive and react less violently – used industrially (e.g. aspirin, paracetamol).

Naming N-substituted amides

Name the acyl part as the amide, then put the groups on N in front with N-:

  • CH₃CONHCH₃ – N-methylethanamide
  • CH₃CH₂CONHC₆H₅ – N-phenylpropanamide
  • CH₃CON(CH₃)₂ – N,N-dimethylethanamide

With NH₃ you get a plain (primary) amide: CH₃CONH₂, ethanamide.
