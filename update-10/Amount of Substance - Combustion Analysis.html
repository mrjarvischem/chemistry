Combustion Analysis

AQA A-level Chemistry · 3.1.2 Amount of substance walkthrough · Mr(CO₂) = 44.0, Mr(H₂O) = 18.0

1. Where do the atoms go?

When an organic compound burns completely in excess oxygen:

  • every C atom ends up in a molecule of CO₂ → n(C) = n(CO₂)
  • every 2 H atoms end up in a molecule of H₂O → n(H) = 2 × n(H₂O)
  • the O in the products comes from the compound and from the O₂, so you can't use it directly. Find it by difference: mass of O = mass of sample − mass of C − mass of H

2. Hydrocarbons (C and H only)

n(C) = mass CO₂44.0     n(H) = 2 × mass H₂O18.0

Then divide by the smallest, as in any empirical formula question.

A compound containing carbon and hydrogen only is burned. 2.64 g of CO₂ and 1.08 g of H₂O are produced. Find its empirical formula. (From your C2.3 lesson.)

n(CO₂) = 2.64 ÷ 44.0 = 0.0600 mol → n(C) = 0.0600
n(H₂O) = 1.08 ÷ 18.0 = 0.0600 mol → n(H) = 2 × 0.0600 = 0.120Don't forget the × 2!
C : H = 0.0600 : 0.120 = 1 : 2 → CH₂
If you forget the × 2, this example gives CH instead of CH₂. That's the most common mistake in combustion questions.

3. Compounds containing oxygen

Work out the masses of C and H, then find the oxygen by difference:

mass C = n(C) × 12.0  ·  mass H = n(H) × 1.0  ·  mass O = sample − C − H

Split up the sample

The bar is the sample. Step through to see how much of it is C, H and O.

0.360 g of a compound containing C, H and O burns completely to form 0.880 g of CO₂ and 0.360 g of H₂O. Its Mr is 72.0. Find its molecular formula.

n(C) = 0.880 ÷ 44.0 = 0.0200 mol → mass C = 0.0200 × 12.0 = 0.240 g
n(H) = 2 × (0.360 ÷ 18.0) = 0.0400 mol → mass H = 0.0400 g
mass O = 0.360 − 0.240 − 0.0400 = 0.0800 g → n(O) = 0.0800 ÷ 16.0 = 0.00500 mol
C : H : O = 0.0200 : 0.0400 : 0.00500 = 4 : 8 : 1 → C₄H₈O
Mr(C₄H₈O) = 72.0 = Mr → molecular formula C₄H₈O (butanone)

4. When you know the moles of compound

If you know the moles of compound that burned, you can find the number of atoms in one molecule directly – no empirical formula needed:

C atoms per molecule = n(CO₂)n(compound)    H atoms per molecule = 2 × n(H₂O)n(compound)

Then use Mr to find how many O atoms make up the rest. If you're given the mass and the moles, Mr = mass ÷ moles.

AQA A-level Paper 2, June 2024, Q07

Compound L (Mr = 88.0) contains C, H and O only. A 6.56 × 10⁻⁴ mol sample of L burns completely in air to form 2.62 × 10⁻³ mol of water and 2.62 × 10⁻³ mol of carbon dioxide. Deduce the formula of L. [4 marks]

2.62 × 10⁻³6.56 × 10⁻⁴ = 4M1: each molecule of L makes 4 CO₂ and 4 H₂O.
so 1 L → 4CO₂ + 4H₂OM2
so L contains 4 C and 8 HM3. 4 H₂O contain 8 H atoms.
C₄H₈ = 48 + 8 = 56  →  88.0 − 56 = 32 = 2 O  →  C₄H₈O₂M4
The mark scheme gives only 1 mark for C₃H₄O₃ – the answer you get if you forget that 4 H₂O means 8 H atoms.

5. How it's done in the lab

The sample is burned in a stream of excess oxygen. The gases pass through two tubes, and each tube is weighed before and after:

  1. Anhydrous calcium chloride absorbs the water.
  2. Soda lime absorbs the carbon dioxide.

The increase in mass of each tube is the mass of H₂O or CO₂ formed.
