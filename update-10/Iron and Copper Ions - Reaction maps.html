Section 2

Chloride ligands: the shape changes

Adding concentrated HCl swaps the six water ligands for four chloride ligands. The co-ordination number falls from 6 to 4 and the shape changes from octahedral (90°) to tetrahedral (109.5°).

Choose a metal ion, then slide from water to concentrated HCl and back
add waterconc. HCl

Why only four?

Cl⁻ ions are larger than H₂O molecules, so only four fit around the metal ion. As negative ions they also repel each other.

Why is it reversible?

Adding water lowers [Cl⁻] and raises [H₂O], so the position of equilibrium shifts to the left and the aqua ion forms again.

Working out the charge

Add up the metal ion and the ligands: Fe³⁺ + 4Cl⁻ = (+3) + (−4) = −1, so [FeCl₄]⁻. With Cu²⁺ or Fe²⁺ the complex is 2−.

Section 3

Ammonia has two jobs

Ammonia can act as a base (it removes H⁺ from water ligands) or as a ligand (it replaces water ligands). Add it drop by drop and watch which job it is doing.

Choose a metal ion, then add NH₃(aq)
[Cu(H2O)6]2+ + 2NH3 → [Cu(H2O)4(OH)2] + 2NH4+  NH₃ as a base
[Cu(H2O)4(OH)2] + 4NH3 → [Cu(NH3)4(H2O)2]2+ + 2H2O + 2OH−  NH₃ as a ligand
[Cu(H2O)6]2+ + 4NH3 → [Cu(NH3)4(H2O)2]2+ + 4H2O  overall, in excess
Why doesn't the shape change this time? NH₃ and H₂O are a similar size and are both uncharged, so the co-ordination number stays at 6. Substitution with copper is incomplete: only four of the six water ligands are replaced.
Iron precipitates don't dissolve in excess ammonia. NH₃ only acts as a base with iron, so no soluble ammine complex forms. Only the copper precipitate dissolves.
Section 4

Switching between Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺

Oxidation with acidified KMnO₄

Fe2+ → Fe3+ + e−
MnO4− + 8H+ + 5e− → Mn2+ + 4H2O
MnO4− + 8H+ + 5Fe2+ → Mn2+ + 4H2O + 5Fe3+

The purple colour disappears while Fe²⁺ remains. The solution must be acidified because H⁺ is a reactant; without enough acid, brown MnO₂ forms instead.

Reduction with zinc and acid

2Fe3+ + Zn → 2Fe2+ + Zn2+

E°(Zn²⁺/Zn) = −0.76 V is more negative than E°(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) = +0.77 V, so zinc is the stronger reducing agent. e.m.f. = 0.77 − (−0.76) = +1.53 V, which is positive, so the reaction is feasible.

It fizzes because zinc also reacts with the acid: Zn + 2H⁺ → Zn²⁺ + H₂

Oxidation by air

The green Fe(II) hydroxide precipitate slowly turns brown at the surface as oxygen in the air oxidises it to the Fe(III) hydroxide.

4[Fe(H2O)4(OH)2] + O2 → 4[Fe(H2O)3(OH)3] + 2H2O

You need the observation and the reason. The balanced equation is an extension.

Section 5

Drawing complexes

Use wedges for bonds coming towards you and dashed lines for bonds going away. Put the whole complex in square brackets with the overall charge outside, and make sure each bond goes to the atom with the lone pair: the O in H₂O, the N in NH₃.

Choose a complex
Write OH₂ on the right-hand side (or H₂O on the left) so the bond clearly goes to the oxygen. A bond drawn to the H atoms loses the mark.

All the colours in one place

Cover the colour column and test yourself, or click a row to hide and reveal it.

Why does [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ look yellow-brown? The ion itself is pale violet, but in solution some of it loses H⁺ to form [Fe(H₂O)₅(OH)]²⁺, which gives the yellow-brown colour. Why does [CuCl₄]²⁻ often look green? It is yellow, but the solution is usually a mixture with some blue [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ left.
