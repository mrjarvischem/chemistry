Dissolving a transition metal salt

In the solid, metal ions and chloride ions are held in an ionic lattice. Polar water molecules pull ions off the surface. Each metal ion picks up six water ligands; the chloride ions move away on their own.

What happens to each ion

Metal ion: the δ− oxygen of water points in. Each O donates a lone pair, forming six co-ordinate bonds. The water becomes a ligand inside the complex ion.

Chloride ion: the δ+ hydrogens point in. These waters are only attracted, not bonded, so Cl⁻ is not part of the complex. It moves away as Cl⁻(aq).

Meet the ligands

A ligand donates a lone pair of electrons to the metal ion to form a co-ordinate bond. Count the lone pairs it can use at the same time: that is its denticity.

    2. Charge = metal oxidation state + the charges on all the ligands.
