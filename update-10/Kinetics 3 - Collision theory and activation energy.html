Kinetics 3 – Collision Theory and Activation Energy

AQA A-level Chemistry · Kinetics walkthrough

1. Collision theory

For particles to react they must:

  1. collide, and
  2. collide with energy greater than or equal to the activation energy (E ≥ Ea), and
  3. collide in the right orientation.

Activation energy, Ea: the minimum energy particles need to collide with for a reaction to happen. It's the energy needed to start breaking bonds.

Most collisions are not successful. Rate depends on the number of successful collisions per second.

rate ∝ (collisions per second) × (fraction of collisions with E ≥ Ea)

Simulation: count the successful collisions

Blue (A) and orange (B) particles react only when an A hits a B. Collisions flash green if E ≥ Ea (successful) and red if not.

Try: double the concentration (collisions per second roughly double, % successful stays the same). Then raise the temperature (collisions per second rise a little, % successful rises a lot).

3. Reaction profiles and activation energy

  • Ea is the height from the reactants to the top of the hump.
  • ΔH is the height from reactants to products: negative (down) for exothermic, positive (up) for endothermic.
  • A catalyst provides an alternative route (pathway) with a lower activation energy. ΔH doesn't change.
Ea(reverse) = Ea(forward) − ΔH
