Equilibrium (Kc) 1 – Dynamic Equilibrium and Writing Kc Expressions

AQA A-level Chemistry · Equilibria walkthrough

1. What is a dynamic equilibrium?

A reversible reaction can go both ways: A ⇌ B. In a closed system (nothing gets in or out) it reaches dynamic equilibrium.

  • The rate of the forward reaction = the rate of the reverse reaction.
  • The concentrations of reactants and products stay constant (they are not usually equal).
  • It is dynamic: both reactions are still happening, just at the same rate, so there is no overall change.

Animation: watch an equilibrium set up

Each blue particle is A, each orange particle is B. A particle can flip A → B or B → A at any moment. Start with only A, then try starting with only B.

The forward reaction is faster per particle here, so more B than A is present at equilibrium. Whichever side you start from, the mixture ends up with the same equilibrium amounts.

What the graphs show

  1. At the start, only the forward reaction can happen, so its rate is high and the reverse rate is zero.
  2. As A is used up, the forward rate falls. As B builds up, the reverse rate rises.
  3. When the two rates become equal, the concentrations stop changing. This is equilibrium.
QUICK CHECK 1 June 2022 Paper 1 Q1.1 (2 marks)

Give two features of a reaction in dynamic equilibrium.

Any two of:
• the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction
• the concentrations of reactants and products stay constant (no longer change)
• it happens in a closed system
"The concentrations are equal" is wrong. They stay constant, but are rarely equal.

2. Writing an expression for Kc

For the general equilibrium   aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD

Kc = [C]c [D]d[A]a [B]b
  • Products on top, reactants on the bottom.
  • Each [ ] means "concentration in mol dm⁻³ at equilibrium". You must use square brackets. Round brackets lose the mark.
  • The balancing numbers become powers.
  • The concentrations are multiplied, never added.
  • At A-level you only need Kc for homogeneous equilibria, where everything is in the same phase (all gases, or all in solution).

Step by step: the Haber process

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)
  1. Product on top, reactants on the bottom: Kc = [NH₃][N₂] [H₂]
  2. Add the balancing numbers as powers: there are 2 NH₃ and 3 H₂.
  3. Kc = [NH₃]²[N₂] [H₂]³
  4. Check: square brackets ✔ products on top ✔ powers match the equation ✔ multiplied, not added ✔

Common errors: reactants on top (upside down); round brackets ( ) instead of square [ ]; adding the terms; forgetting a power; putting the balancing number in front, e.g. 2[NH₃] instead of [NH₃]².

Tip

4. What happens to Kc if you change the equation?

Kc belongs to one particular equation. If the equation is written differently, the expression changes too.
