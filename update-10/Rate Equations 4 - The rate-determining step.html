Rate Equations 4 – The Rate-Determining Step

AQA A-level Chemistry · Kinetics walkthrough

1. The slowest step controls the rate

Most reactions don't happen in one go. They happen in a series of steps called the mechanism.

The rate-determining step (RDS) is the slowest step in the mechanism. The overall reaction can't go faster than this step.

Think of a queue at a single checkout. It doesn't matter how fast people pack their bags afterwards. The till sets the pace.

Animation: a two-step mechanism

(CH₃)₃CBr + OH⁻ → (CH₃)₃COH + Br⁻    (2-bromo-2-methylpropane)
Step 1 (slow): (CH₃)₃CBr → (CH₃)₃C⁺ + Br⁻
Step 2 (fast): (CH₃)₃C⁺ + OH⁻ → (CH₃)₃COH

[RBr]:   [OH⁻]:

● RBr   ● carbocation   ● OH⁻   ● product

Double [RBr] and the rate doubles. Double [OH⁻] and nothing changes: the OH⁻ ions just wait for carbocations to appear. So the rate equation is:

rate = k[(CH₃)₃CBr]

The concentrations are held constant (reacted particles are replaced). The counts are random, so watch the trend over 10–20 s.

The key rule

The rate equation tells you which species, and how many of each, are involved up to and including the rate-determining step.

  • A reactant that is zero order only reacts after the RDS.
  • An order of 2 means two of that species take part up to and including the RDS.
  • A catalyst can appear in the rate equation even though it isn't in the overall equation.

2. Mechanisms, intermediates and catalysts

Adding the steps gives the overall equation. Anything that appears on both sides cancels out.

  • Intermediate: made in one step and used up in a later step. It is not in the overall equation.
  • Catalyst: used up in an early step and made again in a later step. It is not in the overall equation, but it can be in the rate equation.

3. Which step is rate-determining?

  1. Look at the rate equation. Note which species appear and how many of each (the order).
  2. Go through the mechanism step by step and keep a running tally of what has been used up.
  3. The RDS is the step where the tally first exactly matches the rate equation. Nothing extra, nothing missing.

This is how AQA phrases it. In June 2023 the mark was for "by the end of step 2, 1 × H⁺ and 2 × B have been used".

4. Writing the rate equation from a mechanism

If you're told which step is rate-determining, the rate equation contains everything used up to and including that step.

Intermediates never appear in the rate equation. If the RDS uses an intermediate, replace it with the species that made it in the earlier step.
Rate Equations 6 Q5

2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂
Step 1 (fast): NO + NO → N₂O₂
Step 2 (slow, RDS): N₂O₂ + O₂ → 2NO₂

  1. The RDS uses N₂O₂ and O₂.
  2. N₂O₂ is an intermediate. It was made from NO + NO in step 1, so replace it with 2 × NO.
  3. Up to and including the RDS: 2 × NO and 1 × O₂ are used, so rate = k[NO]²[O₂].

QUICK CHECK 5 Rate Equations 6 Q1

A + B → C is first order in A and zero order in B. Explain how this shows the reaction must happen in more than one step.

If it were one step, A and B would collide in that step and both would be in the rate equation. B is zero order, so it isn't in the RDS. There must be a slow step involving only A, followed by a later step where B reacts.
