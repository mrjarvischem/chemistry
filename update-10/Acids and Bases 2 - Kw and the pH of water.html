Kw and the pH of water

Where the ionic product of water comes from, why it changes with temperature, and why pure water is always neutral.

Water is very slightly dissociated

A tiny proportion of water molecules dissociate:

H2O ⇌ H+ + OH−

From Kc to Kw

1
Write Kc for the equilibrium:

Kc = [H+][OH−][H2O]

2
So little water dissociates that [H2O] is very large and effectively constant.
3
Multiply both sides by [H2O]. Kc × [H2O] is a new constant called Kw, the ionic product of water.

Kw = [H+][OH−]

4
Units: (mol dm−3) × (mol dm−3) = mol2 dm−6
5
At 298 K (25 °C):

Kw = 1.00 × 10−14 mol2 dm−6

Kw applies to every aqueous solution, not just pure water. If you know [H+] you can always find [OH−], and the other way round.

CHECK 1

CHECK 2

Why does [H2O] not appear in the expression for Kw?

Very few water molecules dissociate, so [H2O] is very large and effectively constant. It is incorporated into the constant Kw.

Why does Kw increase as temperature increases?

  1. The dissociation of water is endothermic (O–H bonds are broken).
  2. When the temperature rises, the equilibrium shifts to the right to oppose the change.
  3. [H+] and [OH−] both increase, so Kw increases.

CHECK 4

Use Le Chatelier's principle to explain why the pH of pure water falls as it is heated.

Dissociation is endothermic, so the equilibrium H2O ⇌ H+ + OH− shifts right when the temperature rises. [H+] increases, so pH = −log[H+] decreases.

Calculating the pH of pure water

In pure water every H2O that dissociates gives one H+ and one OH−, so [H+] = [OH−].

Kw = [H+]2    so    [H+] = √Kw

Worked example

At 50 °C, Kw = 5.47 × 10−14 mol2 dm−6. Calculate the pH of pure water at 50 °C.

1
In pure water [H+] = [OH−], so Kw = [H+]2
2
[H+] = √(5.47 × 10−14) = 2.34 × 10−7 mol dm−3
3
pH = −log(2.34 × 10−7)

pH = 6.63

Neutral does not always mean pH 7

Acidic

[H+] > [OH−]

Neutral

[H+] = [OH−]

Alkaline

[H+] < [OH−]

Pure water at 50 °C has a pH of 6.63. It is still neutral, because [H+] and [OH−] have both increased by the same amount. pH 7.00 is only neutral at 25 °C.

"Water at 100 °C has a pH of 6.14, so hot water is acidic."

A solution is acidic only if [H+] > [OH−]. In pure water [H+] = [OH−] at every temperature, so it is neutral.

Working backwards: Kw from pH

At 37 °C the pH of pure water is 6.81. Calculate Kw at this temperature.

1
[H+] = 10−6.81 = 1.55 × 10−7 mol dm−3
2
Pure water, so [OH−] = [H+]
3
Kw = [H+]2 = (1.55 × 10−7)2

Kw = 2.40 × 10−14 mol2 dm−6

Using Kw in any solution

[OH−] = Kw[H+]    and    [H+] = Kw[OH−]

Worked example

A solution at 25 °C has [H+] = 2.00 × 10−3 mol dm−3. Calculate [OH−].

1
Kw = [H+][OH−] = 1.00 × 10−14
2
[OH−] = (1.00 × 10−14) ÷ (2.00 × 10−3)

[OH−] = 5.00 × 10−12 mol dm−3

CHECK 11

A solution at 25 °C has [OH−] = 0.0200 mol dm−3. Calculate [H+].

[H+] = (1.00 × 10−14) ÷ 0.0200 = 5.00 × 10−13 mol dm−3

CHECK 12

Calculate the pH of the solution in the last question.

pH = −log(5.00 × 10−13) = 12.30

CHECK 13

A solution at 25 °C has a pH of 9.20. Calculate [OH−].

[H+] = 10−9.20 = 6.31 × 10−10 mol dm−3
[OH−] = (1.00 × 10−14) ÷ (6.31 × 10−10) = 1.58 × 10−5 mol dm−3
