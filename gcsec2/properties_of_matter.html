Key points

  • Melting: the forces holding the particles in fixed positions are overcome, so the particles can move past each other.
  • Boiling: the forces between the particles are broken completely, so the particles separate.
  • Melting and freezing happen at the melting point; boiling and condensing happen at the boiling point.
  • The stronger the forces between the particles, the more energy is needed and the higher the melting and boiling points.
  • A single atom is not solid, liquid or gas: atoms themselves do not have the bulk properties of the material.

Limitations of the model (HT)

  • In the simple model there are no forces between the spheres. Untick "Show forces" to see the simple model.

  • The spheres are solid, but real atoms are mostly empty space.

What state is it?

How to work it out

  • Below the melting point: solid.
  • Between the melting point and the boiling point: liquid.
  • Above the boiling point: gas.
  • Take care with negative numbers: −50 °C is warmer than −100 °C.

    Explaining a melting point

    • Name the structure (e.g. giant ionic lattice, small molecules).
    • Name the forces or bonds that must be overcome, and say whether they are strong or weak.
    • Link to energy: strong forces need a lot of energy, so the melting point is high.
