Key points

  • Each carbon atom forms four covalent bonds with other carbon atoms in a giant covalent structure.
  • The bonds continue in all directions, so every atom is held by 4 strong bonds.
  • Diamond is very hard and has a very high melting point: many strong covalent bonds must be broken.
  • Diamond does not conduct electricity: there are no delocalised electrons.

Key points

  • Each carbon atom forms three covalent bonds with three other carbon atoms, forming layers of hexagonal rings.
  • There are no covalent bonds between the layers, so the layers can slide over each other: graphite is soft and slippery.
  • One electron from each carbon atom is delocalised. This makes graphite similar to metals.
  • The delocalised electrons let graphite conduct electricity and thermal energy.
  • Graphite has a high melting point: the strong covalent bonds in the layers must be broken.

Key points

  • Graphene is a single layer of graphite, one atom thick.
  • Each carbon atom forms 3 covalent bonds and has 1 delocalised electron, so graphene conducts electricity. Strong covalent bonds make it very strong.
  • Its properties make graphene useful in electronics and composites.
  • Carbon nanotubes are cylindrical fullerenes with very high length to diameter ratios.
  • Their properties make them useful for nanotechnology, electronics and materials, e.g. reinforcing tennis rackets.

Key points

  • Fullerenes are molecules of carbon atoms with hollow shapes.
  • They are based on hexagonal rings of carbon atoms but may also contain rings with five or seven carbon atoms.
  • The first fullerene discovered was Buckminsterfullerene (C₆₀), which is spherical.
  • Carbon nanotubes are cylindrical fullerenes.
  • Each C₆₀ molecule has delocalised electrons, but they cannot move between molecules, so solid C₆₀ does not conduct electricity.
  • Uses of fullerenes include drug delivery into the body, lubricants, catalysts and, for nanotubes, reinforcing materials such as tennis rackets.

Clues to look for

  • Giant covalent: atoms joined by covalent bonds that carry on beyond the edge of the diagram, in a regular pattern.
  • Polymer: a long chain of the same repeating unit, or a repeating unit in brackets with n.
  • Small molecules: separate molecules, each with only a few atoms, and no bonds between molecules.
  • Fullerene: a hollow cage or tube made only of carbon atoms in rings.
