The model

  • Every collision between reactant particles (A with B, or gas with the solid) is checked. Its energy is compared with the activation energy. If the energy is at least the activation energy the collision is successful (gold flash) and a product particle is made. Otherwise the particles just bounce off.
  • Each successful collision makes one product particle, so successful collisions per second is a measure of the rate of reaction (amount of product formed ÷ time).
  • Particles in the solid can only be hit if they are on the surface. Breaking the same amount of solid into smaller pieces exposes more particles, so collisions with the solid are more frequent.
  • Temperature makes particles move faster. This gives more frequent collisions and a greater proportion of collisions with enough energy – watch the “% successful” tile. Changing concentration changes the collision frequency but not the % successful.
  • A catalyst lowers the activation energy by providing a different reaction pathway. Weak, moderate and very effective catalysts lower it by 20%, 40% and 60%. The catalyst is not used up.
  • Energies and temperature effects are scaled so that the changes can be seen and counted in class. The trends are correct; the actual numbers are not meant to be real values for a particular reaction.

Suggested investigations

  • Concentration: gas + gas, change reactant A from 10 to 40 with everything else fixed. Plot rate against concentration of A.
  • Surface area: gas + solid, try 1 lump → powder. Plot rate against number of pieces or surface particles.
  • Temperature: 0 °C to 100 °C in steps of 20 °C. Compare how the collision frequency and % successful both change.
  • Catalysts: compare none, weak, moderate and very effective, then use the reaction profile tab (tick “Compare all three catalysts”) to link the rate to the height of each activation energy.
  • Class counting: tick “Hide the computer's count”, set speed to ½× or ¼× for fast reactions, and have students count the gold flashes. Enter the class count when time is up and compare it with the computer's count.
  • Reactants used up: untick “Keep concentration constant”, reset and watch the product vs time graph level off. Use “Keep this line” to compare a lump with a powder.
