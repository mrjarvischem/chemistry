Method
  1. Measure the sodium thiosulfate solution and water into a conical flask (total volume always 50 cm³).
  2. Put the flask on a piece of paper with a black cross drawn on it.
  3. Add 10 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid, swirl and start the stopwatch.
  4. Look down through the flask. Stop the stopwatch when you can no longer see the cross.
  5. Repeat with different volumes of sodium thiosulfate (or at different temperatures).
Na₂S₂O₃(aq) + 2HCl(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + SO₂(g) + S(s) + H₂O(l). The sulfur precipitate makes the solution cloudy.

Method
  1. Put 50 cm³ of hydrochloric acid in a conical flask on a balance.
  2. Add the marble chips, put a cotton wool plug in the neck and start the stopwatch straight away.
  3. Record the balance reading at time 0 and then at regular intervals.
  4. Mass lost = starting mass − reading. The mass goes down because carbon dioxide gas escapes.
  5. Repeat with a different size of marble chips, concentration of acid or temperature.
CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g). The marble chips are in excess.
Method
  1. Put 50 cm³ of hydrochloric acid in a conical flask. Set up a gas syringe, or a measuring cylinder full of water upside down in a trough of water.
  2. Add a 4 cm strip of magnesium ribbon (0.04 g), put the bung in quickly and start the stopwatch.
  3. Record the volume of gas collected at regular intervals until the reaction stops.
  4. Repeat with a different concentration of acid, form of magnesium or temperature.
Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g). The acid is in excess, so the same volume of hydrogen (about 40 cm³) is made each time.
