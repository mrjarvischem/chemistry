What entropy measures

  • Entropy, S, is a measure of disorder: the number of ways the particles, and their energy, can be arranged.
  • Units: J K−1 mol−1 (joules, not kilojoules).
  • A perfect crystal at 0 K has S = 0. Above 0 K every substance, including every element, has a positive entropy.
  • S rises gradually on heating as particles vibrate and move more, then jumps at each change of state.
  • The jump on boiling (+109) is far bigger than on melting (+22): gas particles are free to occupy the whole container.
  • Solid < liquid ≪ gas.

Standard entropies at 298 K / J K−1 mol−1

Harder, more rigid solids have the lowest values. Bigger, more complex gas molecules have more ways to rotate and vibrate, so higher values.

From marbles to entropy

  • The number of ways of arranging m identical marbles in n holes is
    W = n! ÷ ( m! × (n − m)! )
  • Boltzmann linked this to entropy: S = k ln W, where k = 1.38 × 10−23 J K−1.
  • More space (more holes) gives far more arrangements. This is why a gas, which can spread through the whole container, has a much higher entropy than a solid.
  • For a mole of particles, W is unimaginably large, so we work with S in J K−1 mol−1 instead.

Diamond

  • Every carbon atom has four strong covalent bonds in a rigid 3D tetrahedral network.
  • The atoms are locked in place. They can only vibrate very slightly about fixed positions.
  • There are very few ways to arrange the atoms or spread energy among them, so W is small and S is the lowest of any element.

Graphite

  • Each atom has three covalent bonds within flat hexagonal layers.
  • The layers are held together only by weak forces, so they can flex, ripple and slide.
  • More ways to move means more ways to spread the energy, so graphite's entropy is higher, though still very low.

Reading the graph

  • A reaction is feasible when ΔG ≤ 0.
  • ΔG = ΔH − TΔS is a straight line, y = mx + c: the intercept is ΔH and the gradient is −ΔS.
  • Convert ΔS to kJ K−1 mol−1 (÷ 1000) before using it with ΔH in kJ mol−1.
  • Where the line crosses ΔG = 0, T = ΔH ÷ ΔS.
  • Feasible does not mean fast. A large activation energy can make a feasible reaction too slow to observe.
