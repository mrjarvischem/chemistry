Percentage yield and atom economy

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.3.3.1 and 4.3.3.2 (chemistry only)

Key points

  • The theoretical yield is the maximum mass of product that could be made, calculated from the equation.
  • The actual yield is the mass you really get.
  • Percentage yield = actual mass ÷ maximum theoretical mass × 100.
  • It is not always possible to get 100 % because: the reaction may be reversible and not go to completion; some product may be lost when it is separated from the reaction mixture; some reactants may react in different ways to the expected reaction.

Method

  • Use a reacting mass calculation to find the theoretical mass of product: mass → moles → ratio → moles → mass.
  • Then percentage yield = actual mass ÷ theoretical mass × 100.

Key points

  • Atom economy measures how much of the starting materials ends up as useful product.
  • Atom economy = relative formula mass of the desired product from the equation ÷ sum of the relative formula masses of all the reactants from the equation × 100.
  • "From the equation" means you do multiply each Mr by its coefficient (big number) here.
  • A high atom economy is important for sustainable development and for economic reasons: less waste, and fewer raw materials used.
  • A reaction with only one product has an atom economy of 100 %.
