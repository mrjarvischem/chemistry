The mole

AQA GCSE 4.3.2.1 / Trilogy 5.3.2.1 (HT only)

Substance

Amount1 mol

Key points

  • Chemical amounts are measured in moles. The symbol for the unit is mol.
  • One mole of any substance contains the same number of particles: 6.02 × 10²³ (the Avogadro constant).
  • The mass of one mole of a substance, in grams, is equal to its relative formula mass.

Method

  • One mole has a mass of Mr grams.
  • To find the number of moles, ask: how many lots of Mr grams fit into the mass? moles = mass ÷ Mr
  • To find a mass: each mole has a mass of Mr grams, so mass = moles × Mr

Method

  • Each mole contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles.
  • number of particles = moles × 6.02 × 10²³
  • The particles can be atoms, molecules, ions or electrons: read the question carefully.
  • Type standard form like this: 3.01x10^23

Method

  • One mole has a mass of Mr grams and contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles.
  • Share the mass equally between the particles: mass of one particle = Mr ÷ 6.02 × 10²³
  • Type standard form like this: 2.99x10^-23
