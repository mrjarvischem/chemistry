Moles of gases

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.3.5 (chemistry only, HT)

Gas

Amount1 mol

Key points

  • Equal amounts in moles of gases occupy the same volume under the same conditions of temperature and pressure.
  • One mole of any gas has a volume of 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure (20 °C and 1 atmosphere).
  • 24 dm³ = 24 000 cm³.
  • The mass is different for each gas, because the molecules have different masses.

Method

  • Each mole of gas takes up 24 dm³, so volume (dm³) = moles × 24.
  • To find moles, ask how many lots of 24 dm³ there are: moles = volume (dm³) ÷ 24.
  • If the volume is in cm³, divide by 1000 first (or use 24 000 cm³).
  • To link to mass, go through moles: moles = mass ÷ Mr.

Method

  • For gases, the ratio of volumes is the same as the ratio of moles in the equation.
  • N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃: 1 volume of nitrogen reacts with 3 volumes of hydrogen to make 2 volumes of ammonia.
  • Total volume of gases after a reaction: one reactant is in excess. Find how much of it is left over, find the volume of each gas product (check the state symbols), then add them.
  • From a mass of solid: mass → moles → ratio → moles of gas → × 24 for the volume in dm³.
