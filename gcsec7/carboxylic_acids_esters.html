Walkthrough 6 · Carboxylic acids and esters (chemistry only)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.7.2.4 · Chemistry only — not Combined Science · about 25 minutes

Carboxylic acids CHEMISTRY ONLY

Carboxylic acids have the functional group –COOH. Learn: methanoic, ethanoic, propanoic and butanoic acid.

Count every carbon, including the one in –COOH. Ethanoic acid {CH3COOH} has 2 carbons; methanoic acid {HCOOH} has 1.

Reactions of carboxylic acids

With carbonates

carboxylic acid + metal carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide
e.g. ethanoic acid + sodium carbonate → sodium ethanoate + water + carbon dioxide

In water

They dissolve to form acidic solutions (pH about 3–4). They are weak acids — they only partially ionise.

Compare: ethanoic acid vs hydrochloric acid (same concentration)

Making esters

Carboxylic acids react with alcohols, in the presence of an acid catalyst (e.g. a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid), to make esters and water.

ethanoic acid + ethanol ⇌ ethyl ethanoate + water
Esters have the functional group –COO–. They are volatile and have fruity smells, so they are used in flavourings and perfumes, and as solvents. Learn the name ethyl ethanoate.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Carboxylic acids: –COOH; methanoic, ethanoic, propanoic, butanoic acid.
  • React with carbonates → salt + water + CO₂ (salts end in -oate, e.g. ethanoate).
  • Weak acids: partially ionised in water; pH 3–4.
  • Acid + alcohol (acid catalyst) → ester + water: ethanoic acid + ethanol → ethyl ethanoate.
