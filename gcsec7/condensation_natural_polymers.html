Walkthrough 8 · Condensation and natural polymers (chemistry only)

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.7.3.2–4.7.3.4 · Chemistry only — not Combined Science · condensation polymers and amino acids are Higher tier · about 30 minutes

Condensation polymerisation CHEMISTRY ONLYHT

Condensation polymerisation uses monomers with two functional groups. When the monomers react they join together and a small molecule (usually water) is lost each time.

The simplest example: a diol (two –OH groups) + a dicarboxylic acid (two –COOH groups) → a polyester + water. Monomers are often shown as blocks: HO–□–OH and HOOC–○–COOH.

Amino acids and proteins HT

Amino acids have two different functional groups in one molecule: an amine group (–NH₂) and a carboxylic acid group (–COOH). They react by condensation polymerisation to make polypeptides.

Glycine is the simplest amino acid: {H2NCH2COOH}. Different amino acids combine in the same chain to make proteins.

Naturally occurring polymers

DNA

Deoxyribonucleic acid is a large molecule essential for life. It encodes genetic instructions for the development and functioning of living organisms and viruses. Most DNA molecules are two polymer chains, made from four different monomers called nucleotides, in the form of a double helix.

Summary

  • (HT) Condensation: two functional groups per monomer; water lost each time a bond forms.
  • (HT) Diol + dicarboxylic acid → polyester + water. Amino acids (–NH₂ and –COOH) → polypeptides / proteins + water.
  • Natural polymers: DNA (nucleotides, double helix), proteins (amino acids), starch and cellulose (sugars).
