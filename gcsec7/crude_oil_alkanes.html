Walkthrough 1 · Crude oil, hydrocarbons and alkanes

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.7.1.1 · Combined Science 5.7.1.1 · about 25 minutes

Where does crude oil come from?

Crude oil is a finite resource found in rocks. It is the remains of an ancient biomass consisting mainly of plankton that was buried in mud.

Crude oil is a mixture of a very large number of compounds. Most of them are hydrocarbons: molecules made of hydrogen and carbon atoms only.

Alkanes

Most of the hydrocarbons in crude oil are alkanes. Alkanes contain only single C–C bonds — they are saturated. Change the number of carbon atoms.

General formula of the alkanes: CnH2n+2. Learn the first four: methane {CH4}, ethane {C2H6}, propane {C3H8}, butane {C4H10}.
Each alkane differs from the next by {CH2}. A family like this, with the same general formula and similar chemical properties, is a homologous series.

Summary

  • Crude oil: finite resource, remains of ancient plankton buried in mud; a mixture of mostly hydrocarbons.
  • Hydrocarbon = compound of hydrogen and carbon only.
