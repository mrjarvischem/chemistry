Lesson 6 · Moments, levers and gears Physics only

The turning effect of a force

A force can make an object turn about a pivot. The turning effect is called the moment of the force.

M = F d Learn this — not on the equation sheet

M = moment (newton-metres, N m), F = force (N), d = perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force (m).

Force: N
Distance from pivot: m
To get a bigger moment with the same force, push further from the pivot. That’s why a long spanner undoes a tight nut more easily.

The principle of moments

Tap a position to add a 10 N weight. Tap again to remove it. Can you balance the beam?

If an object is balanced, the total clockwise moment about a pivot equals the total anticlockwise moment about that pivot.

Levers and gears

Simple levers and gear systems can transmit the rotational effects of forces. They act as force multipliers.

Levers work the same way: a small force a long way from the pivot (e.g. on a crowbar handle) makes a large force close to the pivot.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Moment = turning effect. M = F d (learn it), in N m. d is the perpendicular distance to the pivot.
  • Balanced: total clockwise moment = total anticlockwise moment.
  • Levers and gears transmit rotational effects and act as force multipliers.
  • A small gear driving a large gear: the large one turns more slowly but with a bigger moment.
