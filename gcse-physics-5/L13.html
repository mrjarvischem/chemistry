Lesson 13 · Stopping distances and reaction times

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Forces 4.5.6.3 · about 25 minutes

Thinking distance + braking distance

Stopping distance = thinking distance (distance travelled during the driver’s reaction time) + braking distance (distance travelled under the braking force).
Speed: m/s ( mph)
Reaction time: s

Thinking distance increases with…

speed · tiredness · drugs · alcohol · distractions (e.g. a phone)

Braking distance increases with…

speed · wet or icy roads · worn brakes · worn tyres · a heavier load

Double the speed → thinking distance doubles, but braking distance becomes four times bigger (it depends on v²). Try 13 m/s then 26 m/s.

Measure your reaction time

The ruler-drop test: a partner drops a ruler and you catch it. The further it falls, the slower your reaction. Press Ready, then press Catch! (or the space bar) as soon as the ruler starts falling.

Typical reaction times are 0.2 s to 0.9 s.
The ruler falls with acceleration g, so distance fallen d gives the time: t = √(2d ÷ g). Repeat several times and take a mean.

What happens when you brake

When the brakes are applied, the friction between the brake pads and discs does work. This transfers energy from the car’s kinetic store to the thermal store of the brakes, so their temperature rises.

The faster a car goes, the greater the braking force needed to stop it in a given distance. A large deceleration can cause the brakes to overheat and/or the driver to lose control (skidding).

Estimating the force Higher tier

A 1200 kg car brakes from 20 m/s to rest in 4.0 s.
a = Δv ÷ t = −20 ÷ 4.0 = −5.0 m/s² → F = m a = 1200 × 5.0 = 6000 N.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance.
  • Reaction times are typically 0.2–0.9 s; tiredness, drugs, alcohol and distractions increase them.
  • Braking distance increases with speed (v²), wet/icy roads, poor brakes and tyres.
  • Braking: friction does work, kinetic → thermal store of brakes. Large decelerations are dangerous.
