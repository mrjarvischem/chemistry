Lesson 7 · Pressure in fluids, upthrust and the atmosphere Physics only

AQA GCSE Physics (8463 only) · Forces 4.5.5 · about 25 minutes

Pressure

A fluid is a liquid or a gas. The pressure in a fluid causes a force normal (at right angles) to any surface.

p = F ÷ A Learn this

p = pressure in pascals (Pa), F = force normal to the surface (N), A = area of the surface (m²). 1 Pa = 1 N/m².

Force: N

The same force on a smaller area gives a bigger pressure. That’s why a drawing pin goes into wood but your thumb doesn’t get hurt, and why snowshoes stop you sinking.

Pressure in a liquid Higher tier

p = h ρ g Given on the equation sheet

p = pressure due to the column of liquid (Pa), h = height of the column (depth, m), ρ = density of the liquid (kg/m³), g = 9.8 N/kg.

Depth of the hole: m
Pressure in a liquid increases with depth (more liquid above pushing down) and with the density of the liquid.

Upthrust and floating

A submerged object has more liquid above its bottom than above its top. The pressure is bigger on the bottom, so there is a resultant upward force: upthrust.

Upthrust = the weight of fluid displaced. An object floats if its weight equals the upthrust before it is fully submerged — i.e. if it is less dense than the liquid. A denser object sinks because its weight is bigger than the maximum upthrust.

Atmospheric pressure

The atmosphere is a thin layer of air round the Earth that gets less dense with altitude. Air molecules colliding with a surface create atmospheric pressure. The higher you go, the less air above you, so the weight of air above a surface — and the atmospheric pressure — decreases with height.

Lesson summary

  • Fluid pressure acts at right angles to surfaces. p = F ÷ A (Pa = N/m²).
  • HT: p = h ρ g. Pressure increases with depth and density.
  • Upthrust: bigger pressure on the bottom of a submerged object. Floats if less dense than the liquid.
  • Atmospheric pressure decreases with height: less air above.
