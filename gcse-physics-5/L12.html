Lesson 12 · Newton’s third law

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Forces 4.5.6.2.3 · about 25 minutes

Newton’s third law

Whenever two objects interact, the forces they exert on each other are equal in size and opposite in direction.

Pick an interaction. Notice that the two forces always act on different objects and are the same type of force.

Third-law pair or balanced forces?

A book rests on a table. Its weight and the normal contact force are equal and opposite — but they are not a Newton’s third law pair!

Balanced forces

Act on the same object. Can be different types (weight is gravitational, normal contact force is contact). They decide whether the object accelerates (first law).

Third-law pair

Act on two different objects. Always the same type. e.g. the Earth pulls the book down (gravity) ↔ the book pulls the Earth up (gravity).

Third law in action

🚀 Rocket

The rocket pushes exhaust gases backwards; the gases push the rocket forwards with an equal force.

🏊 Swimmer

Her hands push water backwards; the water pushes her forwards.

🚶 Walking

Your foot pushes backwards on the ground (friction); the ground pushes your foot forwards.

🔫 Recoil

A cannon pushes the ball forwards; the ball pushes the cannon backwards — it recoils.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Newton’s third law: interacting objects exert equal and opposite forces on each other.
  • The pair acts on different objects and is the same type of force.
  • Balanced forces act on the same object — not a third-law pair.
  • Examples: rockets, swimming, walking, recoil.
