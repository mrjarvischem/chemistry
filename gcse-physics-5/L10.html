Lesson 10 · Newton’s first law and terminal velocity

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Forces 4.5.6.2.1 · terminal velocity 4.5.6.1.5 (Physics only) · about 25 minutes

Newton’s first law

If the resultant force on an object is zero: a stationary object stays stationary, and a moving object carries on at the same speed in the same direction (constant velocity).

Give the puck one push and watch. Then change the surface.

So the velocity (speed or direction) of an object only changes if a resultant force acts on it. A car at a steady 30 m/s still needs its engine: the driving force balances friction and air resistance.

Inertia Higher tier

Inertia is the tendency of objects to continue in their state of rest or of uniform motion. It is why you lurch forwards when a bus brakes suddenly — your body keeps moving until a force (the seat belt) stops it.

Terminal velocity Physics only

Jump from the plane. Open the parachute when you like. Watch the forces and the velocity–time graph.

Falling through a fluid: weight is constant, but air resistance (drag) increases with speed. When drag = weight, the resultant force is zero and the object falls at a constant terminal velocity. Opening a parachute increases the area → much more drag → slows down to a new, lower terminal velocity.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Newton’s first law: zero resultant force → stationary stays still; moving keeps constant velocity.
  • Velocity only changes if there is a resultant force.
  • HT: inertia is the tendency to keep doing what you are doing.
  • Physics only: drag increases with speed; drag = weight → terminal velocity. Parachute → lower terminal velocity.
