Lesson 9 · Acceleration and velocity–time graphs

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Forces 4.5.6.1.5 · about 25 minutes

Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. A negative acceleration is a deceleration — slowing down.

a = Δv ÷ t Learn this — not on the equation sheet

a = acceleration (m/s²), Δv = change in velocity (m/s) = final − initial, t = time taken (s).

Anything falling freely near the Earth’s surface accelerates at about 9.8 m/s². A car pulling away might accelerate at about 2–3 m/s².

Velocity–time graphs

Tap a section of the graph to find its acceleration. Tap Area to find the distance travelled.

Tap A, B or C on the graph.
On a velocity–time graph the gradient = acceleration. Flat line = constant velocity. Sloping down = deceleration.
HT: the area under the graph = distance travelled.

Uniform acceleration

v² − u² = 2 a s Given on the equation sheet

v = final velocity (m/s), u = initial velocity (m/s), a = acceleration (m/s²), s = distance (m). Use it when there is no time in the question.
