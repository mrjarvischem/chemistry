Lesson 4 · Work done and energy transfer

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Forces 4.5.2 · about 25 minutes

Work done

When a force causes an object to move through a distance, work is done on the object. Work done is the same as the energy transferred.

W = F s Learn this — not on the equation sheet

1 joule = 1 newton-metre (1 J = 1 N m): the work done when a force of 1 N moves an object 1 m.

Force: N
Distance: m

Work done calculations

If the force doesn’t move anything, no work is done — pushing hard on a wall that doesn’t move does zero work on the wall.

Work done against friction

Rub your hands together: they warm up. Work done against frictional forces transfers energy to the thermal store — the temperature of the object (and surroundings) rises.

Brakes

The brake pads push on the discs. Friction does work, transferring the car’s kinetic energy to the thermal store of the brakes. They can get very hot.

Re-entry

A spacecraft returning to Earth does work against air resistance. Huge amounts of energy go to the thermal store of the heat shield.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Work is done when a force moves an object through a distance. Work done = energy transferred.
  • W = F s (learn it): J, N, m. 1 J = 1 N m.
  • No movement → no work done.
  • Work done against friction increases the temperature (thermal store).
