Lesson 8 · Speed, velocity and distance–time graphs

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Forces 4.5.6.1.1–4.5.6.1.4 · about 25 minutes

Distance, displacement, speed and velocity

Scalars

Distance: how far an object moves (no direction).
Speed: how fast — distance per unit time.

Vectors

Displacement: distance in a straight line from start to finish, with a direction.
Velocity: speed in a given direction.

An object moving in a circle at constant speed has a changing velocity, because its direction keeps changing.

Typical speeds

Walking1.5 m/sCar in town13 m/s
Running3 m/sCar on motorway31 m/s
Cycling6 m/sTrainup to 55 m/s
Wind5–20 m/sSound in air330 m/s

Calculating speed

s = v t Learn this — not on the equation sheet

s = distance travelled (m), v = speed (m/s), t = time (s). Speed is rarely constant, so this often gives the average speed.

Distance–time graphs

Watch a cyclist’s journey build the graph. Then tap a section to measure its gradient.

Press Play.
On a distance–time graph the gradient = speed. A straight sloping line = constant speed; steeper = faster; flat = stationary. A curve means the speed is changing.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Distance and speed are scalars; displacement and velocity are vectors.
  • s = v t (learn it). Know typical speeds.
  • Distance–time graph: gradient = speed; flat = stationary.
  • HT: for a curve, draw a tangent and find its gradient for the speed at that instant.
