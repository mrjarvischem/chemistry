AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Forces 4.5.6.2.2 · Required practical · about 25 minutes

Newton’s second law

The acceleration of an object is proportional to the resultant force acting on it, and inversely proportional to its mass.

F = m a Learn this — not on the equation sheet

F = resultant force (N), m = mass (kg), a = acceleration (m/s²).

Resultant force: N
Mass: kg

Required practical: force and acceleration

A trolley is pulled along a bench by a hanging mass. Two light gates measure its speed, and so its acceleration. To change the force without changing the total mass, move slotted masses from the trolley to the hanger.

Masses on hanger:
Why move the masses rather than add new ones? The whole system (trolley + hanger) accelerates. Keeping its total mass constant makes mass a control variable, so any change in acceleration is due to the force alone.

Inertial mass Higher tier

Inertial mass is a measure of how difficult it is to change the velocity of an object. It is defined as the ratio of force over acceleration: m = F ÷ a. A loaded lorry is much harder to speed up or stop than a car.
