Lesson 2 · Gravity, weight and mass

AQA GCSE Physics / Combined Science · Forces 4.5.1.3 · about 25 minutes

Mass and weight are different

Mass

How much matter an object contains. Measured in kilograms (kg). A scalar. It is the same everywhere.

Weight

The force acting on an object due to gravity. Measured in newtons (N). A vector (it acts downwards). It depends on where you are.

Astronaut’s mass: kg

Calculating weight

W = m g Learn this — not on the equation sheet
SymbolQuantityUnit
Wweightnewtons, N
mmasskilograms, kg
ggravitational field strengthnewtons per kilogram, N/kg

On Earth g = 9.8 N/kg. The weight of an object is directly proportional to its mass (W ∝ m).

Newtonmeters and centre of mass

Mass hung on it: g

Newtonmeter

A calibrated spring-balance: the spring stretches in proportion to the force, and the scale reads weight in newtons.

Centre of mass

The weight of an object can be thought of as acting at a single point called its centre of mass. For a uniform, symmetrical object it is at the centre.

Exam practice

Lesson summary

  • Mass (kg) is the same everywhere; weight (N) is the force of gravity on it.
  • W = m g (learn it). g = 9.8 N/kg on Earth. Weight ∝ mass.
  • Gravitational field strength depends on the planet (or moon).
  • Measure weight with a newtonmeter. Weight acts at the centre of mass.
