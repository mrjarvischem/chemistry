Walkthrough 5 · Atmospheric pollutants from fuels

AQA GCSE Chemistry 4.9.3.1–4.9.3.2 · Combined Science 5.9.3.1–5.9.3.2 · about 25 minutes

What is released when fuels burn?

The combustion of fuels is a major source of atmospheric pollutants. Most fuels may also contain some sulfur. Choose a fuel and the oxygen supply.

Products

Carbon monoxide and soot (carbon particles): from incomplete combustion (not enough oxygen). Sulfur dioxide: from sulfur in the fuel. Oxides of nitrogen: nitrogen and oxygen from the air react at the high temperatures in engines. Particulates: solid particles of carbon and unburnt hydrocarbons.

Effects of the pollutants

Carbon monoxide

A toxic gas that is colourless and odourless, so it is not easily detected. It stops the blood carrying oxygen.

Sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen

Cause respiratory problems in humans and cause acid rain (damages buildings, trees and lakes).

Particulates (soot)

Cause global dimming (less sunlight reaches the ground) and health problems (lung damage).

Carbon dioxide

Not toxic at these levels, but a greenhouse gas (walkthrough 3).

Equations for incomplete combustion

{2CH4 + 3O2 -> 2CO + 4H2O}   (carbon monoxide)
{CH4 + O2 -> C + 2H2O}   (soot)
Balance C first, then H, then O. If you need an odd number of O atoms, double everything.

Quiz and exam practice

Summary

  • Combustion of fuels releases CO₂, H₂O, CO, soot, SO₂, oxides of nitrogen and particulates.
  • CO: toxic, colourless, odourless. SO₂ and NOₓ: respiratory problems and acid rain. Particulates: global dimming and health problems.
  • Incomplete combustion makes carbon monoxide and/or carbon (soot).
