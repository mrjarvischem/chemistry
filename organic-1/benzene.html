Predicted vs measured ΔHhydrogenation

Cyclohexene is the reference: one C=C gives −120 kJ mol⁻¹. Each extra C=C should add another −120, unless the π electrons are delocalised.

Cyclohexa-1,3-diene vs cyclohexa-1,4-diene

1,3-diene (conjugated): the C=C bonds alternate with one single bond, so the p orbitals on C2 and C3 sit next to each other and overlap slightly. There is a little delocalisation, so it is 8 kJ mol⁻¹ more stable than predicted.

1,4-diene (isolated): the C=C bonds are separated by a CH₂ carbon with no p orbital. No overlap, no delocalisation, so measured = predicted.

The same idea applies to open-chain isomers: hexa-1,3-diene is conjugated (partial delocalisation); hexa-1,5-diene has isolated C=C bonds (none).

Exam working

delocalisation energy = 3 × (−120) − (−208) = −360 + 208 = 152 kJ mol⁻¹

Benzene releases less energy on hydrogenation because it starts lower in energy: the delocalised ring needs more energy to break.

Hypothetical cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene

The σ framework (AQA)

6 C–C σ bonds and 6 C–H σ bonds hold the ring together, all in one plane with 120° bond angles. The remaining 6 electrons (one p electron from each carbon) form the delocalised π system.

Both shapes have 120° angles. The triene would be an irregular hexagon (sides alternate); benzene is a regular hexagon. X-ray diffraction shows six equal bonds.
