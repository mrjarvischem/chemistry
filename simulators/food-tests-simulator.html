Teacher notes

Method this models

  • Grind the food with distilled water and filter, then test separate samples of the solution.
  • Benedict’s: add an equal volume of Benedict’s solution and heat in a water bath at about 80 °C for 5 minutes.
  • Iodine: add a few drops of iodine solution. Biuret: add Biuret reagent and shake gently.
  • Sudan III: add a few drops of Sudan III stain and shake. The ethanol emulsion test is an alternative for lipids.

Safety

  • Eye protection throughout. Biuret reagent contains sodium hydroxide, which is corrosive. Sudan III in ethanol is flammable: no naked flames, so use a water bath.

Questions to draw out

  • Why is distilled water tested? What does a result here tell you?
  • Glucose drink, milk and bread give different Benedict’s colours. What does the colour tell you about the amount of sugar? Why is the test only semi-quantitative?
  • Use mystery samples: which food groups does each contain, and which food could it be?
  • Why would a coloured food, such as beetroot, make these tests harder to judge?

The data are simulated. Each food gives the result expected for its main nutrients; real foods can give fainter or mixed results.
