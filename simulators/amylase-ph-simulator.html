Teacher notes

Variables

  • Independent: pH of the buffer solution.
  • Dependent: time for the starch to be fully digested (iodine stays orange-brown).
  • Control: temperature (35 °C water bath), volume and concentration of amylase and starch, volume of buffer.

Method this models

  • Warm 2 cm³ amylase, 2 cm³ starch and 1 cm³ buffer in the water bath, then mix and start the clock.
  • Every 30 s, transfer a drop of the mixture to a fresh well of iodine.
  • Stop when the iodine no longer changes colour. Repeat with other buffers.

Questions to draw out

  • Why is the recorded time only ever a multiple of 30 s? Switch to 10 s sampling and compare pH 6, 7 and 8.
  • Why is the end point hard to judge? Look at the brown wells just before it.
  • Why does the time graph have a minimum where the rate graph has a maximum?
  • Why is there no result at pH 3 or pH 10? Link to the active site changing shape (denaturing).
  • Better methods: a colorimeter for the end point, a pH probe to check each buffer.

The data are simulated. The model puts the optimum close to pH 7 and 40 °C, in line with human amylase; real class results vary.
