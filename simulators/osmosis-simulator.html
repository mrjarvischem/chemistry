Teacher notes

Variables

  • Independent: concentration of the sucrose solution.
  • Dependent: percentage change in mass of the cylinder.
  • Control: tissue (same potato), cylinder length and diameter, volume of solution, time in solution, temperature.

Method this models

  • Cut six cylinders with a cork borer, trim to the same length, blot and weigh each one.
  • Put one cylinder in each boiling tube of sucrose solution, 0.0 to 1.0 mol/dm³.
  • After a fixed time, remove, blot dry, reweigh and calculate the percentage change in mass.

Questions to draw out

  • The cylinders start at different masses. Why does that make percentage change the fair comparison?
  • Turn blotting off and repeat. Which way does every point move, and why?
  • Compare 30 minutes with 24 hours. Why are the short-run changes smaller?
  • Where does the line cross zero, and what does that say about the concentration inside the cells?
  • How could the estimate be improved? More concentrations between the two either side of zero, and repeats.

The data are simulated. The crossing points for the three tissues are typical values chosen for the model; real vegetables vary.
