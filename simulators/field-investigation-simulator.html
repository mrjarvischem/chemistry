Teacher notes

Method this models

  • Random sampling: lay two tape measures at right angles, use random numbers as coordinates, and count the plants in a 0.25 m² quadrat at each point.
  • Estimated population = mean number per quadrat × (total area ÷ area of one quadrat).
  • Transect: lay a tape from the base of a tree into open ground, place the quadrat at regular intervals and record the count and the light intensity.

Questions to draw out

  • Take 5 quadrats and estimate the population, then take 20. Which estimate is closer to the real value, and why?
  • Use “Let me choose where” and aim for the daisy patches. What happens to the estimate? Why must sampling be random?
  • Daisies grow in clumps here and dandelions are spread evenly. Which species needs more quadrats for a reliable mean?
  • On the transect, how does the number of daisies change with distance from the tree? Which abiotic factors could explain it?
  • Why is a transect used here and not random sampling?

The data are simulated. Each new field has a different random arrangement of plants. Daisies are clumped, dandelions are spread evenly and plantain follows a trampled path.
