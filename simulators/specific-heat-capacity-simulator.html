Teacher notes

Variables

  • Independent: energy transferred to the block (work done by the heater).
  • Dependent: temperature of the block.
  • Control: mass of the block (1.00 kg), the material, the insulation.

Method this models

  • Measure the mass of the block. Put the heater in the large hole and the thermometer in the small hole, with a drop of water for good thermal contact.
  • Wrap the block in insulation. Record the starting temperature, then switch on the heater and start the clock.
  • Record the joulemeter reading and the temperature every minute for 10 minutes.
  • Plot temperature against energy transferred. Gradient of the straight section = 1 ÷ (m × c).
  • Without a joulemeter: energy = p.d. × current × time, using the voltmeter and ammeter readings.

Questions to draw out

  • Why do the first few points sit below the line of best fit? The heater has to warm up first, and energy takes time to reach the thermometer.
  • Why is the calculated value higher than the accepted value? Some energy heats the heater itself and some is transferred to the surroundings, so the temperature rise is smaller than it should be.
  • Why is the gradient method closer than using only the first and last readings?
  • Run the same block with and without insulation and compare the two curves and the two values of c.
  • Compare aluminium with copper at the same power: which heats up faster, and what does that say about c?
  • Switch the thermometer to 1 °C resolution: what happens to the scatter about the line?

The data are simulated. Accepted values used: aluminium 900, iron 450, copper 385, brass 380 J/kg °C. With insulation the gradient method typically comes out 5 to 15% high, in line with real class results.
