Distance to reaction time

The ruler falls freely, so time = √(2 × distance ÷ 9.8), with distance in metres.

Teacher notes

Variables

  • Independent: the factor being changed, for example which hand, caffeine, distraction or practice.
  • Dependent: distance the ruler falls before it is caught, converted to reaction time.
  • Control: same person, same ruler and starting position, same height of drop, no warning of the drop.

Method this models

  • Person A sits with their forearm on the bench and their hand over the edge. Person B holds the ruler with 0 cm level with A’s thumb and finger.
  • B drops the ruler without warning. A catches it and the distance is read from the top of the thumb.
  • Repeat ten times, then change the factor being tested.

Questions to draw out

  • Why does a longer distance mean a longer reaction time? Why is the scale not linear?
  • Does reaction time change over the ten attempts? What does that say about practice?
  • Why ten repeats and a mean? Which results look anomalous?
  • Trace the pathway: receptor in the eye, sensory neurone, brain, motor neurone, effector muscle in the hand.
  • Why would a computer test give more precise times than a ruler?

The data are simulated. Simulated students have typical reaction times of about 0.2 s; the size of each effect is illustrative. A screen and mouse add a small delay to your own times.
