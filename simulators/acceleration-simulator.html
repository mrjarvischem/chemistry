Teacher notes

Effect of force

  • Independent: the resultant force (weight of the hanging masses, mass × 9.8 N/kg). Dependent: acceleration. Control: the total mass of the system.
  • Five 100 g masses are shared between the trolley and the hanger. Moving a mass from the trolley to the hanger increases the force but keeps the total mass at 1.0 kg.
  • Why move masses across instead of just adding to the hanger? The hanging masses accelerate too, so adding to the hanger would change the mass as well as the force.

Effect of mass

  • Independent: total mass of the system. Dependent: acceleration. Control: the force (200 g hanging, 1.96 N).
  • Switch the x-axis to 1 ÷ mass: why does the curve become a straight line through the origin?

Questions to draw out

  • Set the runway to level. Why does the line of best fit no longer pass through the origin? What does the intercept on the force axis tell you?
  • Why is the runway tilted slightly in the standard method? To compensate for friction.
  • Why are light gates better than a stopwatch here? Look at the times involved.
  • Does the data support acceleration ∝ force and acceleration ∝ 1 ÷ mass?

The data are simulated. Light gates are 0.20 m and 1.00 m from the release point. On the level runway the model adds a constant 0.15 N of friction.
